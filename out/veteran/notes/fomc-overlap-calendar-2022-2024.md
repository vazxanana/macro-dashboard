# FOMC formal-statement overlap calendar, 2022–2024

Created 9 November 2026 UTC. Metadata only: no Treasury study results or price data were opened, and no return calculations were performed.

## Gate result

**Complete for the currently published formal FOMC policy-statement scope:** 24 releases, eight in each year; every official calendar statement URL matches an annual FOMC release-archive entry. The full annual press archives were also screened for all Monetary Policy entries and FOMC/Committee-titled entries, including releases outside scheduled meetings. No additional unscheduled formal FOMC policy statement was found. This conclusion is based on reconciled sources, not on assuming there must be eight meetings.

This is **not complete coverage of Federal Reserve policy or news**. Minutes, speeches, press conferences, Board discount-rate actions, liquidity facilities and joint announcements are outside the frozen statement-only gate. Three March 2023 unscheduled broader-policy candidates are preserved separately below.

## Applying the frozen overlap rule

Use the existing interval endpoints: **prior weekday 15:30 New York through ending weekday 15:30 New York**, with the frozen weekday/holiday treatment unchanged. Compare actual timezone-aware timestamps, not date equality.

- Exclude an observation if a formal statement lies strictly inside its interval.
- Exclude equality with either endpoint as a boundary ambiguity. Any unresolved relevant clock/timezone means that interval cannot be declared clean.
- Screen the original matched-control intervals by the same rule. Keep only original eligible matches; do not rematch, refill or select replacement controls.
- An interval extending beyond verified calendar coverage requires official coverage for that extension before it can pass.

For example, a 14:00 Wednesday statement is inside an interval ending Wednesday at 15:30. It is before the start of an interval beginning that Wednesday at 15:30, so an ending-Thursday observation is not excluded merely because the previous date had a statement. This is interval logic only; no study rows were inspected or filtered here.

The completeness claim is limited to the formal statement class in the official sources currently published.
