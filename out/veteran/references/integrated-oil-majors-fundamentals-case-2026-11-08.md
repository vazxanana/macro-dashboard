# Integrated oil majors fundamentals case — 8 November 2026

**Cutoff:** information public by 8 November 2026. The latest reports for ExxonMobil and Shell both cover the quarter ended 30 September 2026. All financial amounts below are US dollars unless noted. This is a filing-based cash-conversion comparison, not a valuation or trading recommendation.

## Why put these companies beside each other?

Both companies operate across oil and gas production, processing and sales, but their earnings mix and reporting measures differ. The case shows why headline earnings, cash flow, “free cash flow,” production and net debt need company-by-company definitions before the analyst makes a peer claim. Shell completed the ARC Resources acquisition during Q3; Exxon had substantial Middle East and Kazakhstan volume disruptions. Those different perimeter and operating effects matter as much as the quarter's oil-price environment.

## What shaped cash and earnings

### 1. Strong reported earnings do not identify the durable margin

Exxon's adjusted earnings exceeded GAAP earnings by about $2.1bn. Its reconciliation includes $2.541bn of estimated timing effects, mainly unsettled derivative marks before physical-shipment recognition and estimated derivative/physical timing differences, primarily LIFO; identified items added $449m. Upstream higher crude and gas realizations added $3.680bn to earnings, while Middle East volume disruption reduced earnings by $860m. Energy Products also benefited from stronger refining margins and optimization, which added $7.660bn to segment earnings, while estimated timing effects reduced it by $1.980bn.

Shell's Q3 operating cash flow included $3.4bn of commodity-derivative inflows, while $3.2bn of tax paid and about $2.2bn of timing outflows for emissions-certificate and biofuel-program payments in Europe and North America affected cash. Chemicals and Products generated $8.527bn segment cash flow, but included $1.454bn of derivative inflows and $1.239bn of working-capital inflow. Marketing cash flow was negative $210m. Shell's cash-capex figure included ARC acquisition consideration; divestment proceeds also supported investing cash flow and FCF.

These are not symmetric adjustments. For Exxon, large derivative and LIFO timing effects bridged GAAP and adjusted earnings. For Shell, derivative cash flows and acquisition/divestiture cash moved operating/investing cash. Use each issuer's own reconciliations and keep recurring operating earnings, timing effects, working capital, divestments and acquisitions in separate rows.

### 2. Production volume is not a pure operating-performance measure

Exxon reported 4.496m boe/d, down 273k boe/d year-on-year. Management attributed the decrease mainly to Middle East and Kazakhstan disruptions and lower Guyana entitlement volumes, partly offset by Permian and Guyana growth. Price/spend/other entitlements reduced the comparison by 78k boe/d; divestments reduced it by 34k boe/d. Production-sharing agreements can change entitlement volumes when prices or spending change, so reported oil-equivalent production may move without a like-for-like change in operated physical output.

Shell reported 2.574m boe/d available for sale; its quarter-on-quarter increase included ARC, completed in September, with about 370k boe/d contribution cited by management. Before reading this as organic production growth, split acquired volumes from the pre-existing portfolio and compare with the prior ownership perimeter. Shell reported LNG liquefaction of 7.43 Mt and LNG sales of 19.25 Mt; those are different physical measures and do not equal the group's production volume.

### 3. Cash-flow strength needs a working-capital and investment bridge

Exxon reported $25.650bn operating cash flow and $19.109bn company-defined FCF, while working capital used $2.216bn. CFO excluding working-capital movement was $23.434bn. Q3 cash capex was $7.002bn, but the company's FCF reconciliation also includes additional investments, other investing cash flows, asset-sale/return proceeds and non-controlling-interest project flows. It should not be recreated as a simple CFO-minus-capex calculation.

Shell reported $21.043bn operating cash flow, including a $638m working-capital outflow, and $16.322bn company-defined FCF. Its FCF is cash from operations plus investing cash flow; Q3 investing cash flow was negative $4.721bn, with $7.922bn cash capex partly offset by about $2.7bn of divestment proceeds. The ARC acquisition makes Shell's reported capex and net-debt change particularly sensitive to portfolio transactions in this quarter.

### 4. Net debt and liquidity definitions can reverse a casual peer read

Exxon ended September with $12.153bn cash and $34.3bn total debt; reported net debt was $22.1bn. The company excludes restricted cash in its net-debt calculation and reported debt-to-capital of 11.2% and net-debt-to-capital of 7.5%. It also reported $7.4bn in undrawn short-term lines and $0.3bn long-term lines.

Shell reported $36.425bn cash, $76.075bn total debt and $39.733bn net debt under its definition, which includes $34.147bn of lease liabilities and reflects debt-related derivatives/collateral adjustments. Net debt excluding lease liabilities was $5.586bn, with gearing 2.7% versus 16.6% including leases. ARC added about $2.5bn net debt plus leases; the financing and acquired perimeter changed during the quarter. Comparing Shell's lease-inclusive figure with Exxon's net-debt measure without normalizing leases, restricted cash and adjustments would be misleading.

## Operating mix and commodity-price exposure

Exxon did not publish a clean numerical Q3 average realized oil/gas price table in the reviewed release or 10-Q. It disclosed a qualitative earnings bridge showing that higher crude and gas realizations added $3.680bn to Upstream earnings. Do not infer a precise realized price from the segment earnings movement.

The reports show integrated major exposure is not equivalent to long crude. Production-sharing entitlements, refinery throughput/margins, LNG trading, chemical spreads, physical inventory, LIFO and derivative timing can offset or amplify upstream revenue effects. Shell also reported robust LNG trading and refinery utilization above 100% excluding Rheinland; Exxon cited historically high global refining margins. These segment factors make direct stock-price or FCF beta estimates from a single spot-price move unreliable.

No common company-provided oil-price sensitivity table was used in this comparison. A defensible next step would collect each issuer's disclosed sensitivities, match the same benchmark/tenor, then model volume, entitlement, realized-price, refinery/trading, FX, tax, working-capital and capex channels separately. If a company does not provide a needed sensitivity, label the model input as analyst-estimated.

## Monitoring checklist for the next quarter

1. Rebuild the perimeter bridge: acquired/divested volumes, equity-accounted and non-controlling interests, production-sharing entitlements and operational disruptions.
2. Reconcile adjusted earnings to GAAP and identify which adjustments are non-cash, timing-related, acquisition-related or cash costs.
3. Bridge CFO and issuer-defined FCF through working capital, capex, acquisition purchases, divestment proceeds, tax and derivative cash flows.
4. Normalize net debt for leases, restricted cash, financing derivatives, collateral and quarter-end transactions before comparing balance-sheet capacity.
5. Separate buybacks and dividends from acquisition spending and asset sales. Compare returns with operating cash generation over several quarters, not one exceptionally volatile period.
6. Record the contemporaneous oil/gas curves, refining cracks and analyst expectations before the next report. Without a point-in-time benchmark, the reports do not show what the market had priced.

## Boundaries

This comparison does not estimate equity value, specify a share-price target or infer a trade. Exxon and Shell publish different accounting and non-GAAP measures; the headline rows are navigation aids, not a normalized ranking. The operating and cash-flow discussion needs a next pass using matched segment definitions, commodity sensitivities, valuation and a frozen expectations snapshot.
