# Gold market brief — 7 November 2026

**Information cutoff:** Saturday, 7 November 2026 (London).  
**Market observations:** October month-end, plus US releases and market close on Friday, 6 November.  
**Scope:** Macro, flows and positioning in USD gold. This is analytical research, not an instruction to trade.

## Readout

**Near-term read: mixed, high event sensitivity.** WGC reports that benchmark gold fell 6.3% over its calendar-month October window. Treasury's published 10-year real yield was lower on 29 October (2.63%) than on 1 October (2.88%). These adjacent but not exactly matched windows suggest higher real yields alone do not explain the decline; they are not a synchronized return comparison. During October, global physically backed ETF holdings nevertheless rose 19 tonnes; futures positioning contracted. WGC attributes the decline plausibly to de-grossing, asset rotation into Treasuries, and Fed-path repricing, while acknowledging a model residual. This makes positioning/liquidity and cross-asset portfolio rebalancing material explanations to test alongside rates.

The first November week then produced a sharp rebound followed by a pullback that coincided with the payroll release. Friday’s October payroll report showed +165,000 jobs and 4.1% unemployment; the wage measure rose 0.4% month over month and 3.1% over 12 months. Gold futures settled at $4,074.90/oz, down 0.9% on Friday, while spot gold remained up 5.2% for the week. On Friday, nominal 10-year Treasury yield ended at 4.86% and the 10-year real yield at 2.63%. This intraday/multi-day divergence is consistent with high sensitivity to news and positioning; it does not establish a durable trend.

**Longer-horizon backdrop: supportive demand, but not a reliable short-term floor.** WGC estimated 321 tonnes of central-bank net demand in Q3, of which only 120 tonnes was reported and about 201 tonnes was estimated as unreported activity. That is useful evidence of strategic demand but includes estimation and reporting-lag risk. China exchange data add physical-market context: SGE reported 613.3 tonnes delivered and 92.1 tonnes withdrawn in October, while SHFE registered gold warrants rose 1.5 tonnes to 118.4 tonnes by 6 November. The Perth Mint also reported 32,933 oz of minted gold products shipped worldwide in October and PMGOLD holdings up 5,296 oz to 409,020 oz. The shipment series excludes cast bars and storage activity; listed-product holdings are a separate channel. These measures do not estimate final global consumption or a global tightness balance. Neither channel can offset a sudden rise in real yields or forced selling on a known timetable. [SGE October data](https://en.sge.com.cn/upload/file/202611/04/daed424d-8d57-4769-8409-74547a3fc1af.pdf), [SHFE weekly inventory](https://www.shfe.com.cn/data/tradedata/future/stockdata/weeklystock_20261106/ZH/all.html), [Perth Mint October update](https://www.perthmint.com/news/investor/market-research-and-analysis/october-2026-sales-update/)

**Confidence:** Medium that flow/liquidity dynamics materially contributed to October; low on the relative size of each driver and on a short-term directional call. The key next macro test is October US CPI, scheduled for Tuesday, 10 November at 8:30 a.m. ET. BLS says most October 2025 CPI indexes were not published, so many October 2026 year-over-year changes will also be unavailable; prioritize monthly headline/core readings and component detail, and do not treat private annual-rate reconstructions as official BLS values. See the [October CPI pre-release plan](us-october-2026-cpi-pre-release-plan.md) and [BLS shutdown-impact FAQ](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/additional-resources/2025-federal-government-shutdown-impact-cpi-faq.htm).

## Reconciling the move: a plausible chain, not a proven attribution

1. **October price action diverged from a simple real-rate model.** Real yields eased about 25 basis points from 1 October to 29 October, but gold fell 6.3% for the month. That points toward additional drivers, but it does not mean rates ceased to matter.
2. **Investor channels diverged.** Physical ETF holdings increased 19 tonnes, while managed-money futures net length fell over October (WGC estimates a 53-tonne-equivalent reduction). The 3 November Bank Participation Report also shows banks with much larger gross COMEX gold futures shorts than longs, but it cannot tell whether those futures hedge OTC, client or inventory exposure. COT and the Bank Participation Report draw on the same large-trader reporting system and can overlap at the entity level; treat them as different classifications of overlapping reportable activity, not independent flow confirmation. Together with ETF holdings, they are consistent with strategic listed-fund buying being outweighed at the margin by positioning reduction and broader portfolio rotation; they do not identify the ultimate seller.
3. **The WGC attribution is a useful hypothesis, not a causal decomposition.** Its rolling five-year regression says lower yields supported gold in October, even as the two moved in the same direction, and the unexplained residual was negative. Its narrative—Fed-path repricing, speculative de-grossing, and rotation from equities/gold into bonds—is plausible but depends partly on market commentary and proprietary underlying data. The model does not identify every seller or prove which channel caused the decline.
4. **Early November did not resolve the story.** Gold recovered sharply from the 30 October low through 5 November. The CFTC Tuesday snapshot still showed managed-money net length contracting. Friday’s payroll release coincided with a modest Treasury-yield increase and a gold decline; this timing alone does not establish that payrolls caused either move. One cannot label the weekly rebound a new uptrend from these observations alone.
5. **Demand data sit on different clocks.** ETF data are monthly and relatively timely; COT is weekly but reports a Tuesday snapshot; central-bank demand is quarterly and partly estimated; real yields and prices are daily. A strong long-run central-bank story does not invalidate a short-run liquidity squeeze.

## WGC “interim bottom” framework: test it, do not adopt it

The WGC tested three candidate conditions in bear regimes since 2005: (1) gold unusually far below its 200-day average, (2) a sharp 52-week dollar rise, and (3) speculative futures positioning unusually low relative to open interest. It reports that at least two conditions were present in 10 historical episodes, with 90% positive three-month returns and a 4.2% median return; all three conditions were present in five episodes, all positive, with a 9.8% median return.

This is an interesting prior, but the small sample is a serious limit: ten and five episodes are not enough to establish a robust forecasting edge. The “most extreme 30%” thresholds are selected within bear regimes and therefore require careful point-in-time implementation. The following checks separate uncertainty about the reported hit rate from the work needed to replicate and extend the rule.

### How much uncertainty is hidden in 9/10 and 5/5?

Treating each episode as an independent positive/negative three-month outcome, exact 95% binomial confidence intervals for the underlying hit rate are approximately **55.5%–99.7% for 9/10** and **47.8%–100% for 5/5**. The observed 90% and 100% hit rates therefore do not pin down the true forward hit rate with useful precision. A simple fair-coin sign test would give one-sided probabilities of 1.07% for 9/10 and 3.13% for 5/5, but those are **not valid adjusted significance claims here**: the indicator family was explored before the three rules were selected, episode independence is uncertain, and the sample is regime-clustered. The intervals and sign probabilities are optimistic reference calculations, not a corrected test of the published rule.

There is also a useful baseline comparison in WGC's own table: its 24 observations labeled “All bear months,” sampled at month-end and spaced at least three months apart, had a 63% positive rate and 3.2% median three-month return; the “at least two” subset had 90% and 4.2%. This is a selected, spaced in-sample baseline, not every bear month or an unconditional market base rate. The median-return difference is only 1.0 percentage point in-sample, before fees, execution timing, or selection adjustment. The stronger apparent change is in hit rate, precisely the statistic with wide uncertainty at n=10.

### Replication plan that would make the claim testable

1. **Faithful reproduction:** obtain the exact daily gold price, DXY and CFTC position series used by WGC, plus its operational definitions and licensed/source data where required; reproduce its bear-regime definition (50-day average below the 200-day for at least 20 consecutive trading days), its three indicator definitions, its “most extreme 30%” thresholds, its month-end sampling, its three-month forward return and its minimum three-month spacing. WGC cites Bloomberg and its own data; exact reproduction depends on access to those inputs and definitions. First reproduce the published 24, 10 and 5 observations and table statistics. If only public substitute series are available, label the result a **public-proxy reconstruction**, not a faithful replication. Any mismatch must be resolved before interpretation.
2. **Point-in-time version:** separately rebuild every signal using only data public by the month-end decision time. For COT, use the latest report actually released by then, which reflects the earlier Tuesday snapshot; never insert a later weekly report into the month-end signal. Calculate percentile cutoffs from prior data only. This version is implementable but is not expected to match WGC's retrospectively sampled series exactly.
3. **Freeze before extension:** document the feature definitions, thresholds, signal spacing, forecast horizon, and baseline now. Do not tune them against later observations. The forward ledger should save the signal, price, COT release date, data vintage, and three-month outcome for every eligible month-end.
4. **Judge incremental information:** compare the signal with the same bear-regime baseline, unconditional three-month gold returns, a simple trend-only rule, and a passive exposure-matched hold. Report hit rate, median/mean excess return, drawdown, and distributions; include uncertainty clustered by bear episode. A higher hit rate without higher risk-adjusted net return is not enough for a trading claim.
5. **Keep forecasting separate from execution:** first establish predictive value on untouched forward observations. Only then simulate the intended instrument (MeridianX cTrader demo XAUUSD feed), its quote timing, bid/ask, spread, financing and order rules. WGC's LBMA benchmark returns are not cTrader execution returns.

**Current status:** the published WGC result is a research lead, not independently replicated or out-of-sample validated in this work. It should not be used as an automated entry rule. A 3-month forecast horizon also means early signals cannot be judged until their full label window has elapsed.

## What we can and cannot say

**Supported by available data:** October gold weakness coincided with strong ETF additions and falling futures positioning, while real yields fell through most of October. The divergence suggests other flow and portfolio-rebalancing forces mattered. Strategic central-bank demand remained strong in the latest quarterly estimate.

**Not supported:** that central banks caused the weekly rebound; that a bottom is in; that the labor report guarantees a Fed hike; or that the October price decline was “irrational.” The evidence is not sufficiently timely or complete to identify all OTC activity, dealer exposure, or the causal contribution of each flow.

- **Primary official releases:** BLS, US Treasury, Federal Reserve and CFTC.
