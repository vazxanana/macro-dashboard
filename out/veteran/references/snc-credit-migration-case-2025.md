# Shared National Credit: read the 2025 non-pass decline with its denominator

**Information date:** 12 January 2026, the date of the 2025 Shared National Credit (SNC) report release  
**Observation date:** Facilities originated on or before 30 June 2025  
**Purpose:** A worked example in reading an annual credit-quality report without mistaking a falling ratio for a broad improvement in underwriting.

## What the official report said

The Federal Reserve, FDIC and OCC reported **$6.9 trillion** of SNC commitments in the 2025 review, up **6%** from the previous year. The share of commitments rated non-pass fell to **8.6%** from **9.1%** in 2024. The agencies said the decline was primarily driven by growth in new commitments, rather than an improvement in the underlying credit quality of the portfolio.

The composition also matters:

| Published measure | 2025 observation | Read it as |
|---|---:|---|
| Total SNC commitments | $6.9tn, +6% year over year (about +$389.5bn) | The denominator expanded materially. |
| Non-pass commitments | $592.9bn, down $2.9bn (-0.5%) from $595.8bn | The dollar amount edged down while the denominator grew much faster. |
| Non-pass share of commitments | 8.6%, versus 9.1% in 2024 | A lower ratio does not by itself show healthier underlying credit; the agencies attributed it mainly to new commitment growth. |
| Nonaccrual commitments | $84.9bn, up 30.4% from $65.1bn | A separate distress measure worsened even as non-pass commitments declined slightly. It is not the same classification as non-pass and should be tracked alongside it. |
| Leveraged-loan share of non-pass commitments | 81% | Weak classifications were heavily concentrated in leveraged loans within the reviewed universe. |
| U.S. banks' share of all SNC commitments | 45% | Banks held under half of the full commitment amount. |
| U.S. banks' share of non-pass commitments | 22% | Their share of non-pass commitments was much lower than their share of all commitments. |

## What follows—and what does not

The observable story is **portfolio growth alongside a modest decline in the reported non-pass commitment ratio**, with non-pass exposure concentrated in leveraged lending and a lender mix that differs from the overall commitment mix. The regulator's own explanation rejects a simple “credit quality improved” reading of the lower ratio.

These aggregates do not identify which borrowers are deteriorating, how much loss a lender may incur, or whether a particular loan is marked at current market value. The SNC definition covers large shared credits—generally commitments aggregating at least $100 million shared among three or more unaffiliated federally supervised institutions, or a portion sold to at least two such institutions. The report is a supervisory classification snapshot, not daily trading data or a borrower-level public tape.

## A repeatable analyst check

1. Record non-pass **commitment dollars**, non-pass **share of total commitments**, and nonaccrual commitments. Keep the reviewed population and cutoff date fixed when comparing years.
2. Decompose the change into numerator and denominator. If commitments grow much faster than non-pass commitments, the ratio can fall even when non-pass dollars barely move; check whether related stress indicators, such as nonaccrual commitments, are rising.
3. Track leveraged-loan and lender-type shares separately. A change in the overall total can conceal a shift in where risk sits.
4. For a named issuer, move from this sector-level signal to the actual credit agreement, SEC filings, rating actions, refinancing calendar, and traded bond or loan evidence. Do not infer issuer distress from the SNC aggregate alone.
5. Keep the observation date and publication date separate. The 2025 report appeared in January 2026 for a June 2025 review cutoff; the lag is material and the next annual vintage may not follow an identical schedule.
