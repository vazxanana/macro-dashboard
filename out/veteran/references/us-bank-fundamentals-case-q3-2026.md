# U.S. bank fundamentals case — Q3 2026

**Cutoff:** filings public by 8 November 2026. All four reports cover the quarter ended 30 September 2026. Dollar figures are USD. This filing-based comparison studies how rates, credit, liquidity and capital policy enter bank results; it is not a valuation or trading recommendation.

## The comparison problem

Large U.S. banks report under U.S. GAAP, but the same labels can still hide different business mixes, denominators, legal entities and company adjustments. Start with the filed 10-Q for final quarter-end balances and regulatory ratios, then use the earnings release and supplement for management's operating detail. Do not read one quarter's reported profit as a clean forecast of run-rate earnings: a securities loss, regulatory settlement, loan-loss provision, fee cycle, asset mix or segment reclassification can move the headline in very different ways.

Primary filing links: [JPMorgan Chase Q3 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/19617/000162828026069523/jpm-20260930.htm), [Bank of America Q3 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/70858/000007085826000548/bac-20260930.htm), [Citigroup Q3 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/831001/000083100126000056/c-20260930.htm), and [Wells Fargo Q3 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/72971/000007297126000327/wfc-20260930.htm). Their Q3 releases were published 13–14 October; the filed 10-Qs followed by 28 October for Wells, 30 October for Bank of America, 3 November for JPMorgan and 5 November for Citi.

## Earnings quality and the rate channel

### Headline income contains different one-off and mix effects

- **JPMorgan:** reported net income was $15.305bn. The release also showed $15.8bn of net income excluding a $658m pre-tax ($498m after-tax) discretionary investment-securities loss. That is a narrow non-GAAP bridge, not a full-cycle earnings measure. Net income applicable to common shareholders was $14.936bn.
- **Bank of America:** reported net income was $6.933bn; its $8.1bn adjusted figure excluded a $1.2bn non-tax-deductible regulatory settlement accrual. The charge is separate from recurring deposit-cost and securities-portfolio effects and should stay visible in the GAAP result.
- **Citi:** reported net income was $5.245bn. Its 40% year-on-year increase partly reflects comparison with a $714m after-tax Banamex goodwill impairment in Q3 2025; use the company's ex-notable-item comparison only as a separately defined bridge.
- **Wells Fargo:** reported net income was $5.951bn. Its net interest income rose 5% year-on-year, while provision for credit losses increased from $681m to $1.029bn.

### NII is not a complete margin comparison

Reported NII was $27.036bn at JPMorgan, $16.449bn at BofA, $16.982bn at Citi and $12.517bn at Wells Fargo. Their taxable-equivalent (FTE) amounts were $27.145bn, $16.610bn, $17.009bn and $12.591bn, respectively; cite the exact table if using FTE in a model. The FTE adjustment is not GAAP NII.

For its overall balance sheet, JPMorgan reports “net yield on average interest-earning assets”: 2.48% on a managed basis, rather than calling that a standard firmwide NIM; its non-Markets measure was 3.70% and is non-GAAP. BofA calls its FTE ratio “net interest yield” and reported 2.16%. Citi reported 2.43% NIM on an FTE basis. Wells reported 2.43% FTE NIM, down from 2.61% a year earlier; growth in lower-yielding Markets assets and interest-bearing deposits weighed on the ratio. These ratios have different mixes and labels; compare them only after reconstructing each numerator, average earning-asset denominator and annualization.

Deposit beta is not a standardized metric. BofA showed average interest-bearing deposit cost of 2.03% versus 2.40% a year earlier; Wells reported average deposit cost of 1.56% versus 1.54%; JPMorgan described average deposits up 8% year over year and average loans up 10%; Citi's higher deposit beta reduced the marginal benefit in its rising-rate sensitivity. For a calculated beta, state benchmark rate, date window, lag, account scope and denominator, and separate noninterest-bearing accounts from rate-bearing deposits and CDs. A single quarter's average cost is not a deposit beta or a stable estimate of next-quarter NII.

### Portfolio and segment mix alter the apparent rate sensitivity

BofA's average earning-asset base includes more than $600bn of balances in Federal Reserve deposits, repo, trading assets, securities and other earning assets. Wells said lower-yielding Markets assets and interest-bearing deposits reduced reported NIM year on year, while Markets NII rose to $428m from $144m. JPMorgan's NII excluding Markets was $24.726bn, but this is a non-GAAP company measure. Citi's NII and NIM also reflect a large global Services and Markets franchise. Separate average balances, asset yields, deposit costs, noninterest income and hedge/timing effects before translating a change in the policy rate or curve into earnings.

## Credit costs and loss absorption

The provision numbers in the snapshot have different accounting perimeters. Citi's $2.586bn reported line includes provisions for credit losses **and benefits and claims**; its loan-only provision was $2.416bn, made up of $2.217bn of net loan credit losses and a $199m loan allowance build. BofA reported a $1.269bn provision and $1.349bn of net charge-offs. Wells reported a $1.029bn provision. JPMorgan reported $2.743bn total provision, with $2.2bn net charge-offs and a $539m net reserve build. These are issuer-line facts; align credit commitments, securities, insurance-benefit items and unfunded exposures before building a peer series.

Product-level readings also differ. JPMorgan's Card Services net charge-off rate was 2.91%; BofA's card rate was 3.22%; Citi reported 3.51% for its Global Payment and Credit Card average-loan portfolio and 4.19% for private-label cards; Wells reported 3.73%. Product definitions, portfolios, average balance denominators and charge-off timing are not identical, so these observations motivate investigation rather than establish a card-loss ranking.

| Wells Fargo | Loan ACL $14.458bn, or 1.38% of total loans; NPAs were $7.4bn, or 0.70% of loans. | An ACL ratio is an accounting estimate over a defined portfolio, not a standalone measure of collateral liquidation or future stress. |

For a live research series, retain each company's allowance roll-forward, provision, gross and net charge-offs, delinquency buckets, nonaccruals, criticized/classified balances, modifications, and the related average or ending denominator. For CRE, compare property type, maturity, criticized status, collateral, extensions and loan commitments. For cards and autos, use vintage, risk-score and delinquency-transition tables. U.S. CECL allowance estimates are not directly comparable with IFRS 9 staging in non-U.S. bank peers.

### Office CRE is a perimeter test

The available Q3 office indicators are labeled differently:

| Issuer | Filed observation | Scope control |
|---|---|---|
| JPMorgan | $14.676bn office credit exposure, including loans/commitments plus $4m derivatives; 77% drawn. | This is a credit exposure measure, not office-only losses or a reserve. |
| BofA | $10.908bn office loans; office criticized exposure $2.6bn. | Loan balances and criticized exposures are separate measures; the latter is not the full office portfolio. |
| Citi | The filing did not provide a separate office-specific exposure table; it reported broader mortgage/real-estate corporate-loan categories. | Do not relabel a broad real-estate category as office CRE. |
| Wells Fargo | $18.643bn office loans outstanding, including $1.876bn nonaccrual; $19.669bn total commitments. | Outstanding loans, nonaccrual loans and total commitments have different denominators; commitments are not drawn balances. |

These are not a normalized league table. Keep drawn loans, undrawn commitments, derivative exposure, criticized assets, nonaccruals and loss reserves in separate fields. An office maturity is a refinancing checkpoint, not an expected loss without information on borrower equity, collateral, debt service, recourse and extension terms.

| BofA | 10.9% standardized CET1; $198.407bn capital and $1.821tn standardized RWA. Requirement was 10.0% (90bp CET1 cushion); a disclosed 50bp G-SIB surcharge step-up on 1 Jan 2027 would increase the minimum, all else equal. | Citi | 12.77% standardized CET1 versus an 11.6% requirement; 117bp buffer. Advanced CET1 was 12.00% against 10.5%. | SLR 4.99%. The regulatory figures here come from the filed 10-Q; the earlier release showed rounded preliminary ratios. |
| Wells Fargo | 10.03% standardized CET1 versus an 8.50% requirement; 1.53 percentage-point buffer. Standardized RWA was $1.341tn. | Average LCR was 122%. Its available liquidity sources include pledged collateral and are not all unencumbered or transferable among legal entities. The Federal Reserve lifted the asset-growth restriction in 2025 and terminated its related enforcement action in March 2026; do not describe the old cap as current. |

The ratio alone is not “excess capital.” Verify actual requirements, stress-capital buffer, G-SIB surcharge, countercyclical buffer, RWA movement, leverage rules, AOCI treatment and distribution restrictions for each legal entity. A change in the denominator can move CET1 even when capital dollars are steady. At BofA, the 90bp CET1 cushion was not the binding overall constraint: Standardized total capital was 14.2% against a 13.5% requirement, a 70bp cushion. Distinguish completed common dividends and net repurchases from authorizations. JPMorgan reported $4.433bn of common dividends and $664m of net repurchases in Q3; Citi returned $4.142bn ($3bn repurchases and $1.142bn dividends); BofA bought back about $6bn of common stock and paid about $2.2bn of common dividends; Wells repurchased $2.5bn and declared a $0.50-per-share dividend.

## Quarter-specific comparability breaks to retain

- Citi's 2026 segment presentation changed: Retail Banking moved from the former U.S. Personal Banking presentation into Wealth and the remaining businesses into U.S. Consumer Cards; other TCE and interest-rate-risk allocations also changed. The 10-Q says consolidated results were unchanged, but segment history must use the restated comparison series.
- Wells Fargo's asset cap is no longer in force: the Federal Reserve removed the growth restriction in June 2025 and terminated its enforcement action in March 2026. The change affects interpretation of post-cap asset growth and is distinct from today's capital, liquidity or credit constraints.
- BofA disclosed a forthcoming G-SIB surcharge change. Its stated capital headroom can narrow without any immediate deterioration in assets if the requirement rises as announced; recheck the official effective-date disclosures each reporting quarter.
- All bank deposit, loan and liquidity figures should be timestamped as average, period-end, legal-entity or consolidated measures. Each can tell a different story about a quarter-end liquidity event or a change in product mix.

## Monitoring plan for the next report

1. Freeze the expected Fed path and curve shape before earnings; record consensus, deposit rates, short-term funding, and the date/time of each observation.
2. Rebuild reported NII and any FTE/managed/ex-Markets measures separately; decompose average asset balances, yields, deposit mix/cost, wholesale funding and hedges.
3. Track provision and charge-offs separately, then reconcile the allowance roll-forward and changes in criticized, delinquent, nonaccrual and modified assets by product.
4. Refresh office/CRE maturities and property-level disclosures without conflating commitments, drawn loans, criticized loans, nonaccruals or loss reserves.
5. Compare final CET1 capital, RWA and legal-entity ratios with each bank's current requirements, upcoming buffers and actual capital returns.
6. Review AFS/HTM marks, deposit composition, unencumbered collateral, HQLA and facility use. State which liquidity measures are regulatory, company-defined, bank-level or holding-company-level.
7. Save the initial release, filed 10-Q, supplement and any amendments separately. Point-in-time work should use only facts public at the decision timestamp.

**Boundary:** This case describes the filings; it does not estimate equity value, identify a preferred bank, or claim that a single quarter establishes a sector trend. A decision-ready investment case would still need a frozen expectations snapshot, valuation, matched multi-quarter histories and subsequent resolution data.
