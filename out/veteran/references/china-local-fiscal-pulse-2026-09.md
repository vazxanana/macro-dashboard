# China local fiscal pulse: September 2026 cumulative accounts

**As of:** 8 November 2026. The latest release used here covers January–September and was published by the Ministry of Finance on 23 October 2026.  
**Question:** What do the latest national and local fiscal-execution figures say about property-linked revenue and public spending?  
**Decision supported:** Add a fiscal-execution cross-check to China property, credit and growth research. This is a data note, not a market forecast.  
**Conclusion:** The release shows a clear split between the general public budget and government-fund accounts. Jan–Sep general-budget revenue rose 5.1% y/y while spending rose 1.0%; local own general-budget revenue rose 2.7% and local general-budget spending 0.2%. By contrast, local government-fund revenue fell 22.3%, with land-use-right transfer receipts down 27.9%; related spending fell 18.5%. This points to pressure in a property-linked local revenue channel, but does not by itself measure home demand, the net local fiscal impulse or LGFV funding capacity.

## Source and units

The MOF monthly [National Fiscal Revenue and Expenditure archive](https://www.mof.gov.cn/zhengwuxinxi/redianzhuanti/quanguocaizhengshouzhiqingkuang/) links dated Chinese-language releases. The [September 2026 report](https://gks.mof.gov.cn/tongjishuju/202610/t20261023_4003380.htm) reports cumulative Jan–Sep values, nominally, in 亿元 (100 million yuan). Divide by 10,000 to convert 亿元 to trillion yuan: for example, 21,328 亿元 = ¥2.1328tn. The report's own year-over-year rates are cumulative-comparison rates.

**Calculated scale check:** 16,087 ÷ 21,328 = about **75.4%**. Thus reported land-transfer revenue was roughly three-quarters of local government-fund revenue in this cumulative period. This ratio does not mean that all local funding or spending depends on land transfers; other accounts, transfers, borrowing and carryovers sit outside that simple comparison.

## Interpretation and limits

- **Observed:** Land-transfer revenue was down 27.9% year over year through September, while local government-fund revenue was down 22.3%. The land-linked revenue decline is therefore a large part of weakness in this local account, but the aggregate also includes other receipts.
- **Observed:** Local general-budget spending was nearly flat (+0.2%), and local government-fund spending declined 11.6%. This indicates weaker nominal execution growth in those reported lines; it is not a complete measure of public demand or real fiscal impulse.
- **Interpretation:** The figures are consistent with a strained property-linked local revenue base and slower spending growth. They do not identify the amount of cash available to local governments or financing vehicles, the timing of auction settlements, or whether borrowing and transfers offset revenue weakness.
- **Not established:** A land-transfer receipt is not a home sale, land price index, net local fiscal income, unsold inventory measure, or direct LGFV cash-flow statement. It is a fiscal receipt with its own collection timing and budget treatment.
- **Point-in-time rule:** Archive each release as published. To estimate a single month, subtract the previous cumulative report only after matching the same account definitions and checking for revisions or supplementary releases. Never substitute a later revised series into an event-time reconstruction.
