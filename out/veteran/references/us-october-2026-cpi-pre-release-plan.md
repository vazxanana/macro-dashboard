## The data issue that changes the event

BLS says it could not collect most October 2025 CPI survey data and did not publish an October 2025 CPI release. It explicitly states that it will not publish October 2026 12-month percentage changes for indexes whose October 2025 index is missing. Therefore, do not assume that October 2026 headline CPI-U or core CPI will have a normal official year-over-year rate. Check the actual first-release tables and footnotes. If an annual number appears in a news preview, vendor calendar, or model, determine whether it is a BLS-published index comparison, a private reconstruction, or a forecast; never label those interchangeably. The October month-over-month seasonally adjusted changes and the component detail are the main planned observations. [BLS shutdown-impact FAQ](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/additional-resources/2025-federal-government-shutdown-impact-cpi-faq.htm), [archived BLS September CPI release](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_10142026.htm)

This is not a routine base-effect footnote. Missing October 2025 reference indexes mean some familiar year-over-year numbers cannot be calculated under the official published series. Do not present an October 2026 headline or core CPI-U y/y as a BLS figure. The Cleveland Fed's October y/y nowcasts use its own October 2025 estimate in place of the missing BLS data, so they are model estimates rather than a BLS comparison. BLS also planned to finalize October–December 2025 chained CPI-U values on 10 November; inspect the actual Table 5 before describing any C-CPI-U value. Treat those C-CPI-U estimates as a separate companion data item, not as headline/core CPI-U or part of the main CPI surprise. C-CPI-U is a separate index and does not restore the missing headline/core CPI-U comparison. [Cleveland Fed nowcast and methods](https://www.clevelandfed.org/indicators-and-data/inflation-nowcasting), [BLS shutdown-impact FAQ](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/additional-resources/2025-federal-government-shutdown-impact-cpi-faq.htm)

## At release: fields to transcribe

Use the official BLS release and retain the original table or page, publication timestamp, retrieval timestamp, and file hash if archived.

| Field | Record | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Headline CPI-U | SA monthly percentage change; index level if published | Main monthly broad-price signal. |
| Core CPI-U | SA monthly percentage change; index level if published | Helps distinguish broad persistence from energy/food volatility. |
| Major components | Food, energy, gasoline, shelter/rent/OER, core goods, services and other detailed categories; m/m changes, relative importance and Table 6 seasonally adjusted effects on All Items where provided | Establishes breadth and contribution to the headline; a component price change or relative importance alone is not its contribution. |
| Year-over-year values | Copy only what BLS actually publishes; mark missing with BLS footnote | October 2025 index gaps prevent the headline/core CPI-U 12-month comparisons. Some component series may differ; check BLS footnotes. C-CPI-U is separate; preliminary estimates are revised until final, so retain the status and reference period. Never fill a missing official value with a private estimate. |
| Prior/revisions | Prior-period changes as shown in the release, including revised seasonal factors when applicable | Separates a fresh surprise from a changed comparison base. |
| Consensus | Exact pre-release provider snapshot for the same field and unit | The surprise is actual minus a comparable expectation; there is no single provider-independent consensus. |

Calculate a raw surprise only for comparable fields: `first-release actual − frozen expectation`, in percentage points. Preserve each source's published rounding and units; do not imply precision finer than the published actual or expectation. Do not calculate a standardized surprise unless the historical surprise distribution is built point-in-time and uses the same provider/definition. Keep missing values missing; don't silently interpolate.

For C-CPI-U, distinguish the initial estimate, interim revisions, and final estimate. Do not describe a value already labeled final as ordinarily awaiting the preliminary-series revisions. [BLS chained CPI FAQ](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/additional-resources/chained-cpi-questions-and-answers.htm)

## Conditional interpretation matrix

These are hypotheses to test against market prices, not mechanical trading rules.

| CPI outcome | First interpretation | Evidence needed for a rates/gold channel |
|---|---|---|
| Headline and core both hotter, with broad component pressure | Stronger evidence of persistent inflation than an energy-only beat | Check whether the 2-year nominal yield and policy-path pricing rise, whether 10-year real yields rise, and whether USD strengthens. Gold weakness is consistent with this channel only if the gold feed confirms it after spread/quote checks. |
| Headline hotter mainly from energy while core is softer or unchanged | Headline impulse may be concentrated in volatile energy; persistence is less clear | Verify gasoline/energy detail, core breadth, and contemporaneous oil. Look for rates/FX confirmation before assigning a policy channel. |
| Core hotter while headline is modest | Underlying services/goods pressure may matter more than the headline | Inspect shelter, services ex-shelter if available, goods, and breadth; check 2-year rate and real-yield changes. |
| Headline and core both softer | Disinflationary evidence, conditional on component breadth and prior revisions | Check policy-path repricing, nominal and real yields, USD, and risk sentiment. Gold need not rise if the USD or other drivers offset the rate move. |
| Mixed components, substantial revisions, stale/poor quotes, or overlapping news | No clean CPI-only signal | Classify the event as mixed or confounded; preserve the evidence and lower causal confidence. |

Across all rows, the chain is a hypothesis: **CPI surprise → expected policy path / inflation compensation → nominal and real yields / USD → gold quote**. A coincident price move does not identify causation. Explain the move versus what was already priced and list counterevidence.

## Market response protocol

Set `t0` to the **observed BLS publication timestamp** (scheduled for 8:30 a.m. ET / 13:30 UTC if on time); record scheduled and actual timestamps separately in ET and UTC. Capture a pre-event drift window from −30 to −5 minutes, then response windows of **0–1, 0–5, 0–30, and 0–55 minutes**. If publication is on time, the 55-minute endpoint is 9:25 a.m. ET, five minutes before the US cash equity open; retain it as a pre-open window and note if another venue has opened. If publication is delayed, recompute every endpoint from actual `t0` and flag any window crossing the cash open. Keep **0–120 minutes** separate; label US cash close and next-session moves exploratory because they include additional news and changing liquidity. Since SIFMA recommends a full close in US dollar fixed-income markets on 11 November, label the next full bond-market comparison separately (expected Thursday, 12 November; verify venue calendars). For each window use a synchronized, same-source pre-release baseline and exact-horizon endpoint. For traded instruments calculate midpoint return as `100 × (mid_h / mid_pre − 1)`. If yields are quoted in percent, calculate basis-point change as `100 × (yield_h − yield_pre)`; for example, 4.10% to 4.15% is +5 bp. If the feed stores decimal fractions instead, multiply the difference by 10,000; record the input unit explicitly. Preserve bid, ask, midpoint/yield, spread, quote age, session, source and exact timestamp at both endpoints. Predeclare feed-specific maximum quote-age and spread filters before viewing the outcome; retain raw observations and flag missing/stale/filtered quotes rather than silently carrying prices forward.

Track:

- **Gold:** same MeridianX demo XAUUSD feed for broker-specific response; separately, a named spot benchmark or COMEX contract if licensed/available. Keep contracts/benchmarks and time conventions distinct.
- **USD:** a named and stable measure (for example ICE DXY only if that specific series is available and licensed); do not relabel a broad Federal Reserve dollar index as DXY.
- **Rates:** US 2-year and 10-year nominal Treasury yields; 10-year TIPS real yield; record source and observation time. Treasury daily curves are useful official end-of-day context, not event-minute prices. Use timestamped point-in-time market data for intraday response if licensed and accessible.
- **Additional confirmation:** named EUR/USD and USD/JPY quotes; 5-year nominal yields, real yields and breakevens if reliable; gold futures, silver, oil, copper, and broad equity-risk measures. Treat intraday real-yield/breakeven data as lower-confidence unless source, liquidity and timestamps are clear.
- **Policy expectations:** timestamped futures/OIS pricing from a named provider. Treat implied probabilities as market pricing under that contract/method, not objective probabilities.
- **Quote quality:** bid, ask, midpoint, spread, quote age, session, and any broker interruption. Ignore apparent jumps that cannot be reconciled with the quote record.

The first 5-minute reaction is a short-window response; the 2-hour/close/next-session moves are persistence tests and include more confounds. Use a single named consensus provider snapshot near 8:25 a.m. ET where possible, with its exact capture time and contributor count; preserve the source and do not mix vendors. The 6 November values above are preliminary context, not that frozen snapshot. If no comparable provider value or contributor count is available near release, record it as unavailable. A robust repeated-event study would estimate the reduced-form CPI surprise-to-gold response in narrow windows, standardize surprises using only past releases, screen overlapping events, include pre-event placebo windows and matched non-event controls, and report dependence-aware uncertainty and out-of-sample checks. Rates and FX are mechanism evidence; controlling for them in a total-effect model can remove part of the CPI transmission channel. Cross-asset co-movement alone does not establish causality.
