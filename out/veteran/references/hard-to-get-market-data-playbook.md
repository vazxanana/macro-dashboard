# Hard-to-get market data playbook: macro, flows and gold

**Prepared:** 7 November 2026  
**Purpose:** An analyst's guide to underused but useful public datasets for macro and gold research. This is a source map and research method, not a trading signal.

## How to use this guide

Most useful information is not truly secret. It is scattered across release calendars, exchange files, statistical tables, and source notes. The edge comes from knowing what each series measures, what it omits, when it becomes public, and whether it confirms or contradicts the price move.

For every observation, record four dates separately:

1. **Observation date** — when the underlying activity happened.
2. **Publication date/time** — when a trader could first have known it.
3. **Download/check date** — when the analyst retrieved the file.
4. **Revision date** — when a later release changed the historical value.

Never backdate a release. In a historical study, use the vintage available on the day, not today's revised series. [ALFRED](https://alfred.stlouisfed.org/help/downloaddata) can reconstruct first-release values for covered US series, but its vintage dates are not necessarily exact publication timestamps and its coverage is not universal; verify event-time assumptions against source-agency archives and release schedules.

**Vintage protocol:** preserve reference period, exact release/publication timestamp, first published value, prior value as then published, later revisions, seasonal-adjustment status, vintage identifier, and method/classification changes. Backtests join on first known availability, not period-end; when the source supplies only a vintage date, do not claim intraday timing. Forecast evaluation must state whether outcomes use the first release or latest revised value.

## The genuinely difficult information: what is observable and what is not

### 1. OTC gold positioning is only partly visible

London is a large OTC market. Public clearing data help describe settlement activity, but they do not expose each dealer's gross inventory, customer direction, or every matched trade. The daily LBMA Gold Price is a benchmark, not a complete transaction tape. Do not pretend that a public proxy reveals dealer books. Use triangulation: OTC clearing context, COMEX positioning and spreads, ETF flows, exchange inventory, SGE premiums/turnover, and price reaction around catalysts.

### 2. Official buying is opaque until it is reported

Central-bank reserve reports are delayed and do not show execution dates, counterparties, custody location or whether a reported change was a market purchase. Some central-bank buying is inferred by the WGC from residual supply-demand estimates and market intelligence; this estimate is useful but is not the same evidence as a named central bank's disclosed reserve series. Keep reported and inferred numbers separate and label confidence.

### 3. A price premium is not automatically a shortage

Shanghai premiums can reflect import quotas, taxes, FX conversion, local funding, market hours, logistics, or a genuine local imbalance. Normalize the benchmark, units, tax treatment and timestamp. Confirm with physical turnover, imports and delivery data. For futures, compare calendar spreads and delivery behavior before calling warehouse inventory “tight.”

### 4. The real yield is a constructed signal

The 10-year TIPS yield is a useful opportunity-cost proxy, not the exact discount rate of every gold holder. It can move because of real-rate expectations, term premium, liquidity and TIPS technicals. Compare nominal yields, inflation compensation, policy-rate expectations and the dollar. Use a basket of horizons (2-year, 5-year, 10-year) rather than assigning all gold moves to a single 10-year series.

### 5. Intraday dealer gamma and options flow are usually proprietary

Public options open interest is not the same as dealer exposure: it omits who is long or short, offsetting OTC positions, and dynamic hedges. Vendor “gamma walls” or “dealer positioning” are model outputs. Use them only if the vendor publishes a transparent method and you can reproduce it. Otherwise, treat them as hypotheses and test their out-of-sample value before risking money.

## A disciplined daily/weekly collection routine

### Daily (before interpreting the move)

- Save the XAUUSD or chosen gold instrument OHLC and the broker's timestamp/time zone; record spread and session coverage.
- Record DXY or a clearly defined USD basket, nominal Treasury yields, TIPS real yields and breakevens, with release/source timestamps.
- Check the gold move against silver, copper, oil and broad risk assets. Label these as cross-asset context, not causes.
- On material delivery or dislocation days, check CME stocks/notices and the SGE benchmark/premium using aligned time windows.
- Log the catalyst and the first market reaction, then the close reaction. A headline that fails to move prices can be useful evidence too.

### Weekly

- Update COT using the report's actual position date, not the date the web page was read. Express net positioning as a share of open interest and a rolling percentile.
- Check the WGC weekly ETF holdings data, distinguishing tonnes from currency AUM.
- Review the central-bank calendar and data release calendar for the next week's high-impact events.
- Compare price, real rates, dollar, ETF holdings/flows and positioning. If the familiar drivers disagree, investigate positioning, liquidity, local physical demand or event risk before forcing a story.

### Monthly / quarterly

- Reconcile WGC ETF commentary, official reserve releases, China customs/SGE material and LBMA clearing data.
- Review TIC and BIS for slower cross-border flow/liquidity context; record their data vintages and revisions.
- Refresh the macro regime table: inflation momentum, growth momentum, labor-market momentum, policy-rate repricing, real yields, dollar trend, credit conditions and geopolitical risk.
- Re-run the historical test with point-in-time vintages where available. State how many observations, which regime, what transaction cost and what would falsify the result.

## Research interpretation: a live dated illustration

This example demonstrates source triangulation using facts published by 7 November 2026; it is not a call on price direction.

- The WGC's October 2026 ETF report, published in early November, reports global holdings up 19 tonnes to 4,275 tonnes while ETF AUM fell 6% to $542bn because the gold price was lower during the month. Reading only AUM would suggest redemptions; reading tonnes and AUM together changes the interpretation. The report title highlights Europe, so regional flows should be inspected before generalizing the global total. [WGC October ETF report](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-etfs-holdings-and-flows/2026/11)
- The latest IMF COFER release available by this date covers 2026Q2. The IMF reports total FX reserves at $13.22tn, with the dollar share down to 56.70% from 57.18%, while euro claims rose nearly $100bn. It also notes that currency moves were range-bound and says FX translation effects were limited, but explicitly allows for other price changes and active reallocations. COFER excludes gold, so this is reserve-composition context, not a direct gold-purchase statistic. [IMF COFER release, 30 September 2026](https://data.imf.org/news/imf%20data%20brief%20september%2030)
- COT data are a Tuesday snapshot, not Friday's positioning. On 7 November the latest visible COMEX futures-only report identifies 3 November as its position date; 27 October is the prior comparison snapshot. State the position date and publication date separately. [CFTC COMEX report](https://www.cftc.gov/dea/futures/deacmxlf.htm)
- BIS LBS is a quarterly structural indicator. Its portal lists 29 October 2026 as the last release and 7 December as the next; it cannot validate a same-day claim of dollar funding stress. [BIS LBS release information](https://data.bis.org/topics/LBS)
- Treasury's August TIC release was available; September TIC was scheduled for 18 November. The August release is a lagged cross-border flow observation, not a current positioning read. Its custodial data cannot perfectly identify beneficial owners. [Treasury TIC release calendar](https://home.treasury.gov/data/treasury-international-capital-tic-system/release-dates-of-tic-data)

**What this supports:** multiple public measures can describe different investor channels and time scales. In this dated example, listed ETF holdings increased even as dollar AUM fell; official reserve allocation and bank cross-border data are slower; futures positioning has a known snapshot date.  
**What it does not support:** a causal explanation of the monthly gold-price fall, proof of central-bank buying in October, or a forecast. Those claims would require price decomposition, more complete flow data, and explicit tests against alternatives such as yields, FX, profit-taking, liquidity and event repricing.

## Deeper layers: where the useful detail is hiding

### Reconstruct what the market knew at the time

For macro releases, use the issuing agency's release and archived table as the primary record, then use FRED/ALFRED for convenient retrieval of many historical vintages. ALFRED can recover how many economic series looked on an earlier date, but it does not provide a historical market-consensus snapshot. The event record should preserve the release timestamp, first actual, previously published value, revision, and a separately sourced consensus snapshot. Do not substitute today's revised history for the first-print value. [FRED/ALFRED API real-time periods](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/fred/realtime_period.html), [FRED API observations](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/fred/series_observations.html)

Consensus and estimate histories are a specific information bottleneck. Commercial estimate products may offer point-in-time consensus, but compare contributor count, timestamp, horizon, fiscal-period mapping, survivorship, and redistribution rights before relying on them. If no valid as-of archive exists for the event date, label that input unavailable; do not reconstruct it from later reports or a current consensus page.

### Stress and leverage that headline rates miss

|---|---|---|---|
| Is global dollar credit expanding outside the US? | BIS [GLI E2.1](https://data.bis.org/topics/GLI/tables-and-dashboards), LBS [A6.1/A6.2](https://data.bis.org/topics/LBS/tables-and-dashboards), CBS [B2/B3/B4](https://data.bis.org/topics/CBS/tables-and-dashboards), and [bulk/API access](https://stats.bis.org/api-doc/v2/) | GLI tracks nonbank USD/EUR/JPY credit outside currency areas; LBS adds borrower country, sector, currency and booking-office location; CBS adds consolidated parent nationality and risk-transfer views. LBS includes intragroup positions and is unconsolidated; CBS excludes them. FX swaps create off-balance-sheet dollar obligations not represented in funded debt stocks; country allocation is unavailable. Never sum the three or treat the swap estimates as current country data. |
| Are debt-service burdens becoming dangerous? | [BIS Debt Service Ratios](https://data.bis.org/topics/DSR) and [credit-to-GDP gaps](https://data.bis.org/topics/CREDIT_GAPS) | Cross-country private-sector debt-service burden and credit-cycle warning measures. | Estimated aggregate measures with slow cadence; useful for vulnerability regimes, not short-term entry timing. |
| Is hedge-fund leverage or repo exposure building? | [OFR Hedge Fund Monitor](https://www.financialresearch.gov/hedge-fund-monitor/) and its [API](https://www.financialresearch.gov/hedge-fund-monitor/api/) | Aggregated measures covering leverage, counterparties, liquidity, complexity, risk management and repo activity. | Aggregated, delayed, and drawn from regulatory/private sources; it does not identify each fund's live book. |
| Are banks tightening credit before defaults rise? | [Federal Reserve SLOOS](https://www.federalreserve.gov/data/sloos.htm), H.8, and bank earnings filings | Surveyed lending standards, bank balance-sheet aggregates, and institution-specific reported commentary. | SLOOS is a survey; H.8 is aggregate; company commentary is selective. Confirm with loan pricing, charge-offs, and credit spreads. |
| Is market plumbing under strain? | [New York Fed markets data hub](https://www.newyorkfed.org/markets/data-hub), SOFR, repo operations and primary-dealer statistics; [OFR short-term funding monitor](https://www.financialresearch.gov/short-term-funding-monitor/) | Repo rates/volumes, operation take-up, dealer positioning aggregates and funding stress. | Some series are lagged or aggregate; distinguish quarter-end effects, policy operations and revisions from persistent stress. |

The practical signal is usually a **combination**: for example, widening cross-currency basis plus higher secured funding rates, weakening Treasury market depth, and tighter bank lending standards. One noisy stress index or one high reserve number is not proof of a funding event.

### Market plumbing and expectations that are public but easy to miss

- **Treasury dealer absorption:** Weekly [Primary Dealer Statistics](https://www.newyorkfed.org/markets/counterparties/primary-dealers-statistics) publish FR 2004A positions, 2004B cumulative-week transactions, 2004C Wednesday financing and cumulative-week fails, and SI specific issues. Thursday releases cover the previous week, at least eight days lagged. FR 2004A uses fair value/trade date (with a buyback exception); SI uses original par/settlement date. Cumulative fails sum daily outstanding fails, including weekends/holidays, and repeat persistent failures; they are not unique trades or a point-in-time stock. Compare with issuance, repo rates and issue evidence while preserving differing accounting bases, historical definitions, suppression and dealer membership. [Official forms/instructions](https://www.federalreserve.gov/apps/reportingforms/Report/Index/FR_2004), [API](https://markets.newyorkfed.org/static/docs/markets-api.html).
- **Daily sovereign cash and funding:** Treasury's [Daily Treasury Statement](https://fiscal.treasury.gov/accounting/daily-treasury-statement) and operating-cash [API](https://api.fiscaldata.treasury.gov/services/api/fiscal_service/v1/accounting/dts/deposits_withdrawals_operating_cash) expose TGA/cash, receipts, withdrawals and Table II tax categories. Withheld Individual/FICA, nonwithheld Individual/SECA (electronic and other separately), and corporate deposits are rough income/payroll cash pulses, not direct employment, wages or profits. The statement uses modified-cash recognition, arrives next business day by about 4 p.m., and is affected by deposit schedules, weekends/holidays, tax refunds and reclassification; align multi-day windows, report refunds separately and archive each statement. The Monthly Treasury Statement provides receipts/outlays/deficit; distinguish seasonal tax flows, debt settlement and Fed balance-sheet factors, because TGA movement is not a one-for-one reserve change or NIPA fiscal impulse. TreasuryDirect [buyback results](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/auctions/announcements-data-results/buy-backs/) distinguish liquidity support from cash management.
- **Global supply comparison:** UK DMO [gilt and money-market calendars](https://www.dmo.gov.uk/calendars/) expose auction, redemption and cash-management operations. There is no easily comparable daily TGA-style cash series for every sovereign; mark ECB/country balance-sheet proxies as proxies rather than daily fiscal cash.
- **Treasury cash-market activity:** FINRA's [Treasury TRACE daily/monthly aggregates](https://developer.finra.org/news/new-trace-treasury-daily-and-monthly-aggregates-dataset) provide a cash-volume denominator that futures turnover omits. Volume is not depth, executable liquidity, quote spread, direction or beneficial owner; Treasury trade-level details are not publicly disseminated.
- **The official-custody clue:** weekly Fed H.4.1 custody holdings for foreign and international accounts are available sooner than monthly TIC. Use the Wednesday-level [current FRED series](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/WMTSECL1) as a high-frequency *stock* clue, then reconcile against monthly TIC, Treasury auctions and market yields. The legacy series was discontinued/replaced, so verify the current identifier and definition before automating. It can move without a matching net-purchase flow, does not identify the beneficial owner, and must not be called “foreign buying” on its own. [H.4.1 release](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h41/), [TIC](https://home.treasury.gov/data/treasury-international-capital-tic-system)
- **A public data boundary in repo:** OFR's public daily repo release covers selected centrally cleared and tri-party segments. The separate non-centrally cleared bilateral repo collection is daily transaction-level reporting by covered firms for official-sector monitoring; it is not a public tape. Bilateral repo counterparty exposures therefore remain unobservable in ordinary public research even though regulators now collect more granular data. [OFR bilateral repo collection](https://www.financialresearch.gov/data/collections/non-centrally-cleared-bilateral-repo-data/), [public repo data and caveats](https://www.financialresearch.gov/short-term-funding-monitor/datasets/repo/)
- **Monthly collateral structure:** New York Fed Tri-Party/GCF data reveal collateral mix, haircut distributions and dealer concentration, but only for a representative day each month and only for covered repo segments. Use them to identify structural concentration or margin shifts, not to timestamp a daily funding shock. Track the November 2025 methodology break. [New York Fed dataset and release notes](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/tri-party-repo)
- **Newly visible private-credit and hedge-fund system totals:** September 2026 Z.1 added private debt funds (S124.8), BDCs (S124.5), interval/tender funds (S124.6), hedge funds (S124.7), private-credit loans (F4.4) and private debt fund shares (F522.3), with history back to 2012 Q4. F4.4.s shows $1.5399tn of private-credit loans at 2026 Q2. At 2025 Q4 the methods note reports $1.857tn of U.S.-domiciled vehicle assets and $1.083tn of their loans to U.S. businesses; domestic and foreign vehicles together held $1.458tn of such loans. These are distinct dates and perimeters. Estimates rely on confidential Form PF, classifications and imputation and can include syndicated loans. Quarterly D3.t/F4.4.t flows are SAAR; printed M3.t is an annual 2025 sector-by-instrument matrix, not current-quarter or bilateral funding. Preserve the June table mapping; use documented private-credit/EFA detail for counterpart estimates. [Z.1 release](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/z1/20260911/z1.pdf), [methods, Table 1](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/introducing-private-credit-into-the-financial-accounts-of-the-united-states-20261016.html), [DDP transition](https://www.federalreserve.gov/data/data-download-fred-information.htm)
- **Bank filings and private-credit portfolio evidence:** FFIEC Call Reports/UBPR provide quarterly bank asset-quality and funding measures; retain form and amendments because required fields differ and histories refresh. FDIC's [Quarterly Banking Profile](https://www.fdic.gov/quarterly-banking-profile) is a rapid aggregate cross-check, not a replacement for filings; NCUA's [5300 credit-union call reports](https://ncua.gov/analysis/credit-union-corporate-call-report-data/quarterly-data) add separate commercial-loan, delinquency, loss and investment exposures. SEC monthly [BDC XBRL/Schedule of Investments](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/bdc-data-sets) files expose selected private-credit holdings and marks, but have coverage gaps, extraction refreshes and custom-tag blind spots. For covered registered ABS, [Form ABS-EE asset files](https://www.sec.gov/files/formabs-ee.pdf) can expose loan-level pool performance in Schedule AL XML; coverage is asset-class and registration-specific, and large files/schema changes require validation. Deal-level [CLO Form 10-D filings](https://www.sec.gov/files/form10d.pdf) are distribution-centered; detailed trustee reports may be investor-gated. These are fragmented windows, not a complete loan mark, covenant or private-fund leverage database. [FFIEC availability notes](https://cdr.ffiec.gov/Public/HelpFiles/WelcomeAdditionalInfo.htm), [SEC BDC README](https://www.sec.gov/file/business-development-company-bdc-data-set-readme), [SEC Regulation AB II](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2014/33-9638.pdf)
- **Insurance balance sheets and private-credit allocation:** NAIC annual/quarterly statutory statements and investment schedules can show holdings in bonds, mortgage loans, real estate and other long-term assets, but granular historical schedule products may be paid or access-controlled and statutory carrying values are not GAAP market values. NAIC-adopted electronic reporting instructions add a public/Rule 144/private-placement classification field effective 31 December 2026; treat it as a new measurement field, not retroactive evidence about earlier holdings. The NAIC archive lists its [year-end-2025 private-credit analysis](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/capital-markets-special-reports-private-credit-analysis.pdf) on 23 September 2026; a news release announced it on 5 November. The report adds a public, dated aggregate: $1.21tn under its broad definition, including $544bn in privately rated bonds, BDCs and private-credit funds. It is not an insurer-level holdings tape; definitions vary, mortgages are excluded, and certain PIK/non-accrual indicators come from outside sources. The report is © NAIC, all rights reserved; cite/link rather than republishing. Enhanced 2026 disclosure classifications first enter year-end 2026 annual statements and may change future estimates. [NAIC Financial Data Repository](https://content.naic.org/cipr_topics/topic_financial_data_repository.htm), [NAIC data products](https://content.naic.org/prod_serv_redistributors.htm), [adopted changes](https://content.naic.org/committees/e/statutory-accounting-principles-wg/adoptions), [NAIC summary](https://content.naic.org/resource-center/private-credit-data-driven-regulators)
- **Credit derivatives activity versus prices:** DTCC's [public Trade Information Warehouse tables](https://www.dtcc.com/market-index-data) provide quarterly anonymized activity for top CDS names and indices; broader CDS Kinetics products are commercial. Use public tables for activity/liquidity shifts, not spreads, direction or net protection. CFTC's delayed [weekly swap summary](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/SwapsReports/index.htm) covers IRS/CDS/FX, not commodity swaps; its periodic [Entity-Netted Notionals](https://www.cftc.gov/About/EconomicAnalysis/ReportsOCE/index.htm) have bilateral-pair netting limits. Monthly [cleared-margin reports](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/ClearedMarginReports/index.htm) show aggregate collateral demand without institution-level allocation.
- **Money funds as a short-term funding bridge:** SEC [N-MFP monthly holdings](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/dera-form-n-mfp-data-sets) reveal portfolio allocation among Treasury bills, repo, CDs and commercial paper; filings are public upon submission, while the monthly SEC flat-file package has a processing cutoff. Required daily fund-website disclosures of liquidity, NAV and shareholder flows are more timely but decentralized. Capture fund, date and as-of field consistently; monthly holdings are month-end stocks, and daily liquidity/flows do not reveal all institutional cash demand or private repo exposures. [SEC rule on website disclosures](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2023/33-11211.pdf)
- **Cross-border portfolio positions:** IMF [PIP](https://data.imf.org/en/Datasets/PIP) reports bilateral portfolio asset positions with sector/instrument detail, but is a voluntary semiannual stock survey affected by prices, FX and coverage changes. Use it to map slower cross-border holdings relationships, not as transaction flow or beneficial ownership. The Fed's [Distributional Financial Accounts](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/z1/dataviz/dfa/) add household wealth distribution by percentile and demographics, but interpolate/extrapolate the triennial SCF between survey waves and can be revised. These are structural context, not daily risk appetite signals.
- **Mortgage credit formation versus subsequent performance:** FHFA/CFPB's [National Mortgage Database aggregate statistics](https://www.fhfa.gov/data/nmdb) separate monthly/quarterly origination cohorts (rate, payment, DTI, score, LTV/CLTV and market type) from quarterly active-loan delinquency, foreclosure and forbearance outcomes. It is a five-percent sample of closed-end first liens, with thinner coverage of investor and manufactured-home loans; recent performance quarters can be revised for up to six months. Compare markets and cohorts consistently, keep loan-count and dollar/UPB weights distinct where available, and remember that NMDB performance rates are count-weighted only. Freddie Mac's [Single-Family Loan-Level Dataset](https://www.freddiemac.com/research/datasets/sf-loanlevel-dataset) adds loan-level performance and realized loss/recovery history for its purchased/guaranteed mortgages, with quarterly Standard updates and access conditions. Treat Release 30's Non-Standard data as frozen, version the July 2026 Release 47 schema/sign change (recoveries/gains negative; expenses/losses positive), and align the one-month default-reporting lag and 90-day post-zero-balance loss lag. Pair with HMDA for application/origination decision context; HMDA is not a servicing-performance tape.
- **Government-insured mortgage credit after origination:** Ginnie Mae registered [Disclosure Data Download](https://www.ginniemae.gov/disclosure/disclosure-resources/disclosure-data-download-layouts-and-sample-files) files add FHA, VA, Rural Development and Native American-insured collateral performance to conventional Freddie Mac data. Monthly single-family portfolio loan-level files start in December 2013; consolidated quarterly Loan Performance starts in 2023 Q2, with delinquency history, forbearance and liquidation fields. The September 2026 quarter file was posted 19 October 2026; the schedule targets the 12th business day after quarter-end. Coverage is only loans securitized in GNMA MBS, not the full agency-servicing universe. Active-panel exits, unverified issuer submissions, restricted-use terms and the August 2026 schema v2 change require explicit cohort and vintage controls. [Loan Performance layout](https://www.ginniemae.gov/doing_business_with_ginniemae/investor_resources/mbs_disclosure_data/Lists/LayoutsAndSamples/Attachments/471/LoanPerf_layout.pdf), [monthly v2 schema](https://www.ginniemae.gov/investors/disclosures_and_reports/Lists/Bulletins/Attachments/784/MBS_Loan_Level_Disclosure_Layout_V2.0_wReperforming_August2026.pdf), [release schedule](https://www.ginniemae.gov/disclosure/disclosure-resources/data-release-schedule), [production portal](https://bulk.ginniemae.gov/)
- **Multifamily mortgage maturity and performance:** Fannie Mae MFLPD provides monthly loan records and annual DSCR history for nearly 74,000 Fannie Mae multifamily loans acquired from 2000 through 30 June 2026, covering more than 90% of Fannie acquisitions in that period. It adds maturity, payment status, LTV/underwritten DSCR, collateral, property-location, credit-event and loss fields; use the quarterly refresh calendar and archive each download. The 8 Oct 2026 Q2 refresh is the latest checked. The panel is Fannie-specific, not all commercial real estate; annual DSCR before 2007 and modification data before 2010 are unavailable, some fields are blank or deal-level, and registration terms restrict redistribution/external commercial use without written consent. [MFLPD overview](https://capitalmarkets.fanniemae.com/credit-risk-transfer/multifamily-loan-performance-data), [Q2 2026 update](https://capitalmarkets.fanniemae.com/credit-risk-transfer/multifamily-credit-risk-transfer/updated-multifamily-loan-performance-data-and-dus-prepayment-history-now-available-data-dynamics-q22026), [refresh calendar](https://capitalmarkets.fanniemae.com/resources/file/credit-risk/pdf/data-dynamics-data-refresh-calendar.pdf), [glossary](https://capitalmarkets.fanniemae.com/resources/file/credit-risk/pdf/mflpd-glossary-file-layout.pdf)
- **Consumer credit access before delinquency:** CFPB's [Consumer Credit Trends](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/consumer-credit-trends/) supplies monthly originations by risk band, age and neighborhood income for auto, credit-card, mortgage and student-loan markets. Its auto-loan [credit-tightness index](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/consumer-credit-trends/auto-loans/inquiry-activity/) holds applicant scores constant to help distinguish lender-policy changes from shifts in the applicant pool. The last six months are provisional and the panel is drawn from one bureau; pair access/origination data with the New York Fed's [quarterly delinquency transitions](https://www.newyorkfed.org/microeconomics/databank). Do not treat fewer originations as default evidence, or assume the two panels track the same borrowers.
- **China housing demand versus property-linked local revenue:** NBS monthly [development/sales releases](https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfbhjd/202610/t20261019_1965615.html) give new-home contracts, urban second-hand online-signed transaction area, construction starts/completions, completed unsold stock and developer funding receipts; the separate [70-city price series](https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfbhjd/202610/t20261019_1965609.html) covers city-level new and existing home prices using different source methods. Pair these with MOF land-transfer revenue only as a separate local-government income channel. NBS flow totals are cumulative YTD and revised comparable growth rates; completed unsold inventory excludes uncompleted presale stock, and 2026 price indices use a new comparison base and weights. Preserve monthly release vintage and derive month-only flows by differencing matching cumulative reports. See [China housing/developer funding](china-housing-developer-funding-2026-09.md) and [China local fiscal pulse](china-local-fiscal-pulse-2026-09.md).
- **Fed holder maps and monthly money-fund composition:** the Fed's [Enhanced Financial Accounts](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/efa/enhanced-financial-accounts.htm) include sector-level issuer-to-holder data and a [monthly MMF holdings detail](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/efa/efa-project-money-market-funds-investment-holdings-detail.htm) broken out by issuer country, fund type, instrument and maturity. The holder map uses observed links where available and proportionally allocates some residuals, so it is not a direct transaction/counterparty tape. The MMF detail has its own coverage adjustments and totals may differ slightly from headline Z.1. Keep the table's documentation and vintage with the download.
- **CCP resilience disclosures:** [CPMI-IOSCO public quantitative disclosures](https://www.bis.org/cpmi/publ/d125.pdf) provide quarterly clearinghouse-level detail on margin, collateral, default resources, liquidity and stress coverage, usually with a one-to-two-month lag. They can compare clearing services' structural risk controls; data are heterogeneous and exclude participant identities/positions, so they cannot identify a member's exposure or a live margin call.
- **Payroll and inflation revisions:** BLS keeps a [CES employment vintage history](https://www.bls.gov/web/empsit/cesvininfo.htm); annual benchmarks later reanchor payroll estimates. CPI seasonal factors are recalculated annually and can revise years of seasonally adjusted history, while NSA values avoid those routine seasonal-factor revisions. Keep first-release employment/CPI values separate from current histories. [CES FAQ](https://www.bls.gov/web/empsit/cesfaq.htm), [CPI seasonal-adjustment guidance](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/seasonal-adjustment/)
- **Non-US real-time macro history:** the ECB [Area-Wide Real-Time Database](https://data.ecb.europa.eu/data/datasets/RTD/data-information) preserves vintages, with dates tied to Governing Council schedules and an ESA 2010/BPM6 comparability break from late 2014. The IMF [WEO archive](https://data.imf.org/datasets/WEO) preserves forecast vintages, but Jan/Jul updates are not the same full dataset as Apr/Oct editions. Evaluate against a declared outcome vintage.
- **GDP source-status evidence:** BEA's [GDP release page](https://www.bea.gov/data/gdp/gross-domestic-product) includes a GDP/GDI vintage workbook and Key Source Data and Assumptions tables for estimates. Those tables identify incomplete inputs and BEA assumptions in an advance estimate; they are not a real-time nowcast. BEA does not publish an advance current-quarter GDI or corporate-profits estimate, so do not treat later-release components as contemporaneous. [BEA release timing/coverage](https://www.bea.gov/news/gdp-release-additional-information)
- **Fed balance-sheet vintage integrity:** Z.1 revisions continue as source information changes, and current history is not a point-in-time archive; store dated release files and distinguish stocks from seasonally adjusted annual-rate flows and revaluations. H.8 is based partly on a voluntary reporting panel and adjusts subgroup histories after panel changes/mergers, so archive its releases for point-in-time studies. [Z.1 notes](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/z1/current/introductory_text.htm), [H.8 methodology](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/H8/about.htm)
- **Treasury cash and Fed bank-credit vintage traps:** the Daily Treasury Statement is modified cash basis and typically available by 4 p.m. next business day; the Monthly Treasury Statement is normally around the eighth workday next month. Do not align either with NIPA accrual measures as though concepts match. Fed H.8 history may be adjusted for changes in its voluntary reporting panel, so today's history is not always the originally observed panel vintage. [Treasury release timing](https://home.treasury.gov/data/receipts-outlays), [Fed H.8 methodology](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/H8/about.htm)
- **Bank derivatives footprint:** the OCC quarterly derivatives report compiles Call Report data into bank-industry notional, product, revenue and gross/net credit-exposure views, including a “commodity and other” bucket. This helps size concentration and system credit exposure, but the commodity bucket cannot identify gold risk; notional is a reference amount, not the amount at risk. It does not reveal dealer/customer direction or counterparties' offsetting books. Use it as a quarterly structural overlay to CFTC Bank Participation Reports and capped/delayed public SDR transactions. [OCC report index](https://www.occ.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/quarterly-report-on-bank-trading-and-derivatives-activities/index-quarterly-report-on-bank-trading-and-derivatives-activities.html), [current Q2 2026 issue](https://www.occ.treas.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/quarterly-report-on-bank-trading-and-derivatives-activities/files/q2-2026-derivatives-quarterly.html)
- **Repo segmentation beyond SOFR:** OFR's [repo dataset](https://www.financialresearch.gov/short-term-funding-monitor/datasets/repo/) reports daily activity across centrally cleared and tri-party segments, with different collateral/tenor slices and preliminary versus quarterly-final vintages. Contextualize its rates and volumes alongside SOFR/BGCR/TGCR and calendar turns; do not directly subtract or compare rates without a methodology bridge. OFR says its transaction sets differ and its rates are volume-weighted means, unlike the reference-rate medians. Missing series values can also reflect confidentiality protections. These are aggregates, not a public dealer-by-dealer transaction tape. [OFR methodology and release cadence](https://www.financialresearch.gov/short-term-funding-monitor/datasets/repo/)
- **FX settlement and OTC swap prices:** BIS's [2025 Triennial Survey](https://data.bis.org/topics/DER/tables-and-dashboards) includes FX instrument/currency/counterparty/location/maturity and settlement-risk context. For transaction pricing, CFTC [Part 43 SDR disclosures](https://www.cftc.gov/Forms/sdrforms.html) expose some OTC swap terms, but block caps/delays and fragmented schemas make them unsuitable for inferring counterparty direction. A complete public daily FX-swap-basis tape was not identified; use a named licensed source or mark the series unavailable.
- **Expectation distributions:** The Philadelphia Fed [SPF](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/surveys-and-data/real-time-data-research/survey-of-professional-forecasters) provides historical individual and probability forecasts; the [New York Fed SME](https://www.newyorkfed.org/markets/market-intelligence/survey-of-market-expectations) publishes underlying response data split between primary dealers and other market participants. These enrich disagreement and calibration analysis, but are intermittent surveys, not release-day consensus or market-implied odds; SME data generally arrive about three weeks after an FOMC meeting. Preserve release vintages, respondent counts, cohort and question wording.
- **Macro vintages beyond ALFRED:** The Philadelphia Fed [Real-Time Data Set for Macroeconomists](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/surveys-and-data/real-time-data-research/real-time-data-set-for-macroeconomists) and [full vintage history](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/surveys-and-data/real-time-data-research/real-time-data-set-full-time-series-history) support dated historical information sets and first/second/third-release checks for selected GDP/GDI series. Pair them with original archived BEA releases; definitions and vintage coverage differ by series.
- **Central-bank event docket:** Preserve immediate decisions, statements and press conferences separately from later minutes/accounts, votes, forecasts and transcripts. Use [Fed calendars/history](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm), [ECB decisions/accounts](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/govcdec/mopo/html/index.en.html), [BOJ minutes/Outlook](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/mpmsche_minu/), [BoE MPC records](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy), and [RBA decisions/minutes](https://www.rba.gov.au/monetary-policy/). Delayed minutes and archival transcripts cannot be used as inputs to a historical event-time forecast before their publication date.
- **Operations versus words:** For actual liquidity provision, track [ECB weekly balance sheets/APP holdings](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/policy_and_exchange_rates/eurosystem_balance_sheet/html/index.en.html), [BOJ market operations](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/measures/mkt_ope/ope_f/), [BoE market-operations results](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/markets/bank-of-england-market-operations-guide/results-and-usage-data), and [RBA statistical tables/operations](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/). Separate operation plans, actual awards, settlements, redemptions and balance-sheet stocks; they answer different questions and publication lags differ.
- **Gold options and implied risk:** [CME Gold CVOL](https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/metals/precious/gold.volume.options.html) and [QuikVol](https://www.cmegroup.com/tools-information/quikstrike/pricing-volatility-strategy-tools/quikvol-tool.html) expose a 30-day implied-volatility measure and futures-options term structure/skew. These are option-market prices, not realized risk, OTC positioning, or objective forecasts; history and detailed surfaces can require licenses.
- **Inflation expectations are not one series:** Pair the [Cleveland Fed model decomposition](https://www.clevelandfed.org/indicators-and-data/inflation-expectations), [NY Fed household expectations](https://www.newyorkfed.org/microeconomics/sce), [Cleveland Fed firm survey](https://www.clevelandfed.org/indicators-and-data/survey-of-firms-inflation-expectations), [Fed TIPS compensation estimates](https://www.federalreserve.gov/data/tips-yield-curve-and-inflation-compensation.htm), and licensed inflation swaps where available. Record horizon, population, timestamp and vintage separately. Breakevens include risk premia/liquidity; the Cleveland Fed series is a model; surveys are not trading consensus. A complete public USD inflation-swap curve was not identified.
- **Exchange liquidity and event slippage:** CME's [Liquidity Tool](https://www.cmegroup.com/tools-information/cme-liquidity-tool.html) provides historical spread/depth/cost-to-trade context. Paid [DataMine market depth/MBO](https://datamine.new.cmegroup.com/) allows event-time book reconstruction for covered Globex products. Visible exchange depth may cancel and excludes hidden and OTC liquidity; check product coverage, timestamp granularity and data rights.
- **Broker-specific demo quote history:** cTrader [Open API symbol data](https://help.ctrader.com/open-api/symbol-data/) can provide broker-specific historical ticks/bars and live spot/depth data where the broker makes them available. Capture symbol, broker, bid/ask, spread, timezone, quote gaps, and API history limits. This is not a universal spot-gold tape and should not route orders in a research workflow.

### Investor holdings: what filings show and what they hide

- **SEC Form 13F** gives a delayed, incomplete view of certain managers' reportable US equity holdings. It is not a full portfolio: short positions, many derivatives, cash, non-US securities and intraperiod trades are not a contemporaneous holdings tape. Treat quarter-end holdings as a delayed snapshot and check amendments.
- **SEC Form N-PORT** adds portfolio holdings beyond money-market funds, but it is not a monthly public holdings feed. Funds report each month of their fiscal quarter; currently only quarter-end-month filings are public, and quarter-end reports are generally due within about 60 days after fiscal quarter-end. First- and second-month holdings are not public, although some return/flow fields cover all three months. The dataset is updated quarterly and contains disseminated, as-filed reports; read its README because several fields remain nonpublic and SEC extraction or filing errors and amendments occur. The 2025 order delays broader 2024 amendments to 17 November 2027 for fund groups with at least $1 billion and 18 May 2028 for smaller groups. A separate February 2026 proposal remains a proposal; do not treat more frequent public disclosure as effective. [SEC N-PORT dataset and README](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/form-n-port-data-sets), [SEC reporting extension](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2025/04/s7-26-22), [SEC 2026 proposal](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2026/02/s7-2026-05), [2024 rule and public-field limits](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2024/ic-35308.pdf).
- **DOL Form 5500** adds annual financial and actuarial data for private-sector ERISA employee-benefit plans. The latest listed vintage at the 8 November 2026 check was form-year 2025; 2009+ files are grouped by form-year, typically refresh near the first of each month, and distinguish `Latest` (newest received record per plan) from `All` (all filings/amendments). Filter pension plans away from welfare plans when studying retirement assets. Structured Schedule H/I totals and Schedule MB/SB actuarial fields are useful for slow funded-status and allocation context, but the Schedule H line 4i asset list is an attachment to the original EFAST2 filing, not part of the structured CSV dataset. It can be irregularly formatted and is not a clean security-level tape. Annual filing is due seven months after plan-year end with a possible 2½-month extension; public filing times are staggered and attachments can take up to 21 days. Governmental plans generally fall outside ERISA, Form 5500-EZ is not published online, and small/foreign/sensitive filing exceptions apply. [DOL dataset and data dictionary](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/public-disclosure/foia/form-5500-datasets), [dataset guide](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/EBSA/about-ebsa/our-activities/public-disclosure/foia/form-5500-dataset-guide.pdf), [EFAST2 public-access FAQ](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/faqs/efast2-form-5500-processing), [2025 filing instructions](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/EBSA/employers-and-advisers/plan-administration-and-compliance/reporting-and-filing/form-5500/2025-instructions.pdf).
- **OFR Hedge Fund Monitor and SEC Private Fund Statistics** give complementary public aggregate windows into private-fund leverage and funding. OFR draws on Form PF and other supervisory/market sources; the SEC report analyzes Form PF/ADV submissions and publishes fund counts, assets and selected strategy, borrowing, derivatives, leverage and liquidity measures. As of 8 Nov 2026, the latest SEC report was for 2025 Q4 filings through 3 July, typically six or more months lagged; large hedge/liquidity advisers file quarterly, while many advisers and private-equity funds file annually. Form PF is confidential, and SEC aggregates can be rounded, anonymized or masked and revised after amendments. This is system context, not current named-fund positioning or a live margin-call feed. Preserve report vintage and cutoff; don't assume the 2024 PF amendments, whose compliance date is July 2027, are already reflected. [OFR monitor](https://www.financialresearch.gov/hedge-fund-monitor/), [OFR data/API](https://www.financialresearch.gov/hedge-fund-monitor/api/), [SEC Private Fund Statistics](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/statistics-data-visualizations/private-fund-statistics), [SEC Q4 2025 report](https://www.sec.gov/files/investment/private-fund-statistics-2025-q4.pdf)
- **CFTC COT** shows category-level reportable futures exposure. As checked 7 November 2026, the live schedule remains Tuesday-position/Friday-publication, while the Commission has announced a plan to move to Monday and Wednesday positions published Wednesday and Friday by year-end. Treat the plan as a future schedule until the CFTC updates its operational calendar. CFTC also notes the disaggregated classifications were applied retrospectively using current classifications because historical classifications are not available; treat older category labels cautiously. [CFTC live report schedule](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/ReleaseSchedule/index.htm), [semiweekly plan](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9316-26), [COT reports and notes](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/index.htm)

These sources answer different questions. 13F/N-PORT describe delayed holdings, COT describes classified futures positions, Z.1 provides revised sector balance sheets, and OFR describes aggregated system exposures. None reveals the live, complete net risk of a manager or dealer.

### Company fundamentals beyond headline ratios

Start at [SEC EDGAR company submissions and XBRL facts](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces), then open the original filing for accounting policy and footnotes. For a public-company event file, collect 10-K/10-Q, 8-K, earnings release, earnings-call transcript (where licensed), Form 4 insider transactions, 13D/13G ownership changes, and debt or credit-agreement exhibits. Normalize these separately:

- **Earnings quality:** cash conversion, working capital, capitalized costs, stock compensation, restructuring, segment mix, and non-GAAP reconciliation.
- **Balance-sheet path:** maturity schedule, floating-rate share, covenants, collateral, pension deficits, lease liabilities, and refinancing windows.
- **Demand evidence:** backlog and cancellations, customer concentration, order/price/mix commentary, inventory, receivables, and supplier finance.
- **Ownership and incentives:** insider transactions and beneficial-ownership filings, while distinguishing planned sales from discretionary purchases and filing dates from transaction dates.

For debt-market confirmation, [FINRA TRACE](https://www.finra.org/finra-data/fixed-income) and issuer filings can reveal prints, spreads and terms, but complete intraday depth and some institutional datasets are licensed. Do not equate a handful of prints with executable liquidity. For private credit, loan-level terms, marks, amendments and lender identity are often private; public fund reports and company filings are partial proxies.

### Alternative data: useful, licensed, and easy to misuse

| Data family | Legitimate route | Useful question | Key bias to test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Card/transaction aggregates | Licensed vendor with documented collection, consent, coverage and revision policy | Is consumer spending changing before official retail data? | Panel drift, merchant coverage, refunds, inflation and representativeness. |
| Web/app traffic and search | Licensed or explicitly public aggregate products | Is product interest or customer engagement changing? | Bots, privacy changes, algorithm changes, panel churn and intent not converting to revenue. |
| Shipping/AIS, port and customs | Official customs/port data or licensed vessel/manifest feeds | Are commodity flows, vessel arrivals or inventories shifting? | Transshipment, false/missing AIS, classification error, timing and route substitutions. |
| Satellite imagery | Licensed imagery/analytics or public government imagery where permitted | Are facilities, parking lots, mines, storage or crop conditions changing? | Cloud/coverage, model error, geolocation, changing baselines and proxy-to-financial-outcome gaps. |
| Job postings / employee signals | Public company career pages or a properly licensed aggregator | Are firms expanding, freezing, or changing skill mix? | Duplicate/stale listings, outsourcing, location coverage and hiring intent not equaling realized payrolls. |

Before paying for an alternative dataset, ask for a sample, historical coverage, revision/backfill policy, unit economics, sector/region coverage, point-in-time availability, survivorship handling, and written permitted-use terms. Test it against an official series and a period not used to build the thesis. Proprietary does not mean validated.

### Hard to find versus not publicly knowable

**Hard to find but public:** release archives, agency microdata documentation, exchange warehouse and delivery files, fund filings, auction results, reserve tables, and central-bank speeches/minutes. These can take careful retrieval and timestamping, but are auditable.

**Public, but lagged or aggregated:** COT, 13F, N-PORT, TIC, BIS cross-border credit, OFR hedge-fund aggregates, and central-bank reserve reporting. They help reconstruct context, not live positions.

**Generally licensed or proprietary:** full historical consensus snapshots, exchange tick/order-book replay, complete options surfaces, commercial estimates, some benchmark history, premium alternative data, and consolidated multi-venue depth. Check license terms for automated use, retention, display, and redistribution.

**Not available as a complete public feed:** dealer-by-dealer OTC inventory, all client flows and beneficial owners, a complete live global options book, private credit marks and covenants across lenders, and confidential regulatory filings at institution level. There is also no comprehensive free global refinery-throughput or unified cargo/AIS feed; build a documented patchwork from country balances, operator filings, customs and licensed shipping data, and preserve coverage gaps. Treat precise claims inferred from public proxies as overclaiming.

### Research question to data map

| Research question | First-pass stack | Confirmation / falsification |
|---|---|---|
| Is inflation accelerating or easing? | BLS CPI/PPI, BEA PCE, release vintages in ALFRED | Wage growth, rents, import prices, breadth/diffusion, revisions and market breakevens; label market-implied inflation as risk premia plus expectations. |
| Is growth weakening? | Payrolls, claims, retail sales, industrial production, GDP/PCE | SLOOS, credit spreads, company guidance, freight/energy, revisions and real-time nowcast path. |
| Is monetary policy repricing? | Central-bank statement/minutes/speeches, OIS/Fed funds futures, Treasury curve | Inflation/growth surprise history, term premium, real yields, USD, and futures positioning. Market probabilities are pricing transformations, not objective odds. |
| Is sovereign duration supply a problem? | Treasury auction calendar/results, issuance/refunding, yields and real yields | Bid-to-cover and tail against when-issued, dealer/indirect allocation, repo specialness and market depth. No single auction statistic identifies the final beneficial buyer. |
| Is credit stress broadening? | TRACE/credit indices, CDS where licensed, bank earnings and SLOOS | Defaults, downgrades, refinancing wall, loan fund flows, OFR short-term funding, and BIS debt-service/credit-cycle indicators. |
| Is a gold move macro, positioning, or physical? | Matched gold benchmark/futures, real yields, USD, CFTC COT | ETF tonnes, COMEX stocks/deliveries, LBMA clearing/vault data, SGE premium/turnover, central-bank disclosures and options skew where licensed. Keep instruments and time zones aligned. |
| Is an equity move fundamental? | Earnings release/filing, estimate revisions, guidance and cash flow | Segment/customer evidence, inventory/receivables, insider filings, bond spreads, short interest, and peers' same-period disclosures. |

For each thesis, write the observable that would **disprove** it before reading the next data point. This reduces confirmation bias and makes the research auditable.
