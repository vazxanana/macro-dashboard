## Reported content
> "Little price discovery occurs at the closing auction, suggesting that closing prices may not be significantly more informative than intraday trading." (paraphrased from search summary, described as using "variance ratio and weighted price contribution tests")
> "The closing price often substantially deviates from the closing quote midpoint measured at 4pm, with average (absolute) deviations of 8.1 basis points, and for 1% of stock-days the closing price deviates by more than 0.63%. These auction price deviations revert quickly and almost completely on average, suggesting they reflect temporary price pressure rather than new information." (paraphrased from search summary)

## Relevance
This is the single most important counter-evidence source for central question 3: a peer-reviewed study finds the closing auction itself contributes little new price discovery relative to continuous trading, and that closing-price deviations from the pre-close quote are largely transient liquidity/order-imbalance effects that revert — not durable new information. This directly complicates any claim that "the close = fair value" and should be weighed against the benchmarking/MOC-imbalance narrative in other sources.
