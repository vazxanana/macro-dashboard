## Reported content
> "Dealer hedging is mechanical, not discretionary—when a market maker sells you an option, they hedge the directional risk by trading the underlying, and as the price moves, that hedge must be adjusted... Dealers typically buy on dips and sell on rallies, which dampens volatility and can keep price pinned near certain strikes: a phenomenon often described as 'gamma gravity' or 'pinning'... Large GEX concentrated at a single strike (like a round number) creates a magnetic effect." (paraphrased/aggregated from multiple vendor sources)

## Relevance
Confirms the mechanism is mechanically plausible and widely described in industry literature: positive dealer gamma → two-sided hedging → vol suppression/pinning; negative dealer gamma → one-sided hedging → vol amplification. However all sources found are vendor/blog content selling GEX dashboards (SpotGamma-style products), not independent academic verification of the magnitude of the effect. Treat mechanism as directionally real but evidence quality as low (vendor-asserted, not peer-reviewed).
