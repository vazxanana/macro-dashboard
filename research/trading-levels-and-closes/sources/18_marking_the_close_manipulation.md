## Relevance
This is the OPPOSITION angle for central question 3: the very fact that closing prices are valuable enough to be worth manipulating ("marking the close") is a double-edged finding — it confirms the close matters enough for real money to be gamed around it (supporting its importance), but simultaneously shows that observed closing prices can be artifacts of manipulation/gaming rather than pure "fair value" signals. Regulatory design (call auctions) exists specifically because continuous-market closes were historically exploitable.
