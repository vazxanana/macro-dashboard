## Relevance
Unlike round numbers (Osler, order-book evidence) or published S/R levels (Osler, bootstrap-tested), the "previous close as anchor" claim rests almost entirely on **general anchoring-bias behavioral economics** (a well-established cognitive bias in the broader literature, e.g., Tversky/Kahneman-style research on reference points) applied by inference to trading, not on any trading-specific empirical study found in this search. The "70%/85%" statistics quoted by these blogs have no visible source or methodology and should be treated as unverified marketing copy, not data. This level type should be graded as **weaker evidence than PDH/PDL and much weaker than round numbers** — it is plausible by extension of anchoring theory but not directly demonstrated with market microstructure data in anything surfaced here. Notably, previous close matters mechanically in some markets via specific instruments (e.g., stock exchange circuit breakers, futures settlement calculations often reference prior close) — but that is a different (structural/mechanical) argument than a "reaction level" argument.
