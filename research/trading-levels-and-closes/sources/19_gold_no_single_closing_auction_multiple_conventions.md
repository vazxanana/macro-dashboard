## Relevance
This is the decisive source for central question 4. Spot/OTC gold has AT LEAST THREE distinct, non-equivalent "closes," none of which resembles an equity closing auction that concentrates a large fraction of daily volume into one price-setting mechanism:
1. **LBMA Gold Price auctions** (10:30 & 15:00 London) — a real bank-to-bank auction mechanism, but only twice daily and used mainly for wholesale physical/OTC contract settlement, not for retail charting.
2. **COMEX futures settlement** (13:29-13:30 ET, VWAP-based — see file 17) — a genuine mechanically-defined settlement price, but distinct in timing/purpose from both the LBMA auction and the "daily bar close."
3. **The 5pm ET "daily candle close"** used by MT4/most retail platforms and charting software — explicitly described (by OANDA/forex.com support docs) as an "administrative reset point for rollover purposes," with NO trading halt, NO auction, and NO order-flow concentration mechanism. Trading continues seamlessly through it.
This strongly suggests that whatever significance equity closing-auction research assigns to "the close" (mechanical order concentration, NAV striking, tracking-error minimization, marking-the-close manipulation) does NOT mechanically transfer to the conventional "gold daily close" that most retail chart-based technical analysis (including support/resistance or candle-close rules on daily gold charts) implicitly relies on. The 5pm ET bar-close is an arbitrary data-vendor/broker convention for splitting a continuous 23-hour market into days, not a price-discovery event or liquidity event. Only the twice-daily LBMA auction and the COMEX 1:30pm ET settlement carry anything resembling the institutional weight of an equity close, and even those are a fraction of the market (LBMA-referenced OTC volume and COMEX futures volume, not the full 24h spot/interbank/algo flow).
