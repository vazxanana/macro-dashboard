## Relevance
This is the theoretical resolution to the "is it just self-fulfilling, and does that matter" question posed in the task. The honest synthesis across sources: **the self-fulfilling/reflexive mechanism is real and is not a disqualifying objection to tradeability** — Osler's own round-number and order-clustering findings ARE a self-fulfilling mechanism (orders cluster at round numbers *because* traders choose to place them there for psychological/convenience reasons, and price then behaves differently there *because* those real orders exist and execute). The key qualifier surfaced consistently across sources is that reflexive effects require the level to be **widely known/watched** (round numbers, prior-day levels, VWAP, published bank S/R levels all qualify structurally) — obscure or idiosyncratic indicator levels would not generate the same effect since too few participants are positioned around them. This makes reflexivity a *necessary condition* for a level type to matter at all, not evidence against tradeability — but it also implies **fragility**: if participation shifts (e.g., algos increasingly trade against known retail clusters, or a level becomes so crowded that it gets front-run before being reached), the effect can decay or invert. This is consistent with why Osler found modest (not overwhelming) effect sizes (4-8 percentage points) rather than deterministic reactions.
