## Reported content
> "In the major European markets (FTSE, DAX, and CAC), the share of trading volume executed in the closing auction accounted for 40% to 55% of daily trading volume in 2023. Notably, FTSE stocks showed a dramatic rise with closing auction share increasing from 31% at the beginning of 2019 to 55% at the end of 2023." (paraphrased from search summary)
> "The rapidly rising share of trading taking place during closing auctions – driven by passive flows – is leading to higher transaction costs and lower liquidity for investors, as well as impaired price discovery and reduced market efficiency." (paraphrased from search summary)
> "The mostly liquidity-driven trading interest in the closing auction might lead to distortions of the closing price and deviations from fundamental value due to short-term supply and demand imbalances caused by one-sided order flow from index tracking funds." (paraphrased from search summary)

## Relevance
Strong data point for central question 1 in a European context — closing-auction share is even higher than in the US (40-55% vs ~9-10%), again tied to passive/index-fund growth. Also directly complicates central question 3: an institutional/asset-manager voice explicitly argues rising closing-auction dominance IMPAIRS price discovery and can push the close away from fundamental value due to mechanical index-flow imbalances, not information. This is an important caveat against over-interpreting "close = fair value."
