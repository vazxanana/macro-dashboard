## Reported content
> "Passive managers, aiming to minimize tracking error, concentrate trades at the close, often overwhelming liquidity especially in less liquid markets... trade volume tends to be highly concentrated at the time of market close on reconstitution dates—ranging from 3 times for the CRSP US Mid Cap Index to over 27 times for the S&P 500 index." (paraphrased from search summary)
> "Delayed execution of reconstitution trades—especially at the closing auction of the reconstitution day—can result in substantial increase of implementation costs, though executing trades gradually or in advance may reduce these costs without materially affecting tracking error." (paraphrased from search summary)

## Relevance
Directly supports central question 2/1: index/passive funds are structurally compelled to trade at the close because index-tracking mandates and benchmarks are defined using official closing prices, and deviating from the close creates tracking error versus the benchmark. This is a self-reinforcing mechanical reason (not an information-based one) for closing-price significance in equities — important for the later argument that gold lacks an equivalent mechanical anchor.
