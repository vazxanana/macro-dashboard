## Reported content
> "On expiration dates the closing prices of stocks with listed options cluster at option strike prices, with the returns of optionable stocks altered by an average of at least 16.5 basis points, which translates into aggregate market capitalization shifts on the order of $9 billion... If the open interest on a particular contract is unusually large, delta-hedging in aggregate by floor market-makers can impact the stock price and drive it to the strike price of the option." (paraphrased, aggregated with related pinning literature — see also "Weekly Options on Stock Pinning," wpunj.edu PDF, and "Stock price clustering on option expiry dates," ResearchGate)

## Relevance
This is genuine peer-reviewed academic evidence for expiry pinning, with a quantified effect size (~16.5bps average price distortion, ~$9B aggregate market-cap shift on expiry days) and an explicit dealer delta-hedging mechanism as the causal channel. Importantly, the title and study population is S&P 500 **futures**, i.e., pinning evidence does extend beyond single-name equities into an index futures/derivatives context — the closest analog available to how gold futures (GC) options might behave. This is the strongest piece of evidence in this batch for extending pinning beyond retail equities toward futures markets, though it is still not gold-specific.
