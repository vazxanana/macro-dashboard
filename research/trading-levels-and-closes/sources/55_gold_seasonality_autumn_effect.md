## Reported content
> "A major study analyzing gold returns from 1980 to 2010 found that September and November are the only months with positive and statistically significant gold price changes... September yields an average return of 2.2% whereas November yields an average return of 1.8%. The average returns in the remaining months are all statistically insignificant." (paraphrased from search-engine synthesis of the "Autumn Effect" paper.)
> Separately, an unverified blog claim: "Based on 50 years of historical data (1974-2024), the strongest months are September (+2.1%), January (+1.8%), and November (+1.4%), while the weakest months are March (-0.6%), June (-0.4%), and April (-0.3%)." (paraphrased, single blog source, not cross-checked.)

## Relevance
This is the strongest specific statistical claim found for gold seasonality — one academic-style study (1980-2010) identifying September and November as the only months with statistically significant positive returns, with plausible fundamental explanations (physical demand + equity-hedging flows). However, it was only accessed via a secondary snippet/abstract, not verified full text, and the more expansive "50-year, 3-losing/3-winning-months" framing comes from a single promotional trading blog, not an academic source — that broader claim should be treated with more skepticism than the narrower September/November finding.
