## Reported content
> "London is a key trading hub with more than US$160bn in over-the-counter volumes per day, mostly in the form of spot contracts. The London OTC market comprises approximately 70% of global notional trading volume." (reported figure — not verified against primary text)
> "The usual minimum size of transaction is 2,000 ounces of gold (while the standard size is 5,000 ounces). Trades are primarily done in large bars of gold, each weighing approximately 400 troy ounces." (reported figure — not verified against primary text)
> "90% of global gold market trades take place in the London OTC Market, New York's COMEX, or the Shanghai Gold Exchange, with London being the most dominant among these three centers." (reported figure — not verified against primary text)

## Relevance
Quantifies why London OTC (and by extension the LBMA AM/PM fixes drawn from that market) is treated as the reference price for physical gold: it is roughly 70% of global notional volume and trades in institutional-size 400oz bars, dwarfing typical retail/CFD trade sizes. This underlies why technical/institutional traders treat London fix times as higher-conviction inflection points than random intraday COMEX prints.
