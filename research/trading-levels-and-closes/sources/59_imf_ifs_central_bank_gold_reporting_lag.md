## Reported content
> "IMF IFS data is reported with a two-month lag, and while most institutions report on a regular basis, some may report with a – sometimes significant – delay... most countries' figures reflect holdings as of 31 December, while late-reporting countries may reflect September or earlier." (paraphrased.)
> "Central banks are expected to report their gold holdings monthly to the International Monetary Fund, and those submissions are compiled into the IMF's International Financial Statistics database. However, not every central bank files accurate reports on time, and some major holders have historically gone silent for extended periods without any formal disclosure." (paraphrased.)

## Relevance
This is the **slowest and least reliable** cadence among all sources surveyed once real-world non-reporting/delayed-reporting behavior is factored in — nominally monthly, but effectively ~2 months lagged even for compliant reporters, and materially worse (multi-month to unbounded) for non-compliant major holders (several large sovereign buyers are widely believed to under-report or delay-report their true purchases, e.g., China's reported figures are frequently flagged elsewhere as understating actual accumulation — though that specific claim was not independently re-verified in this pass). Central bank buying, however real and consequential to the multi-year price trend, cannot function as a timely input for a daily trading engine; it belongs only in a slow structural/macro-narrative layer, updated at most monthly and always understood to be looking ~2+ months into the past.
