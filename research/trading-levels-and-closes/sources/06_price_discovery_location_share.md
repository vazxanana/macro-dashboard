## Reported content
> "Although both markets contribute to price discovery, the New York futures play a larger role on average in setting the price of gold when comparing the London spot market and the New York futures market." (reported finding — not verified against primary text)
> "The volume of gold traded in New York is less than a tenth of the London spot volume" yet COMEX has outsized influence on price discovery — attributed to market structure/transparency rather than volume. (reported figure — not verified against primary text)
> "Following COMEX's move to the fully electronic GLOBEX platform... the price discovery shares of the two major trading centers became largely dictated by daylight hours within each market locale – the UK OTC market plays a more important role during UK daylight hours and the US futures market plays a more important role during US daylight hours." (paraphrased); a further electronic-platform upgrade is reported to give COMEX a "consistent lead throughout the day irrespective of market hours." (paraphrased)

## Relevance
This is the key academic reference establishing that price discovery leadership is NOT purely proportional to traded volume — COMEX futures, despite far smaller volume than London OTC spot, dominate price discovery due to transparency/continuous electronic trading, with leadership rotating by regional daylight session. Directly answers "where does gold price discovery happen and in what proportion" with a citable, peer-reviewed source rather than a market blog claim.
