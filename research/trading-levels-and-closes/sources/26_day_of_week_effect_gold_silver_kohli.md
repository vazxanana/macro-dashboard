## Reported content
> (paraphrased from search synthesis) "The day of the week effect is based on the premise that asset returns tend to be notably higher during some days of the week, usually on Fridays, and notably lower during other days, usually on Monday... Kohli (2012) finds the day of the week effect in both gold and silver markets." Related, from a different paper in the same search: "The day-of-the-week effect is evident in the implied volatility of oil, gold, silver and energy equities, with implied volatility significantly lower on Fridays and higher on Mondays."
> Important caveat surfaced in the same search: "the moment an exploitable anomaly is identified and made public, market participants arbitrage it out of existence... if the Monday effect generated alpha in the 1980s, its widespread dissemination in the financial literature has progressively eroded its exploitability."

## Relevance
Directly relevant to Question 3 (is there anything special about the Friday/week-end close). This is genuine academic evidence of a real Friday/Monday return-and-volatility asymmetry in gold specifically — implied vol lower on Friday, higher on Monday, consistent with pre-weekend position-squaring/de-risking rather than new information arriving. However, day-of-week effects are a textbook example of anomalies that decay once published (explicitly noted in the same search results), so any edge from this specific effect today is likely much smaller than in the 2012 sample, or arbitraged away. This supports "Friday close is mechanically special due to position-squaring/gap-risk avoidance" but does NOT support "Friday close carries extra directional information."
