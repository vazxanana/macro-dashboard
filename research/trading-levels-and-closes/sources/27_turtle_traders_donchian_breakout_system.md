## Relevance
Useful historical counterpoint: the most famous systematic breakout system in trading folklore used 20-day and 55-day *daily-bar* lookback windows, NOT a weekly-close rule as such. This is evidence that historically successful trend/breakout systems have been built on daily data with longer lookback windows rather than literally requiring a weekly (Friday) close — suggesting the "N-day channel measured on daily bars" and "weekly close" framings are different constructs that get conflated in retail discourse. Also directly supports the "opposition" angle: even proponents here admit modern crowding has degraded the edge, i.e., any historical edge attributed to breakout/timeframe filtering has itself decayed with adoption — consistent with reflexivity/crowding rather than a stable information advantage.
