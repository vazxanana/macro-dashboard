## Reported content
> "Max pain is not predictive and does not account for real-time hedging flows, fundamentals, macro events, or short-term catalysts. Max pain is observational, not predictive in any rigorous sense, working some of the time (especially on highly-liquid index ETFs into monthly expirations) and failing completely other times (especially when news drives directional moves)... Academic studies show that SPX and SPY have a modest gravitational pull to max pain on monthly expirations, with only 1-2% of moves attributable to the effect, though the effect is stronger on quarterly OPEX when open interest is largest... Max Pain is best used as a hypothesis generator, not as a standalone trading signal." (paraphrased/aggregated)

## Relevance
This is the honest counterweight requested: even sources sympathetic to options-flow analytics concede Max Pain has weak, inconsistent, mostly-anecdotal support — "1-2% of moves attributable" for major liquid index products, worse for less liquid/more news-driven instruments. Gold is heavily macro/news-driven (Fed policy, real yields, geopolitical headlines — exactly the categories these sources say overwhelm Max Pain's pull). This is a strong argument against building a Max Pain feature into the platform even if the OI data were obtainable.
