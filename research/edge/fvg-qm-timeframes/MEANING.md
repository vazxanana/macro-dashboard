# What this result means (25 Nov 2026)

The idea: price continues, comes back and taps a fair value gap, a QM or a lower-timeframe CHoCH confirms, you enter, target the low of the move. The first study tried H1 on five markets (2010 on) and failed. This one tried the other timeframes on data nobody had read for it (2005-2009 on the five markets; 2005 to now on five more, costs assumed).

- **H4 gap with an H1 QM or CHoCH (R1, R2):** 418 and 1,003 trades, −0.035R and −0.064R a trade after costs. Before costs, zero. Not significant either way.
- **M15 gap with an M5 QM or CHoCH (R3, R4):** 3,301 and 8,751 trades, −0.097R and −0.099R after costs (month t −3.8 and −6.1). Before costs about −0.02R: the cost, a few hundredths of an R on these small stops, decides it.
- **H1 gap with the QM relaxed (R5):** +0.042R, but only 180 trades, month t 0.40, negative on the 2005-2009 markets. Nothing to read into it.
- Zero of five readings pass. Buys and sells lose alike.

So the owner's chain, as a mechanical rule, is not an edge on any of these timeframes. Like every FVG, QM and cycle rule here, it sits at zero before costs. This does not judge your own marking of the setups. Do not test variants on these years; a new idea needs new data and its own pre-registration.
