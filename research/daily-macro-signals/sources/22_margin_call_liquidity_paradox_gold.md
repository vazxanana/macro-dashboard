## Relevance
This is a clean, explicit statement of the FAILURE mode the research question asked about: gold's high liquidity is precisely what makes it a preferred asset to sell for margin/redemption calls in "phase one" of an acute liquidity crisis (2008, March 2020), producing a sharp fall at the exact moment a naive safe-haven model would predict a rise. The claimed mechanism is two-phase: liquidity-driven selloff first (days to a few months), then reassertion of the safe-haven/lower-real-yield thesis once central-bank liquidity returns. For a daily bias engine, this means a VIX spike or credit-spread blowout during an acute, cash-scramble phase of a crisis is not just "non-predictive" of a gold rally — it can predict the *opposite* direction until liquidity conditions stabilize, and there is no simple daily rule offered here for distinguishing "phase one" (sell gold for cash) from steady-state safe-haven demand in real time.
