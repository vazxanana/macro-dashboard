## Reported quotes/paraphrases (not independently verified against primary-source text)
> "Gold tends to rally when credit spreads widen, but that's usually the case when the move in credit spreads is driven by broader systemic risks."

> Gold "tends to react to credit spread widening when it is accompanied by a rise in the National Financial Conditions Index (NFCI) — a broad proxy for systemic risk" — framed as gold acting "as a hedge during broader financial system stress" in that specific conditional case.

> "Any widening in spreads that is not deemed to be systemic and perhaps only reflecting sector or company-specific default risk, asset allocation decisions, or just falling Treasury yields — is unlikely on its own to drive investors towards gold."

> Named counter-examples where credit spreads widened but gold did **not** rally: "the 1991 Gulf War, the Dot Com bubble, and the 2014-15 commodity-led slowdown."

> Separately, from a related secondary summary of the broader credit-spread/gold relationship: "A rise in credit spreads between 2007 and 2009 proved to be bullish for gold, while the narrowing of credit spreads between 2011 and 2014 was accompanied by a decline in the price of gold," yet "credit spreads were tightened between 2003 and 2007, while [gold] enjoyed a bull market" — i.e., the correlation sign is not stable across regimes even at the multi-year level, let alone daily.

## Relevance
This is the most direct evidence found on the credit-spreads-to-gold question, and it is explicitly conditional: widening high-yield/credit spreads only tend to coincide with gold strength when the spread-widening reflects *systemic* financial stress (proxied by the NFCI), not idiosyncratic credit risk, sector-specific selloffs, or plain Treasury-yield moves. Three named historical counter-examples (Gulf War 1991, Dot Com bust, 2014-15 commodity slowdown) show credit spreads widening without a gold rally. Nothing here supports a mechanical "HY spreads widen today → gold up" rule; if anything it argues a spread-widening signal needs a systemic-stress filter (e.g., co-occurring with an NFCI/VIX spike) before it should carry any weight in a scoring model, and even then the claims are period-level, not evidence of next-day predictability.
