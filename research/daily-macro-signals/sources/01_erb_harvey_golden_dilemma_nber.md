## Reported quotes/paraphrases (not independently verified against primary-source text)
> "very strong negative correlation between real interest rates and gold prices (from 1997 to 2012) of -0.82"

> "The strong correlation -0.82 is likely spurious with a small sample" — Erb and Harvey caution explicitly against the "correlation implies causation" trap, and compare over-fit macro/gold correlations to Leinweber's (2007) finding that butter production in Bangladesh historically "explained" 75% of the variation in the S&P 500.

> On causal direction: historical data are consistent with low real yields "causing" high real gold prices (a Gibson's-paradox-style reading), but the paper explicitly notes it is equally consistent with reverse causality (high real gold prices "causing" low real yields) or with both being driven by a third, possibly unmeasurable factor such as fear/risk aversion.

> Gold's real price is mean-reverting: when the real gold price is high relative to history, subsequent real gold returns tend to be below average.

## Relevance
This is the foundational academic reference for the "gold vs. real yields" relationship and is almost always the ultimate source behind the popular -0.7 to -0.8 correlation figures cited by industry commentary. Critically, its own authors frame the finding as a **static, long-horizon (over a ~15-year sample) correlation of levels**, not a daily or short-horizon predictive regression, and they explicitly warn the correlation may be spurious/data-mined and that causality direction is unresolved (gold could be causing real yields to fall, not the other way around, via a shared risk-aversion factor). This directly supports the "contemporaneous co-movement, not proven prediction" side of the research question, from the most authoritative source in the literature.
