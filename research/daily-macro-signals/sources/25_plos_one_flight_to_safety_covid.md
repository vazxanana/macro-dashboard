## Reported quotes/paraphrases (not independently verified against primary-source text)
> The study examines "the interactions between the option-implied volatilities of the stock market (VIX) and of the gold (GVZ) and oil (OVX) markets," finding "granger causality in general between the equity market and the gold as well as the oil market."

> Using **asymmetric** causality tests specifically: "positive shocks in VIX cause positive shocks in GVZ, demonstrating that the typical flight to safety effect observed during other financial crises can also be identified for the COVID-19 crisis" — i.e., the VIX→GVZ (gold volatility) link only shows up when isolating positive/negative shocks separately, not in the plain symmetric Granger test.

> "During the COVID-19 crisis, a stronger influence of the equity market on the oil market can be observed, though this cannot be observed for the gold market using symmetric causality tests" — implying the standard (symmetric) Granger causality test failed to find equity→gold volatility causality during COVID-19, and only the asymmetric decomposition recovered a flight-to-safety-consistent signal.

> Separately, the paper is reported to note that Treasury markets behaved unusually in March 2020: "while stock prices fell dramatically and the VIX spiked as expected, Treasury bonds did not follow the established crisis playbook," which the paper flags as needing explanation given Treasuries' normal safe-haven role.

## Relevance
This paper is useful precisely because it shows how fragile the "VIX spike → safe-haven asset moves" relationship is even in gold's own implied-volatility market (GVZ), let alone gold's price/return: the *plain* symmetric Granger test reportedly failed to detect equity-to-gold causality during COVID-19, and only an asymmetric (positive-shock-only) test recovered a flight-to-safety-consistent pattern. Combined with the explicit finding that Treasuries — gold's usual safe-haven companion — broke down entirely in March 2020, this is direct academic evidence that "this time was different": standard flight-to-safety relationships were not straightforwardly present or robust during the most severe and famous liquidity-crisis gold selloff of the modern era, reinforcing sources 22-23 rather than contradicting them.
