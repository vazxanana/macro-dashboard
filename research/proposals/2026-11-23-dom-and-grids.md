# 23 Nov 2026: the combination grids and the DOM zone, one page

Every study of the day with its own verdict line (copied from its RESULT.md). Each folder is under `research/edge/<topic>/` with its pre-registration, code, result.json and test, and is listed on the Edge lab page. Research only: nothing here changes a live rule.

## The finding in short

- About 8 million rule variants of the engulf, Mosha and MSNR pieces (four grids) found one before-cost tilt: a resting limit at the edge of an H1 dominant zone, about +0.05R a trade, also on 2005-2019 and on a second price feed.
- The DOM zone is not what makes it: a limit at a random recent candle body of the same colour did better (+0.080R). The tilt belongs to resting limit orders judged on mid-price candles.
- Mostly a fill effect: with a limit that fills only when price trades through it by half the round trip the tilt falls to +0.011R (2015-19 +0.004R), and limits at random prices make -0.010R. On the demo broker's own bid / ask it loses (`dom-tick-exec`: -0.111R a filled trade, n 1,706, month t -3.73; a bug in its sell targets was found and fixed before the verdict): the DOM zone limit is closed. The DOM demo job (`council/dom-demo.mjs`) records real demo fills from 23 Nov.
- Since then every new grid enters at market or with a trade-through limit. One rule held up on 2010-2019 (`grid5-msnr-box`, below): a Daily engulf zone, an H1 box close-out, an H1 structure break in New York hours, market entry, +0.233R a trade before costs in 2015-19 (n 317, month t 4.40). It was one cell of 11,625 and the grid as a whole sat at chance; on data the grid never read it does not hold (`msnr-box-holdout`: 2005-09 +0.018R, n 261, t 0.29; five other markets 2010-19 -0.016R, n 595; 2020 on +0.009R, n 422). It was the lucky cell. `grid6-eg-box-market` found the same cell on the same years, so it adds no evidence.

- A pattern across the grids of the evening: the side-flipped placebo often did better than the real trades (box inside box, Mosha at market, DOM with a confirmation). On ten markets and 22 years (`grid7-wide-core`) entering WITH an M15 structure break or box close-out after an H4 level loses before costs in both windows (engulf zone: -0.031R a trade, month t -5.02 and -3.56), and fading it passes. Fading lower-timeframe breaks is tested on unread data in `fade-breaks-holdout` (running).
- `grid7-wide-fade` (54 cells fixed in advance, ten markets, discovery 2005-14, confirmation 2015-26): fading the first M5, M15 or H1 structure break at market passes 22 cells before costs in both windows (M15, ATR stop, 2R: +0.054R / +0.042R a trade, month t 11.4 / 11.3); the same signals traded with the break lose. The same five rules hold before costs on eight markets the grid never read and on a second price feed (`fade-new-markets`: M15 ATR 2R +0.032R a trade, month t 8.74, and +0.056R, t 10.98). Next, running: the demo broker's own bid / ask (`fade-tick-exec`, eight probe batches). Where it lives (`fade-conditions`): mostly 20-23 UTC (M15: +0.169R a trade in 2015-26, month t 14.1, against about zero 08-15 UTC), in low volatility and on Mondays; it does not depend on the higher-timeframe structure (`fade-mtf-context`) and is not stronger at opens, round numbers or equal highs / lows. 20-23 UTC holds the daily rollover, when quotes jump: the broker-tick test is what tells a real reaction from a quote effect. With the rollover hours (21-22 UTC) left out, 20 and 23 UTC still hold on both feeds (`fade-hour-check`: +0.109R a trade on OANDA 2015-26, month t 7.12; +0.118R on HistData, t 6.50). `dom-touch-market`: the DOM zone's market-entry tilt is matched by a random band, so it is the same short-term pull-back, not the zone.
