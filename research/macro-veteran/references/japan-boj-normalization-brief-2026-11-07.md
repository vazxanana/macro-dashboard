# Japan: BOJ normalization has three separate market channels

**As of:** 7 November 2026; public information available through the BOJ's 2 November Outlook release.  
**Question:** What did the BOJ's 30 October decision change for Japanese rates, JGB supply absorption, and equities?  
**Decision supported:** Identify which official releases and market series to monitor; this is not a trade recommendation.  
**Instrument / geography:** JPY rates, JGBs, Japanese equities, and USD/JPY.  
**Horizon:** Policy event and coming quarters.  
**Conclusion:** The decision held the overnight policy rate at 1.25% but carried a hawkish internal signal through a dissent for a 25 bp increase and a higher inflation outlook. Separately, the BOJ announced a faster but still gradual and adjustable ETF disposal pace. Its already-announced JGB purchase taper is a third channel: it changes the amount of duration the central bank absorbs, with realized tender data needed to assess implementation. These impulses should not be collapsed into one “tightening” number.  
**Evidence assessment:** Measurement confidence high for the official decisions and schedules; interpretation support medium because rates, FX, and risk assets also respond to global yields, fiscal supply, positioning, and risk appetite.  
**Forecast probability:** No new directional market forecast issued in this brief.

## What matters most

- The BOJ held at 1.25% by an 8–1 vote; the dissent proposed 1.50%. The official statement separately announced an increase in ETF sales from about ¥330bn to ¥860bn per year at book value, starting January 2027.
- ETF book value is not market value or a promised fixed cash-sale amount. The BOJ estimated a market-value equivalent of about ¥2.46tn annually under its stated calculation, and retained authority to adjust or suspend sales temporarily.
- No timestamped pre-decision OIS/futures pricing snapshot has been verified here. Therefore this brief does not claim a measured policy surprise or assert that the decision was “priced in.”

## Scope and method

- **Included:** 30 October MPM statement; 2 November Outlook; June 2026 JGB purchase plan; BOJ operation/account data routes; daily MOF benchmark yields.
- **Excluded:** A causal event study, private dealer consensus, proprietary order flow, and any trading recommendation.
- **Method:** Read primary documents and separate announced settings, planned operations, realized transactions, and market outcomes. Secondary market reporting is used only as a lead, not as causal proof.
- **Key assumption:** The official published schedule is the policy signal; observed tender and account data are needed to judge execution and balance-sheet effects.

## Three channels, three measurements

| Channel | What changed / what to observe | Economic transmission | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Policy rate and expected path | 30 Oct: target held around 1.25%; 8–1 vote, with one member proposing 1.50%. Read future MPM decisions, BOJ communication, and market-implied rates separately. | Short-end yields and expected real rates; potentially yen support and a higher discount rate for risk assets, conditional on other central banks and risk premia. | A hold is not a hike. The dissent is a signal, not the committee decision or a guaranteed next move. |
| JGB purchase taper | June plan: about ¥2.7tn/month in Apr–Jun 2026, ¥2.5tn in Jul–Sep, ¥2.3tn in Oct–Dec, ¥2.1tn in Jan–Mar 2027, then ¥2tn/month from Apr 2027. Compare with actual BOJ offers/results and the MOF issuance calendar. | Changes in BOJ absorption can alter free-float duration and term premium, unevenly by maturity and depending on private demand and issuance. | A purchase plan is not actual net duration supply; scheduled purchases, tenders, redemptions, and government issuance must be reconciled. The Oct–Dec reduction was announced in June, so it is not a new 30 Oct surprise. |
| ETF disposal | From Jan 2027, annual pace rises to ¥860bn book value from about ¥330bn; BOJ estimated roughly ¥2.46tn at market value based on its stated calculation. | Potential equity supply and a signal about balance-sheet normalization; effects may vary with index/constituent exposures and market liquidity. | It does not imply immediate liquidation of the stock, a mechanical index-price decline, or ¥860bn of market-value sales. |

The BOJ said ETF sales began in January 2026 at roughly ¥330bn annual book value, and that the new pace corresponds to about 0.1% of TSE Prime trading value using its Jan–Sep 2026 inputs. The estimate is useful scale, not a forecast of price impact. The BOJ can temporarily adjust or suspend sales if market conditions warrant. J-REIT sales remain at around ¥5bn book value per year. ([30 October policy statement](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/mpmdeci/mpr_2026/k261030a.pdf); [ETF sales decision](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/mpmdeci/mpr_2026/k261030c.pdf))

## Policy backdrop

The 2 November Outlook described moderate growth continuing at a slower pace, with energy and material costs weighing while government measures, accommodative financial conditions, and AI-related demand support activity. The BOJ expected CPI excluding fresh food to rise clearly above 2% before easing toward around 2%; it judged risks to prices as skewed upward and activity risks as broadly balanced. It also noted that its projections incorporate market views on future policy, so the forecast is not an independent market-pricing benchmark. ([BOJ Outlook, October 2026](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/outlook/gor2610a.pdf))

The October decision release gave the policy-rate result at 12:02 JST on 30 October; the full Outlook followed on 2 November at 14:00 JST. This sequencing matters: an event window around the rate statement cannot include the later outlook without mixing two separate information arrivals. The BOJ scheduled its Summary of Opinions for 10 November and minutes for 23 December. ([BOJ release schedule in the statement](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/mpmdeci/mpr_2026/k261030a.pdf))

**Surprise calculation:** Not calculated against market pricing: no defensible, timestamped pre-decision OIS/futures baseline was captured. The economist survey suggests the 25 bp October hike was not the modal expectation, but it cannot quantify the market surprise.  
**Interpretation:** The dissent and higher inflation projections are consistent with a tightening bias, but they do not establish the timing or size of the next move. The reported yen strength is a joint-news observation, not a clean estimate of the BOJ decision effect.

### What is already priced or expected?

- **Benchmark:** The Reuters economist poll fieldwork ended 19 Oct, 11 days before the decision. It is dated survey context, not a market-price benchmark; no contemporaneous OIS/futures snapshot was verified.
- **Implied baseline:** Survey respondents largely expected a hold in October and a hike later, but market-implied pricing remains unknown. No claim that the dissent or ETF announcement was priced or unpriced.
- **Thesis gap:** Cannot be quantified without a frozen pre-release expectation and an event-price series.
- **Limits:** A market-implied rate embeds term/liquidity premia and may not be a pure probability. A survey can be stale or represent only a subset of dealers.

## Competing explanations

| Explanation | Evidence supporting it | Evidence against it | What would distinguish it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| October decision raises the likelihood of further BOJ normalization | One member proposed 1.50%; BOJ described inflation risks as upside-skewed and underlying inflation as broadly consistent with target. | Committee held; one dissent is not a majority. The BOJ’s forecasts use market policy assumptions. | Subsequent votes, wage and service-price data, BOJ guidance, and timestamped OIS repricing. |
| The higher ETF-sales pace matters mainly as a slow supply/signal channel | The schedule begins in Jan 2027, is gradual, and is about 0.1% of the BOJ’s cited trading-value denominator; temporary adjustment/suspension is allowed. | The aggregate ratio can conceal index concentration, liquidity conditions, and marginal-flow effects. | Actual sales and holdings data, constituent-level exposure, ETF liquidity, and abnormal returns around implementation. |
| JGB term premium may rise as BOJ absorption declines | Monthly purchase pace is scheduled to decline toward ¥2tn from Apr 2027. | Private demand, fiscal issuance, maturity mix, and any BOJ adjustment can offset or dominate the taper. | Match actual BOJ tenders and MOF issuance by tenor; monitor auction tails, term premium estimates, and cash/futures basis. |

## Scenarios and monitoring

| Scenario | Conditions / trigger | Conditional transmission | Evidence that weakens it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Faster policy normalization | Wage/service inflation stays firm; upside CPI risks persist; BOJ communication and votes shift toward a majority for increases. | Front-end JGB yields and expected real rates rise; yen support is more likely if foreign rates/risk appetite do not move against it. | Inflation and wage momentum fade; BOJ emphasizes the energy shock as temporary; market pricing does not confirm the signal. |
| Gradual balance-sheet normalization without near-term hike | Rate holds continue, but purchase schedule and ETF sales proceed as announced. | More term/equity supply is absorbed by private investors over time; adjustment may be maturity- and constituent-specific. | BOJ pauses/adjusts; strong private demand offsets supply; no measurable market response around actual operations. |
| Global yields dominate local signal | US Treasury yields or global risk premia move sharply. | USD/JPY and JGB yields can move opposite to the simple BOJ-normalization narrative. | Japan-specific rate differentials and event-window moves explain subsequent price changes better than global factors. |
