# Jobs-report news: multi-field first-release research

The [36-release Employment Situation panel](employment-situation-multifield-with-hours-2022-2024.csv) covers dated BLS releases from January 2022 through December 2024 (reference months December 2021–November 2024). It preserves the **printed current payroll change, unemployment rate, average-hourly-earnings cents and level, average weekly hours**, and the prior two payroll-change revisions when the release has a comparable statement. Monthly wage percentage is present only where printed in the retrieved prose: **32/36 rows**. January 2022's annual benchmark release has no standard two-month revision paragraph in this extraction; the fields are **blank, not zero**.

[Headline source excerpts](employment-situation-headline-extraction-evidence-2022-2024.json), [wage excerpts](employment-situation-wage-extraction-evidence-2022-2024.json), [revision excerpts](employment-situation-revision-extraction-evidence-2022-2024.json), [hours excerpts](employment-situation-hours-extraction-evidence-2022-2024.json), and the [machine validation](employment-situation-headline-validation-2022-2024.json) document each value and limit. The archive URL and scheduled UTC clock are recorded; actual first web publication and receipt remain unauthenticated. Archived pages may have been corrected after posting.

## Why hours need their own vintage

Average weekly hours can move in a different direction from the payroll count. The release's own hours change was numeric in **24/36** cases and expressed as stable or unchanged in **12/36**. The latter are encoded as zero with a separate `explicit_stability_words` basis. For example, the [February 2023 release](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_03102023.htm) reported a **0.1-hour decline to 34.5 hours** while payroll employment rose; the hours measure therefore adds context to the labor-input reading. Compare only the hours change stated within that same release, because its previous-month hours estimate may have been revised.

## Why revisions belong in the news event

Among **35** releases with a prior-two-month revision statement, **20** had an absolute combined revision of at least **50,000** jobs; **11** were at least **100,000**. The median absolute combined revision was **71,000** jobs. There were **22 negative** and **13 positive** sums. These are descriptive first-release figures, not revision surprises or returns. January releases can also contain annual benchmark and seasonal-factor changes, so a revision sum is not always ordinary monthly late reporting.

The [November 1, 2024 release](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_11012024.htm) printed **+12,000** October payroll jobs and revisions of **−81,000** for August and **−31,000** for September. BLS also documented hurricanes and strike effects; it said the net hurricane effect on national payroll change could not be quantified. A rule based only on the +12,000 headline would omit material simultaneous information, while simply adding −112,000 to +12,000 would mix different reference months and would not produce a legitimate surprise measure.

## Why wage vintages must stay together

The [February 4, 2022 release](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_02042022.htm) printed January hourly earnings of **$31.63**. The [March 4, 2022 release](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_03042022.htm) printed February earnings of **$31.58**, yet stated that earnings **rose by 1 cent** from its revised January level; its own table shows January at **$31.57**. Comparing February $31.58 with the older January $31.63 would falsely imply a wage decline. The panel therefore stores each release's *within-release* wage change and never calculates it across two vintages.

## Demo strategy hypothesis N2, now testable as a data contract

The [bounded regular-release date audit](employment-regular-release-date-overlap-audit-2022-2024.json) finds zero same-day matches across seven joined CPI/PPI, BEA, Census, industrial-production, sampled claims and FOMC inventories. This is **not** a clean-window certificate: the DOL sample is incomplete, unscheduled notices are outside the join, and a separate [seven-date ISM evidence ledger](ism-jobs-same-day-verified-2022-2024.json) verifies Manufacturing or Services PMI reports on jobs days. These include [April 1, 2022 manufacturing](https://www.ismworld.org/supply-management-news-and-reports/news-publications/inside-supply-management-magazine/blog/2022/2022-04/report-on-business-roundup-march-2022-manufacturing-pmi/) and [May 3, 2024 services](https://www.ismworld.org/supply-management-news-and-reports/news-publications/inside-supply-management-magazine/blog/2024/2024-05/report-on-business-roundup-april-2024-services-pmi/). [ISM's current standard clock](https://www.ismworld.org/supply-management-news-and-reports/reports/rob-report-calendar/) is 10 a.m. Eastern; the historical article dates do not authenticate precise 2022–2024 receipt times. Long jobs-event horizons must screen those later reports.

1. Before each release, freeze separate timestamped consensus figures for payroll change, unemployment rate and monthly earnings growth; retain the prior two payroll estimates as known just before the release. Missing consensus means that field has **no measured surprise**.
2. At release receipt, save the BLS first print and all fields together: current payroll, unemployment, wages, hours and both revisions. Flag annual benchmark, strikes, severe weather and other unusual methodology/measurement notes instead of inventing a numeric adjustment.
3. Predeclare a small set of *aligned* and *conflicting* surprise patterns. Test whether the first available intraday rate/USD response confirms the hypothesized policy interpretation; do not assign a permanent gold direction from payroll alone.
4. Test one fixed lag and horizon on cTrader **demo** XAU/USD using executable bid/ask ticks, spread, slippage, missed orders and event overlap. Compare with no trade and a simple post-release momentum rule. Score by year and preserve every eligible release.

**Current evidence boundary:** The economic release panel is populated, but timestamped consensus, intraday rates/USD, cTrader quote/receipt alignment and executable returns are not. There is **no demonstrated gold trading edge** and no live route.
