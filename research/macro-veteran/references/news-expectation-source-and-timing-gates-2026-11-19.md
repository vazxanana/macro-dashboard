# News expectations: source and timing gates

**Purpose:** Decide which data can measure a pre-release surprise for MeridianX demo research. A forecast of an economic print and the market's consensus are different objects. A forecast published after the release cannot measure a release surprise. A data provider's current historical row is not proof of its pre-release contents.

| Candidate | What it supplies | Exact admissibility gate | Research use now |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trading Economics economic calendar | Its documented `Forecast` is a survey consensus, distinct from `TEForecast`, its own forecast. Records also identify event ID, scheduled UTC time, reference period, original/revised previous values and last update. Its [point-in-time endpoint](https://docs.tradingeconomics.com/economic_calendar/point-in-time/) says it preserves values as they appeared at release. | Obtain licensed access and a **snapshot actually observed before each release** (or provider proof that PIT contains the pre-release forecast and the as-of clock). Preserve raw response, fetch receipt UTC, provider `LastUpdate`, event ID, units and hash. Reject a response fetched after the event as a pre-release snapshot unless provider lineage proves the earlier value. | Best candidate for prospective CPI/NFP consensus capture; historical PIT is a candidate for audit, not yet accepted as pre-release consensus history. Its [field schema](https://docs.tradingeconomics.com/economic_calendar/schema/) makes `Forecast` versus `TEForecast` explicit. |
| Cleveland Fed inflation nowcast | Public model forecasts for monthly headline/core CPI and PCE. The [Fed says](https://www.clevelandfed.org/indicators-and-data/inflation-nowcasting) it updates about 10 a.m. ET each business day. | Store a dated observation from **before** the 8:30 a.m. ET CPI/PCE release. The same-day 10 a.m. update is post-release and inadmissible for that event. Label it *model forecast*, not economist consensus. Preserve target month, measure and seasonal basis. | Useful model baseline for an inflation forecast experiment if a verifiable pre-release historical or prospective snapshot is captured. The current page is not a historical snapshot. |
| CME FedWatch | Futures-implied probabilities of FOMC target-rate outcomes. Its [tool guide](https://www.cmegroup.com/tools-information/quikstrike/cme-fedwatch-tool-user-guide.html) documents historical downloads; its [API page](https://www.cmegroup.com/market-data/market-data-api/fedwatch-api.html) distinguishes end-of-day snapshots and a 60-second intraday product. | Record probability **before** each FOMC decision, plus delivery timestamp, meeting ID, target-rate bins and market-hours status. An end-of-day value after a decision cannot stand in for the pre-decision distribution. For CPI/NFP response, a 60-second probability feed is a coarse secondary reaction measure, not an 8:30:00 quote. | Candidate for FOMC expectations and slow rate-path confirmation; availability, history depth, terms and actual timing require plan-specific verification. |
| New York Fed Survey of Market Expectations | Primary-dealer and market-participant survey questions ahead of FOMC meetings. The [NY Fed says](https://www.newyorkfed.org/markets/market-intelligence/survey-of-market-expectations) results/data are published roughly three weeks **after** the meeting, with minutes. | A survey's fieldwork date is not its public availability date. Do not use published results as a public pre-meeting trading input. | Ex-post research about expectations, not a public real-time FOMC consensus feed. |
| Philadelphia Fed Survey of Professional Forecasters | [Quarterly survey](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/surveys-and-data/real-time-data-research/survey-of-professional-forecasters) and [data files](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/surveys-and-data/data-files) including payroll and CPI measures. | Join only to quarterly targets and releases after its actual publication date; check unit, horizon and target construction. It does not substitute for monthly 8:30 a.m. event consensus. | Slow forecast baseline and regime context. |

## Prospective event capture contract

1. **Pre-release:** choose a cutoff (for example, 5 minutes before announced time) and save the provider's raw response with fetch-start and fetch-end UTC, source URL/plan, event ID, target period, `Forecast`, `TEForecast`, previous and units. Hash the raw bytes. Keep every version rather than overwriting. If no consensus snapshot exists, mark `consensus_missing`.
2. **At release:** store the agency's first received document/feed with receipt UTC and hash. Keep its release/reference date separate from the provider's event clock. Store all simultaneous fields and revisions, including print status and special notes.
3. **For a measured surprise:** require `consensus_snapshot_end_utc < release_actual_receipt_utc` and, for the frozen design, `consensus_snapshot_end_utc <= scheduled_release_utc - cutoff`. Require exact event, period, unit, seasonal basis and transformation match. The consensus source cannot be a later API row reconstructed without lineage.
4. **For a market-response test:** save synchronized executable demo bid/ask ticks and the first eligible observation/entry/exit clocks. A missing consensus can still permit a separately registered response study; label it accordingly and do not backfill a consensus from post-release reporting.
