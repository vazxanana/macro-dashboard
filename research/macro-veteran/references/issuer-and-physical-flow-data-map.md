# Issuer fundamentals and physical-flow data map

**Prepared:** 7 November 2026  
**Purpose:** A practical guide to deeper public records for company fundamentals, ownership, commodities and supply chains. This is a source and method guide, not a trading recommendation.

## Why these records matter

Market-moving evidence often sits below the headline: a filing footnote changes the quality of earnings; a mine's grade and recovery explain why production missed; a customs quantity confirms a supply shift that an inventory headline cannot; or a disclosure shows that a reported shareholder position is already stale. These records are useful only when their **scope, units, publication time, revisions and blind spots** are kept visible.

For each fact, preserve the period it describes, the date it became public, the date retrieved, and any later amendment. Treat reported facts, issuer estimates, analyst calculations and market interpretations as separate evidence classes.

### Hard-to-find issuer evidence inside public filings

The most informative items often require reading inline exhibits or notes rather than querying a headline feed:

- **Revenue quality:** contract assets/liabilities, deferred revenue, billings, returns, rebates, customer concentration, channel inventory and receivables aging.
- **Cash quality:** cash from operations versus net income; capitalized development or interest; stock-based compensation; factoring; supplier finance; pension contributions; and acquisition-related cash costs.
- **Debt/refinancing risk:** maturity ladders, secured versus unsecured debt, floating-rate exposure, covenant headroom, restricted cash, guarantees, cross-defaults and refinancing assumptions.
- **Commodity producers:** reserve/resource changes, grade, recovery, strip ratio, sustaining versus growth capex, by-product credits, royalties, hedges, offtake agreements, permitting, water/energy constraints and closure liabilities.
- **Industrials and retailers:** backlog conversion, cancellation rights, inventory write-downs, purchase commitments, freight/commodity surcharges, warranty accruals and supplier/customer dependency.
- **Banks and insurers:** deposit mix, uninsured deposits, securities portfolio marks, liquidity sources, loan vintage/charge-offs, reserve assumptions, duration mismatch, reinsurance recoverables and catastrophe sensitivity.

Treat a new disclosure as a mechanism to investigate, not a price signal by itself. Build a bridge from reported fact to earnings/cash-flow impact, identify what was already expected, and state what future filing would confirm or falsify the interpretation.

For gold miners, create a comparable quarterly table at mine and company level: payable ounces, ore milled, head grade, recovery, throughput, strip ratio, cash cost/AISC, sustaining versus growth capex, royalties, by-product credits, hedge deliveries and guidance revisions. Reconcile production releases to 10-Q/10-K/20-F/6-K and cash-flow notes. AISC is issuer-defined and non-GAAP/non-IFRS; production ounces are not ounces sold, and early operating releases may be unaudited. For year-end resource and reserve statements, keep each confidence class separate and build a bridge from the prior year: depletion/production, model or method change, commodity-price and operating-cost assumptions, and acquisitions/disposals. The SEC's mining-property rules explicitly identify these as explanations for reserve changes; a rising total can reflect a changed price deck or portfolio perimeter rather than new geological discovery. [SEC Regulation S-K Item 1304(e)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-229/subpart-229.1300)

For mine-life and project assumptions, inspect SEC [S-K 1300 Technical Report Summaries](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-229/subpart-229.1300) in EDGAR and Canadian NI 43-101 reports filed through SEDAR+. Capture qualified-person/effective date, reserve/resource class, cut-off grade, recovery, schedule, capital assumptions, gold/FX price assumptions, closure liabilities, water/power/tailings, and permitting status. These are point-in-time engineering plans, not forecasts of spot price or realized production.

For royalty and streaming companies, distinguish the issuer's economic interest from the operator's mine ownership and production. SEC rules allow a royalty/stream registrant to omit some underlying-property information when it lacks access for specified reasons, provided it identifies the missing information and explains the access limitation. Therefore, compare the royalty company's dated property disclosure with the operator's latest technical report and operating filings; record interest area/share, covered metals, attributable or delivered ounces, payment/deduction terms, term/expiry, and any cap or buyback provisions in the contract. If operator data are stale, incomplete or unavailable, mark that exposure as less verifiable rather than treating the issuer's portfolio summary as independently confirmed. [eCFR S-K 1300 access exception](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-229/subpart-229.1300), [SEC compliance guide](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/small-business-compliance-guides/modernization-property-disclosures-mining-registrants-small-entity-compliance-guide)

### Energy and industrial commodities

- **EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report:** timely US crude/products inventories, refinery runs, imports/exports and balances, with downloadable tables. Weekly estimates are useful but noisy: exports are estimated using near-real-time customs information and weekly balances can swing with cargo timing. Prefer 4-week averages and later monthly Petroleum Supply Monthly data when the question permits. [EIA WPSR](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/supply/weekly/), [export-estimation methodology](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/supply/weekly/pdf/appendixd.pdf), [EIA on weekly-data volatility](https://www.eia.gov/tools/faqs/faq.php?id=1398&t=6).
- **EIA natural-gas storage:** weekly working-gas inventory by region is a direct inventory observation, but weather-normalization, production, LNG flows and pipeline constraints determine interpretation. Pair the level and weekly change with degree days, output, pipeline/LNG feedgas and regional basis. [EIA Natural Gas Storage](https://www.eia.gov/naturalgas/storage/).
- **IEA Oil Market Report / Monthly Oil Data Service:** broad global supply-demand balances, OECD stocks, refinery throughput and selected non-OECD market information. Some detailed data products require subscription. Treat balances as estimates subject to revisions and incomplete non-OECD observation; compare with national agencies and observed flows. [IEA OMR](https://www.iea.org/data-and-statistics/data-product/oil-market-report-omr), [IEA MODS](https://www.iea.org/data-and-statistics/data-product/monthly-oil-data-service-mods-supply-demand-balances-and-stocks).
- **LME warehouse stocks and warrants:** daily stocks by metal/location, cancellations and warrant status are useful for deliverable-market tightness. A cancelled warrant is a removal intention, not evidence the metal has physically left; exchange stock is only a subset of global inventory. Use the LME's official warehouse reports and rule definitions, then compare nearby spreads, regional premiums and producer/consumer reports. [LME market data](https://www.lme.com/Market-data/Reports-and-data/Warehouse-and-stocks-reports).
- **Agriculture:** USDA WASDE, Crop Progress, Export Sales and Grain Stocks combine forecast, survey and inspection data. Keep estimate versus measured shipment separate, note revisions and distinguish crop-year from calendar-year. Compare reported sales with inspections, futures spreads and weather. [USDA WASDE](https://www.usda.gov/oce/commodity/wasde), [USDA Export Sales](https://apps.fas.usda.gov/esrquery/), [USDA NASS reports](https://www.nass.usda.gov/Publications/).

### Trade and logistics: what you can and cannot observe

1. **Official customs datasets** are usually the most defensible public source for realized cross-border trade. The US Census International Trade API and products offer monthly commodity/partner detail; UN Comtrade harmonizes country-reported annual and monthly merchandise trade. Both can be revised; mirror exports and imports can differ because of valuation, timing, route and classification. [Census API/data](https://www.census.gov/data/developers/data-sets/international-trade.html), [UN Comtrade](https://comtradeplus.un.org/).
2. **Physical shipment timing is harder.** Port/manifest databases, AIS vessel tracks, satellite imagery and bill-of-lading aggregators may be commercially licensed, incomplete or inferred. They can help estimate vessel location, cargo class or likely arrival windows, but they do not automatically reveal cargo quantity, beneficial owner, final destination or whether a ship's signal is complete. Record vendor methodology, refresh lag, coverage and redistribution rights.
3. **Satellite and geospatial measures** (tank fills, flaring, mine activity, crop condition, port congestion) are measurements or model outputs with cloud, revisit, classification and calibration error. Use them to target verification, not to convert a visual estimate into a precise inventory without error bounds.
4. **Price and availability are different.** A quoted premium or freight rate may move because of financing, insurance, route risk, taxes, exchange rates, seasonality or local market hours—not only because physical supply is scarce.

## 3. A triangulation recipe for a physical-tightness claim

Before saying “shortage,” define the **deliverable unit, geography, grade/specification, location, date and price basis**. Then test each link:

| Link in the claim | Evidence to seek | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| Supply is unavailable | Producer output, maintenance/outage notices, mine/refinery data, trade arrivals, warehouse stocks by status | Global aggregate stocks hide location/quality mismatch; planned capacity is not realized output. |
| Buyers need material now | End-use orders, imports, inventory draw, processing/refinery utilization, exchange delivery/withdrawal activity | A one-week draw can be weather, shipment timing or seasonal restocking. |
| Market clearing is stressed | Nearby calendar spreads, cash/forward basis, location/grade premiums, lease/funding rates, delivery queues | A premium may be taxes, FX, financing, logistics or benchmark mismatch. |
| Dislocation persists | Multiple independent sources on consecutive dates, with subsequent revisions checked | A single dataset's noisy first print may reverse; repeated evidence reduces this risk. |

Use at least two independent evidence families (for example, customs arrivals plus exchange spreads; or producer outage plus regional premium). Do not count two dashboards that republish the same underlying source as independent confirmation.

## 4. Positioning and ownership facts that are often overstated

- **FINRA short interest** is an as-of-settlement-date snapshot, not daily short activity. Daily short-sale volume is a transaction flow with a different denominator and cannot be added to outstanding short interest.
- **Form 13F** is a lagged disclosure of reportable long positions and certain long put/call holdings over a limited security universe. Short positions and short options are not reported or netted against longs, so it does not reveal a manager's full net exposure. It is useful for structural ownership questions, poor for “what are they holding today?”
- **Insider filings** reveal reported transactions and codes, not intent. A scheduled sale, option-related sale and open-market purchase have different meanings; footnotes and remaining ownership matter.
- **CFTC COT** gives trader categories for futures/options positions, not trader identities or a complete global book. Definitions are functional classifications; use net positions alongside gross long/short, spreads and open interest. [CFTC COT definitions and reports](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/index.htm).
- **ETF holdings/flows** report a listed vehicle channel. Tonnes, shares outstanding, net fund flows and AUM are distinct fields; AUM changes with price. Do not add ETF tonnage to exchange stocks without checking whether the ETF's bars already sit in those vaults.

## 5. Daily and quarterly collection checklist

### For a company event

1. Save the original filing and accepted timestamp, accession number, form type and amendment flag.
2. Capture the exact reported fact plus units, period, segment, XBRL tag/context and footnote.
3. Compare it with the previous filing, consensus vintage (if legitimately available) and management's earlier guide.
4. Build an explicit bridge to revenue, margins, cash flow, debt or share count; show assumptions and sensitivity.
5. Record a counter-explanation and the next disclosure that can resolve it.

### For a commodity flow event

1. Define the material, grade, location, contract, units and matched timestamps.
2. Store raw inventories/trade data and the source's vintage/revision state.
3. Reconcile flows and stocks (opening stocks + inflows − outflows ± adjustments = closing stocks) where data allow; expose the unexplained adjustment.
4. Match inventory evidence with spreads, basis/premiums, deliveries and producer/consumer evidence.
5. Mark estimates, licensed vendor observations and official measurements separately; do not infer private inventory from a public proxy.

## Source-quality and rights rules

- Official public datasets are not always real-time or complete; “official” does not mean “final.”
- Public visibility does not grant rights to bulk scrape, store or redistribute. Check source terms and vendor licenses before automation.
- Preserve the original data, access timestamp, published timestamp, revision marker, units and transformation code. A chart without these cannot answer what was knowable at the time.
- If a source is proprietary (consensus, AIS, satellite or expert network), record vendor, coverage, methodology, historical availability, survivorship, revisions, cost and reuse terms. Do not call the observation independent if another source resells it.
