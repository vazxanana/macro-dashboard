# Derivatives positioning and flow research map

**Prepared:** 7 November 2026  
**Purpose:** A practical guide to public options, futures-positioning, short-interest and institutional-holdings data. It distinguishes observable facts from vendor inference and hidden positions. This is research infrastructure, not an instruction to trade.

## The key distinction: contract data are not investor intent

Exchange records show transactions, open contracts, prices and sometimes trader categories. They rarely reveal the complete economic exposure of an investor. A futures position can hedge physical inventory, an OTC swap, a bond portfolio or another futures month. An option's open interest has a long and a short side; it does not tell you which side a dealer or customer holds. A manager may offset exposure in another venue or instrument.

Use this evidence ladder in every write-up:

1. **Direct observation:** exchange settlement, volume, open interest, published position report, filing or short-interest record.
2. **Calculation:** net position, percentile, implied volatility, skew, spread, open-interest change or volume-weighted statistic built from named fields.
3. **Interpretation:** “crowded,” “dealer long gamma,” “hedge demand,” “institutional accumulation,” or “short squeeze risk.” These are hypotheses and need more evidence.

Do not jump from step 1 to step 3 without showing the calculation and assumptions.

## Preserve instrument identity and benchmark breaks

Gold series can change meaning even when a vendor keeps a continuous column name. The London Gold Fix ceased after 19 March 2015; the LBMA Gold Price began on 20 March 2015 under ICE Benchmark Administration. Treat the change as a documented benchmark/methodology break, preserve the exact benchmark label and AM/PM fixing, and do not splice without a clear bridge method. The public LBMA page publishes with a delay to midnight London time; real-time prices are distributed under separate arrangements. That public delayed series cannot support an intraday event study. [LBMA Gold Price FAQ](https://www.lbma.org.uk/prices-and-data/lbma-gold-price/lbma-gold-price), [LBMA auction publication details](https://www.lbma.org.uk/prices-and-data/about-lbma-daily-auction-prices)

For COMEX, store the exact product and contract month. Standard GC is a 100-troy-ounce physically delivered contract; CME's 1OZ product is a distinct 1-ounce cash-settled contract linked to GC, with different tick and trading-hour rules. Enhanced-delivery 4GC is a separate, non-fungible physical-delivery product. TAS and TAM are settlement/marker-priced strategies with distinct codes (GC's TAS is GCT and TAM is GCD), so do not read those prints as ordinary outright price trades. Never merge these into one “gold futures” tape without explicit normalization and product-specific rules. [CME GC specifications](https://www.cmegroup.com/content/dam/cmegroup/market-regulation/files/gold-futures-and-options-fact-card.pdf), [CME 1OZ FAQ](https://www.cmegroup.com/articles/faqs/faq-1-oz-gold-futures.html), [CME 4GC FAQ](https://www.cmegroup.com/trading/metals/precious/faq-gold-enhanced-delivery-futures.html), [CME gold product codes](https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/metals/precious/gold.timeAndSales.html)

For continuous futures, retain the outright contract observations and document roll trigger (calendar date or volume/open-interest crossover), roll timestamp, adjustment type (none, additive, ratio, or vendor method), and whether the series is back-adjusted. CME's own continuous price series offers both an active contract (historical liquidity-based switch) and a front contract (switch two business days before expiry), so “continuous futures” alone does not define a reproducible series. Use the actual, unadjusted contract prices for event-window returns and execution research; additive and ratio back-adjustments change history differently. [CME continuous price series description](https://www.cmegroup.com/market-data/cme-group-continuous-price-series.html), [CME research on futures roll-adjustment methods](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/files/demystifiing-time-series-momentum-strategies.pdf)

## How to use positioning without inventing a signal

### COT and TFF

Legacy history is not uniformly weekly: before 30 September 1992, CFTC provides midmonth/month-end observations and warns that some retrospectively compiled midmonth records contain uncorrectable errors. Preserve the native cadence rather than filling it into an assumed weekly history. Nonreportable long/short positions are published residual totals, without trader identities, counts or business classifications. [CFTC historical files](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/HistoricalCompressed/index.htm), [explanatory notes](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/ExplanatoryNotes/index.htm)

- Record the **position date**, publication time, report flavor (futures-only or futures-and-options combined), contract, market, and unit.
- Keep gross long, gross short, spreading, nonreportable and total open interest where present. Net alone discards information.
- For long-format COT, record top-four and top-eight trader concentration ratios alongside category positions. The report provides concentration on gross-long, gross-short, net-long and net-short bases as a share of open interest, pooled across trader categories. A rise can show that reported positioning is increasingly concentrated among a few large traders, but it does not reveal identities or intent. The same trader can appear in both gross-long and gross-short concentration when carrying large offsetting positions, while net concentration offsets that trader's equal long/short exposure. [CFTC concentration-ratio notes](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/ExplanatoryNotes/index.htm)
- For delivery-season analysis, keep delivery notices/stops separate from COT open interest: CFTC states the reported open-interest figure excludes futures contracts against which delivery notices have been issued or stopped. Do not treat notice counts as a direct subtraction from the COT series or as physical metal already removed from warehouses. [CFTC explanatory notes](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/ExplanatoryNotes/index.htm)
- Normalize net positions by open interest and compare with the trader category's own rolling history. State window length and handling of missing/roll observations.
- For managed money, leveraged funds, or asset managers, describe the CFTC category as an aggregate classification. Do not translate it into “speculators” or “real money” without qualification.
- Compare positions with price and open-interest changes. Rising price plus rising open interest is compatible with new risk entering, but cannot identify which side's motives or whether exposure is hedged elsewhere.
- Use futures-only and combined versions consistently over time. CFTC notes combined option positions are delta-adjusted using inputs supplied by exchanges; small arithmetic differences can result from delta conversion.

**A strong weekly note:** “As of Tuesday X, managed-money net length was Y contracts, Z% of open interest and at the Nth percentile of the past five years; it rose/fell by A contracts versus last week. This is a lagged listed-futures snapshot and excludes OTC exposure.”

For a monthly bank cross-check, state the BPR's bank count, U.S. and non-U.S. gross longs/shorts, each as a share of total market open interest, report date and publication date. Keep its 25-month CFTC archive limit in mind and save the official source file and retrieval timestamp prospectively.

**A weak weekly note:** “Funds are bullish and must buy more.” COT gives neither a mandate nor remaining capacity to add.

### Options surface and open interest

Read the surface as a set of prices for risk, not as a forecast with a known probability:

- **ATM implied volatility:** market price of near-ATM variance for a defined tenor.
- **Risk reversal / skew:** relative price of upside versus downside optionality for a specified delta and expiry. Confirm whether the quote is call-minus-put, put-minus-call, or a vendor convention.
- **Term structure:** compare like-for-like tenors around events. Front-end volatility can rise before a known catalyst while longer tenors remain stable.
- **Open interest by strike/expiry:** maps where listed contracts are concentrated. It does not establish dealer gamma sign; every contract has a long and short counterparty, and positions can be hedged OTC.
- **Volume and OI change:** volume is trading during the period; OI is the outstanding total after clearing. Volume above OI does not mean that many new positions were opened because contracts can turn over repeatedly.
- **CVOL:** use CME's published methodology and product-specific history. Decompose auxiliary measures only as defined by CME; do not call an indicator a forecast or dealer-position measure.

Vendor “gamma walls,” “max pain,” dealer gamma and zero-gamma levels are reconstructed estimates. A defensible model must disclose: option universe, implied-vol source, dealer/client sign assumptions, OTC coverage, exercise style, futures/spot mapping, hedge instruments, update cadence and historical validation. If those are absent, label the output a scenario hypothesis only.

### Equity short data

FINRA specifically distinguishes reported short interest from short-sale volume. Build a date alignment table containing settlement date, publication date, shares short, FTD settlement date, free-float definition, average daily volume window and corporate actions. A days-to-cover estimate is a ratio under an assumed volume window, not a promise that shorts need that many days to close. FTD records cannot identify a short sale or explain why delivery was late. Borrow rates, utilization and availability usually come from securities-lending vendors or broker feeds; until Rule 10c-1a data are publicly disseminated, treat them as provider-specific and coverage-limited.

## The hard-to-see exposure map

Public listed records do not fully disclose:

- Dealer/customer direction in OTC options or swaps.
- A fund's total portfolio after futures, swaps, options, cash, cross-asset hedges and prime-broker financing are combined.
- The beneficial owner behind every omnibus or intermediary account.
- Whether an apparent hedge offsets a physical, corporate or balance-sheet exposure elsewhere.
- Intraday changes between CFTC snapshots or SEC filing dates.
- The full amount of hidden/reserve liquidity in an order book or internalized OTC flow.

The honest response is to triangulate and lower confidence when evidence is missing, not to fill the gap with a precise-looking vendor chart.

Regulatory swap data also require field-level cleaning. SEC's annual security-based-swap market report documents duplicate records, invalid notionals and reporting differences between repositories for cleared contracts; treat annual aggregates as a quality-screened cross-check, not a clean timely position series. [SEC security-based swaps report](https://www.sec.gov/files/report-security-based-swaps-062024.pdf). CFTC SDR public streams are anonymized, can cap/round notional and delay block trades; a single SDR is incomplete, while the cross-SDR Weekly Swaps Report arrives later and covers only IRS/CDS/FX. The CFTC's final order, effective 21 July 2026, ended routine daily and event-based Part 20 position reports for physical commodity swaps. It retained recordkeeping and the Commission's authority to call for underlying records and futures-equivalent conversion methods. Public Part 43 transaction dissemination and Part 45 reporting to swap data repositories are not substitutes for public net-position data. [Federal Register final order](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/21/2026-14710/order-sunsetting-certain-large-trader-reporting-requirements-for-physical-commodity-swaps), [CFTC notice](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9269-26), [SDR information](https://www.cftc.gov/Forms/sdrforms.html)

For FX specifically, do not treat SDR Part 43 prints or the Weekly Swaps Report as a complete FX flow tape. CFTC economists state that physically delivered FX swaps, forwards and spot are outside U.S. public swap reporting and account for nearly 90% of the FX OTC market as described in their study; this statistic includes spot activity, so it is not a derivative-only denominator. Public SDR data still do not provide full counterparty-level dealer books. Their 2018–2020 regulatory-data study shows what richer confidential datasets can answer, but it is a historical research paper, not a current public feed. [CFTC FX market data study](https://www.cftc.gov/sites/default/files/2023-12/FX_paper_ada.pdf)

## Cross-check matrix

| Hypothesis | Evidence to combine | What would weaken it |
|---|---|---|
| Gold rally is being driven by fresh listed investment demand | COT managed-money/other reportables, CME volume/OI, ETF tonnage, price/real yield/USD behavior, options skew and risk reversal | Price rises while ETF holdings decline and COT length contracts; price response is explained better by real yields/USD or physical premium movement. |
| A position is crowded and vulnerable to liquidation | Category net/OI percentile, concentration by expiry/strike, volatility/skew, open-interest changes, liquidity/spread, catalyst calendar | Positioning is not extreme; price absorbs adverse news; liquidity and cross-venue confirmation show no forced-flow behavior. |
| Dealer hedging is amplifying intraday moves | Transparent option inventory estimate plus actual option volume/OI and underlying/futures order-book behavior around relevant strikes | Estimate is based solely on public OI with guessed position signs; moves precede option activity or occur across markets for a macro catalyst. |
| A stock has squeeze risk | FINRA short interest at settlement date, borrow fee/availability from named source, float and corporate actions, options positioning, liquidity | Short-sale volume is the only evidence; short-interest dates are stale; float denominator is wrong; borrow remains easy or turnover is deep. |
| Institutional accumulation/distribution is underway | New SEC filing vs prior filing, fund flows/holdings, insider disclosures where relevant, price/volume and corporate filings | 13F snapshot is stale, manager holds only one leg of a hedge, or change is from reclassification/security mapping rather than trade. |

These are research hypotheses; corroboration is not causal identification.

## Collection and calculation checklist

1. Store the source file or response, retrieval timestamp, publication timestamp, observation date, product/contract identifier, and checksum.
2. Keep raw contract counts separate from normalized exposure. If converting to dollars, specify multiplier, underlying price, FX rate and conversion time.
3. Preserve report versions and revisions. Do not overwrite the previous week/month when an exchange or regulator updates a file.
4. Normalize by open interest, float or ADV only after confirming the denominator's date and definition.
5. For options, store underlying, expiry, strike, option type, delta convention, settlement style, implied-vol source, bid/ask quality and open interest date.
6. For COT, store futures-only/combined label and position date. Build percentile features using only data that would have been available on each historical date.
7. For filings, retain accession number, report period, filing date, amendment flag and any confidential-treatment note. Parse holdings only after mapping CUSIP/FIGI/ticker and corporate actions.
8. For equities, keep short interest, short-sale volume and securities-lending measures in separate tables. They are not interchangeable.
9. Before drawing a flow conclusion, state what is unobserved: OTC, swaps, cross-venue hedges, account netting, or the timing gap.
10. Validate any proposed signal against a frozen, point-in-time dataset, realistic trading costs and an untouched forward sample; use the existing [empirical validation protocol](empirical-market-research-validation.md).

## Interpretation boundary

This map supports claims about published listed-market positions, option-implied pricing, reported short positions, and stale disclosed holdings. It does not reveal complete dealer books, real-time institutional portfolios, a specific investor's intent, or a guaranteed price direction. Keep the research/demo cTrader workflow separate from live execution; this document does not enable or authorize live trading.
