# Advanced market intelligence: funding, positioning, filings and physical flows

**Prepared:** 7 November 2026  
**Use:** Companion to the [hard-to-get market data playbook](hard-to-get-market-data-playbook.md), [macro and gold source registry](macro-gold-source-registry.md), and [MeridianX research system blueprint](meridianx-research-system-blueprint.md).  
**Boundary:** This is a research and data-quality guide. It does not provide trade instructions. The cTrader integration remains demo-only; this guide does not enable live trading.

## Why this layer matters

Price is the final, visible result of several less visible processes: funding availability, collateral demand, inventory location, hedging, portfolio constraints, and information arriving at different speeds. Public sources can illuminate parts of these processes, but rarely reveal a complete position or motive. This guide concentrates on the richer, less obvious observations and on the common analytical errors they invite.

The working rule is: **start with a named mechanism, find the closest direct observation, then state the gap between that observation and the claim.** A futures position is not a view; an auction allocation is not a foreign investor; an inventory number is not end-user demand; a filing snapshot is not a current portfolio.

### Funding stress checklist

For a claim such as “dollar liquidity is tightening,” collect a compact dashboard, not a single proxy:

1. **Secured funding:** SOFR/BGCR/TGCR, volumes, distribution and facility operations.
2. **FX funding:** cross-currency basis for USD versus EUR/JPY/GBP, at more than one tenor. Record the market data provider and quote convention; public chart availability may be limited.
3. **Unsecured funding and bank credit:** relevant overnight/term benchmarks, bank CDS or bond spreads where a permitted data source exists, and central-bank facilities.
4. **Collateral and sovereign supply:** Treasury settlement calendar, auction sizes/results, bill supply and repo specialness where accessible.
5. **Slow confirmation:** BIS GLI/LBS and IMF external-liquidity data.

The synthesis is an inference from these distinct measures. A rise in a repo rate around a known tax or settlement date, without broader FX-funding or credit confirmation, is weaker evidence of systemic dollar stress than a persistent, cross-market deterioration.

### Reconcile external-position stocks before calling a capital flow

The [BEA International Investment Position](https://www.bea.gov/data/intl-trade-investment/international-investment-position) reports quarterly US external asset/liability stocks; Table 1.3 in its [Interactive Data Tables](https://apps.bea.gov/iTable/?isuri=1&product=5&reqid=62&step=6&tablelist=146) separates changes associated with transactions, price changes, exchange rates and other changes. BEA's methods describe initial quarterly IIP estimates as released about 90 days after quarter-end and subsequently revised. Read that stock-flow bridge before interpreting a widening net liability position as new foreign financing, a loss of confidence, or capital flight. Pair it with higher-frequency Treasury TIC only as a distinct measure: BEA IIP and TIC differ in scope, timing and methods and should not be added as if they were one ledger. ([BEA concepts and methods](https://www.bea.gov/sites/default/files/2023-06/iea-concepts-methods-2023.pdf))

**Dated illustration:** BEA's 24 September 2026 Q2 release said US assets rose $3.72tn, including $3.03tn from price changes and $663.3bn from financial transactions; US liabilities rose $4.87tn, including $3.95tn from price changes and $978.9bn from transactions. In that quarter, valuation explained far more of the stock increase than transactions. This does not make transactions unimportant; it shows why stock changes alone cannot establish flow-driven selling or buying. The quarter is lagged and subject to revision; preserve the release vintage and use the archive when reconstructing what was known then. ([BEA release](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/us-international-transactions-and-investment-position-2nd-quarter-2026))

For bilateral direct investment, add the IMF [Direct Investment Positions by Counterpart Economy (DIP, formerly CDIS)](https://data.imf.org/datasets/IMF.STA:DIP) to the portfolio-position view from PIP/CPIS. DIP reports annual inward/outward positions by immediate counterpart, equity/debt instrument, and additional financial-company/fellow-enterprise detail where available; it is a position dataset, not a transaction series. As checked 8 November 2026, the latest available reference period was end-2024: the IMF's December 2025 data brief reports 110 economies provided end-2024 data. Distinguish three concepts: direct observations reported by the target economy, mirror values from the partner economy's report (which can differ), and derived values constructed from mirror reports. Label IMF global or nonreporter estimates separately. The first counterpart is not necessarily the ultimate investor. The IMF recommends market value for listed equity/debt securities and own-funds-at-book-value for unlisted/other equity, but country practices differ; other debt instruments are generally nominal. Participation is voluntary, confidential cells remain, and annual revisions can change history. Compare with BEA IIP, IMF PIP/CPIS and TIC only after aligning instrument, immediate-counterpart/residence, valuation, vintage and reference period. ([DIP FAQ](https://data.imf.org/-/media/DIP/CDIS-Frequently-Asked-Questions-Latest-updates.pdf), [10 Dec 2025 availability tables](https://data.imf.org/-/media/DIP/CDIS-Data-Availability-as-of-December-10-2025.xlsx), [IMF December 2025 data brief](https://data.imf.org/en/news/imf%20data%20brief%20december%2016))

For a broader external-debt stock view, use the World Bank/IMF [Quarterly External Debt Statistics (QEDS)](https://www.worldbank.org/en/programs/debt-statistics/qeds/gdds), especially the [SDDS sector](https://databank.worldbank.org/data/embed-int/C2-SDDS-new/id/f75db76d), [currency](https://databank.worldbank.org/embed-int/SDDS-Table-C5/id/7eead674) and [Table 1.6 stock-flow reconciliation](https://databank.worldbank.org/data/embed-int/T1.6/id/aaed4fdf) tables. These give quarterly resident-to-nonresident debt by borrower sector, maturity/instrument and, where reported, domestic/foreign-currency denomination, with transactions, FX valuation, other price and other volume changes in the reconciliation table. As checked 8 November 2026, the SDDS tables covered 1998Q1 onward and were updated through 2026Q2 on 16 October; releases are usually about one quarter lagged and published twice per quarter. Downloadable DataBank files are public; the catalog lists CC BY 4.0, but check the specific file's terms and country metadata. Participation and detail vary: GDDS reporting may cover only public and publicly guaranteed debt, supplementary fields are not universal, and “foreign currency” is not a USD/EUR/other currency split. Treat QEDS as a residency-based liability stock series, not nationality-based issuance, ultimate-risk exposure or a live funding tape; preserve retrieval date and each vintage because historical data can be revised. ([World Bank FAQ](https://www.worldbank.org/en/programs/debt-statistics/faq), [SDDS catalog entry](https://datacatalog.worldbank.org/search/dataset/0037740/quarterly-external-debt-statistics-sdds))

Use the World Bank's annual [International Debt Statistics (IDS)](https://databank.worldbank.org/id/2cbe2a12) and the [International Debt Report 2026 products and methodology](https://www.worldbank.org/en/programs/debt-statistics/idr/products) to add borrower-reported debt flows, creditor composition, loan terms, historical debt service and debt-service projections for low- and middle-income reporting countries. The 5 November 2026 IDR covers external debt through end-2025 for 119 Debtor Reporting System countries. This complements QEDS: QEDS gives the faster quarterly sector/currency stock breakdown, while IDS adds annual creditor and payment detail. The separate IDS-DSSI database includes actual deferred service and projected monthly bilateral payments. DRS long-term public and publicly guaranteed debt is reported loan by loan; private nonguaranteed debt is aggregated and may be estimated, and some short-term debt is voluntary or estimated from QEDS/BIS. Future service projections assume existing commitments are fully drawn and repaid, use end-2025 FX/variable-rate assumptions, and exclude new borrowing or subsequent cancellations/restructurings. Treat them as cutoff-based schedules, not forecasts of actual future payment. DataBank offers public downloads/API, and the catalog lists CC BY 4.0; retain metadata and each release vintage. ([2026 methodology guide](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/6e72b0ded996306fa01f5db7a0c38b19-0050052021/related/IDS-2026-User-Guide-and-Methodology.pdf), [2026 press release](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2026/11/05/developing-countries-external-debt-surges-to-record-9-4-trillion), [Data Catalog/license](https://datacatalog.worldbank.org/search/dataset/0038015/international-debt-statistics))

### Global dollar funding and cross-border bank risk

Use the BIS [Global Liquidity Indicators (GLIs)](https://data.bis.org/topics/GLI) for quarterly foreign-currency credit to non-bank borrowers, including US-dollar credit outside the United States, euro credit outside the euro area, and yen credit outside Japan. The E2 tables combine bank loans with international debt securities; E2.1 separates borrowers outside the US and gives an emerging-market aggregate plus selected country detail. The end-June 2026 release was published 29 October, and the portal listed 7 December 2026 as the next release when checked on 8 November. Data are stocks; BIS annual growth rates use exchange-rate- and break-adjusted changes compounded over four quarters. Historical values can be revised when inputs change. ([BIS GLI tables](https://data.bis.org/topics/GLI/tables-and-dashboards), [methodology](https://www.bis.org/statistics/gli/gli_methodology.pdf), [release calendar/metadata](https://data.bis.org/topics/GLI))

Pair the GLIs with the BIS [Locational Banking Statistics (LBS)](https://data.bis.org/topics/LBS) and [Consolidated Banking Statistics (CBS)](https://data.bis.org/topics/CBS), but do not add their totals together. LBS follows the residence of the booking office, is unconsolidated, and includes intragroup positions; its exchange-rate- and break-adjusted changes approximate flows. CBS consolidates banks by the nationality of the parent group, excludes intragroup claims, and adds immediate-counterparty and ultimate-risk views to assess guarantors and risk transfers. LBS tells where cross-border positions are booked; CBS is closer to a banking-group country-risk view; neither is a complete map of end-borrower currency mismatch or off-balance-sheet hedging.

Use BIS [Debt Securities Statistics (DSS)](https://data.bis.org/topics/DSS) for the securities-financing side of the cross-border picture. C1 summarizes outstanding debt by issuer residence and sector; C5 adds issuer-side issuance and net transactions; C6 shows holder-sector holdings and net transactions; C7 links issuer and holder sectors. Coverage spans more than 50 economies with longer histories for some series. At the 8 November 2026 check, 14 September was the latest published release and the 2026Q2 DSS release was scheduled for 7 December; do not confuse that future DSS release with the separate BIS international-debt-securities dataset's 2026Q2 reference period. Download quarterly CSV/SDMX vintages and note retrieval date because the DSS calendar does not specify a separate revision schedule. Domestic versus international means market of issue relative to issuer residence, regardless of currency. Country coverage and detail vary; outstanding values may use nominal or market value. Issuer-side net issuance is generally at face value; holder-side net transactions and holdings are at market value, subject to country-specific reporting/valuation exceptions. Stock changes therefore do not equal net issuance or investor flows. C6/C7 lack country-to-country counterpart geography and security-level beneficial-owner identities. ([DSS tables](https://data.bis.org/topics/DSS/tables-and-dashboards), [BIS DSS release calendar](https://data.bis.org/release-calendar), [BIS bulk downloads](https://data.bis.org/bulkdownload), [coverage/valuation notes](https://www.bis.org/statistics/debt_sec/coverageC1_C5_C6_C7.pdf))

**Build the panel:** freeze each quarterly vintage and release timestamp; track GLI USD/EUR/JPY credit by borrower geography and bank-loan versus bond component; compare LBS cross-border claims and liabilities by currency, counterparty sector, reporting location and residence; then inspect CBS claims on an immediate-counterparty and ultimate-risk basis. Normalize stock changes for exchange rates, breaks and reporting changes before calling them flows. Compare rising FX debt with local currency depreciation, policy rates, sovereign/corporate spreads, FX reserves, external debt service and documented hedge coverage. Country GLI detail is limited to selected markets; local-bank domestic-currency lending, non-bank balance-sheet leverage, private bilateral hedges and many contingent liabilities are not fully observed here. Treat this as a quarterly vulnerability screen, not a real-time funding tape or a direct prediction of crisis.

### China: separate local budget execution from property sales and debt issuance

China's Ministry of Finance monthly [National Fiscal Revenue and Expenditure](https://www.mof.gov.cn/zhengwuxinxi/redianzhuanti/quanguocaizhengshouzhiqingkuang/) releases separate national general-public-budget accounts from government-fund accounts and split central from local execution. The latter reports local government-fund revenue, including land-use-right transfer receipts, related expenditure and debt-interest spending. This is a useful official window on local fiscal execution and the property-linked revenue channel that may be missed in national property sales or credit aggregates.

**Dated illustration:** the 23 October 2026 release reported Jan–Sep local government-fund budget revenue of ¥2.1328tn (21,328 亿元) and land-transfer revenue of ¥1.6087tn (16,087 亿元), down 27.9% year over year. These are cumulative nominal fiscal receipts, not land auction value, home sales, net local resources or direct LGFV financing. Difference successive cumulative releases to estimate a within-year flow; save each original vintage and check Chinese units, reporting window, revisions and holiday calendar before comparing. ([MOF Q3 2026 report](https://gks.mof.gov.cn/tongjishuju/202610/t20261023_4003380.htm))

For transmission, triangulate these releases with NBS property/land data, local-government bond issuance and debt-service schedules, and property-sector credit indicators. Do not add general-budget and government-fund flows without tracing transfers between them; local bond issuance should be tracked as a separate financing flow.

### China credit, property loans and local-government financing vehicles

Use the PBOC [annual statistical-data archive](https://www.pbc.gov.cn/diaochatongjisi/116219/116319/index.html) and its current [2026 AFRE page](https://www.pbc.gov.cn/diaochatongjisi/116219/116319/2026ntjsj/shrzgm/index.html) to retrieve the latest [flow](https://www.pbc.gov.cn/diaochatongjisi/attachDir/2026/10/2026101417443343908.htm) and [stock](https://www.pbc.gov.cn/diaochatongjisi/attachDir/2026/10/2026101417444554195.htm) tables. The flow table reports monthly financing in 100 million yuan across RMB and FX loans, entrusted/trust loans, undiscounted acceptances, corporate bonds, government bonds, domestic equity, ABS and loan write-offs; the stock table reports month-end balances and growth in trillion yuan. Convert units explicitly. As a dated check, in the 14 October 2026 PBOC table vintage, September AFRE flow was ¥3.5476tn, including ¥1.5362tn in the AFRE government-bond component, while AFRE stock was ¥468.17tn, +7.1% year over year. These monthly flow and month-end stock figures are different measures; AFRE government-bond financing is not LGFV corporate borrowing. Retain each release vintage and do not assume the narrative's year-to-date total equals a sum of monthly rows from a different vintage. The PBOC adjusted statistical coverage from 2023 to include consumer finance, wealth-management and financial-asset-investment companies; use its published comparable year-over-year rates and check vintage notes before reconstructing history.

For property credit, capture the PBOC quarterly financial-institution loan-investment tables for property-development loans, personal housing loans, total real-estate loans and mortgage rates; its [official Q&A](https://wzdt.pbc.gov.cn/eportal/ui?msgDataId=5a1ca14ce45740208156681603554924&pageId=77c3557bd521439ea5cd869f5393ba98) confirms commercial personal mortgage rates are quarterly and does not regularly publish new corporate-loan weighted averages. The official [financial-statistics glossary](https://www.pbc.gov.cn/eportal/fileDir/image_public/UserFiles/diaochatongjisi/upload/File/%E9%87%91%E8%9E%8D%E7%BB%9F%E8%AE%A1%E5%B8%B8%E7%94%A8%E6%8C%87%E6%A0%87%E9%87%8A%E4%B9%89.pdf) distinguishes development lending, real-estate-secured lending and personal mortgages. For local-government financing vehicles (LGFVs), maintain an issuer-level interbank-bond register from [NAFMII issuance statistics](https://www.nafmii.org.cn/sjtj/fx/) and its issuer disclosures: issuer legal name, instrument code/type, amount, coupon, maturity/redemption, controlling locality and the evidence for any LGFV classification. NAFMII's monthly statistics arrive with an irregular lag (its September 2026 issuance file was released 2 November), so record publication date and market reference month separately. Add [MOF monthly local-government bond statistics](https://zwgls.mof.gov.cn/tjsj/202610/t20261027_4003589.htm) as a separate statutory public-debt series, separating new from refinancing issuance and repayments.

These sources do **not** create a complete public LGFV debt total. Government bonds in TSF are not LGFV corporate debt; MOF statutory local-government debt excludes off-budget company liabilities; NAFMII interbank instruments exclude exchange bonds, bank loans, trust/nonstandard credit and contingent guarantees. Do not classify an issuer from its name alone, and do not treat a TSF acceleration as proof that credit reached property developers or LGFVs. Pair issuance with redemption calendars and issuer financial statements, and state the observed perimeter.

### China housing sales and developer liquidity

The NBS monthly [real-estate development and sales report](https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfbhjd/202610/t20261019_1965615.html) provides a separate window on new-home contract sales, construction starts/completions, completed unsold stock and developer funds received. ### Options and positioning: safer inference ladder

- **Observed:** contract volume, open interest, implied volatility, skew, COT reported long/short positions.
- **Calculated:** net position, percentile, term structure slope, realized/implied spread, open-interest change.
- **Inferred:** possible hedging pressure, crowdedness, demand for crash protection, dealer gamma.
- **Not public from these files alone:** the full customer book, dealer net exposure across venues, the side that initiated each trade, or the holder's underlying hedge.

Do not label a vendor's “dealer gamma” plot as exchange data. Record its inputs, options universe, interpolation, sign assignment, OTC assumptions and timestamp. If those are unavailable, treat the output as an unverified hypothesis.

## 3. Physical commodity balance: inventory is location-specific

### Gold and precious metals

The existing [gold playbook](hard-to-get-market-data-playbook.md) describes LBMA clearing, CME stocks and delivery notices, SGE activity, ETF holdings, COT and central-bank reporting. The advanced point is to build a *location and form ledger*: London vault holdings, COMEX registered/eligible stocks, exchange delivery activity, ETF custodial holdings, imports/exports, local premiums, and bar-form/assay constraints. A tonne in the wrong place, form or ownership channel may not be available to meet an immediate delivery need.

For any shortage narrative, ask: Is the spread curve showing nearby scarcity? Are delivery notices or withdrawals unusual after seasonal adjustment? Is metal moving between registered and eligible categories? Are Shanghai prices compared at the same moment and tax/FX basis? Are ETF flows creating or releasing allocated metal? Which pieces are reported measurements and which are estimated?

### LME base-metal warehouse stocks and off-warrant metal

Track the LME warehouse network as its own location-specific panel. The daily [stock-movement report](https://www.lme.com/Market-data/Reports-and-data/Warehouse-and-stocks-reports/Stocks-summary/Stock-movement-report) includes opening stocks, open/cancelled tonnage and deliveries in/out by metal and location; the website's detailed stock files are delayed, while faster vendor products are licensed. Off-warrant stocks are now reported daily: the LME publishes a free T+3 series and offers T+1 access through its licensing portal; the [2026 access note](https://www.lme.com/-/media/files/data/accessing-market-data/historical-data/daily-lme-off-warrant-stock-reports-2026.pdf) lists a $1,200 annual subscription, pro-rated. Daily history begins in 2025, with earlier monthly history available. Record report date, publication date, metal, location, unit and vintage; compare warranted closing stocks, cancelled tonnage, net deliveries and off-warrant stocks separately, then add the monthly [warehouse-company and queue report](https://www.lme.com/Market-data/Reports-and-data/Warehouse-and-stocks-reports/Warehouse-and-queue-data).

The perimeter is LME-brand eligible metal reported in LME-registered warehouses, not all metal worldwide. Off-warrant does not mean exchange-deliverable; cancellation indicates a withdrawal request, not a completed withdrawal. A rise in off-warrant metal can mean metal remains in the LME warehouse network while leaving warrant status, not that global supply increased. Start with the public delayed series and buy faster access only if the research cadence requires it.

### Energy as a case study in “headline inventory” traps

The [EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/supply/weekly/) provides weekly U.S. balances, stocks, imports, exports and refinery information with downloadable tables. Its own [methodology](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/supply/weekly/pdf/appendixd.pdf) explains that weekly export estimates use data supplied by U.S. Customs and Border Protection. EIA warns that weekly product-supplied readings can swing due to sample coverage and cargo timing; its [FAQ](https://www.eia.gov/tools/faqs/faq.php?id=1398&t=6) recommends interpreting noisy weekly values with appropriate context.

For oil, record at least:

- crude and product stocks separately, including Cushing when relevant;
- refinery runs/utilization, production, imports and exports;
- product supplied as a proxy for consumption, not a direct retail-sales census;
- four-week averages and the monthly Petroleum Supply Monthly reconciliation;
- the futures curve (front spreads and calendar structure) using matched settlement conventions;
- freight, sanctions, weather and port disruptions only from clearly identified sources.

Do not read a single weekly crude-stock draw as a global shortage. The U.S. balance is not the global balance; a stock move may reflect imports, exports, refinery demand, measurement noise, timing, or changes in crude quality/location.

### Europe gas: build a daily physical balance from separate measurements

Use [GIE AGSI](https://agsi.gie.eu/) for underground storage stocks and injection/withdrawal, [GIE ALSI](https://alsi.gie.eu/) for LNG-terminal inventory and send-out, and the [ENTSOG Transparency Platform](https://transparency.entsog.eu/) for pipeline flows, nominations, capacity and interruptions. The [ENTSOG dashboard](https://gasdashboard.entsog.eu/) is useful for orientation, but it aggregates data from ENTSOG and AGSI by supply corridor; use the underlying platform for analysis and preserve the original observation/status.

GIE reports daily-resolution coverage of EU27 underground storage and large-scale LNG terminals. Its operator reporting is a transparency benchmark, not a direct record of every arriving cargo or every molecule supplied. API access is free after registration, with a key. The 2022 API manual says unavailability is not covered, then documents an unavailability endpoint; verify current API access and use the website as a fallback. GIE requires source attribution. Historical coverage varies by facility, facilities may change ownership/grouping, and retroactive corrections can occur. ([GIE platform scope](https://www.gie.eu/agsi-and-alsi-transparency-platforms/); [API manual](https://www.gie.eu/transparency-platform/GIE_API_documentation_v007.pdf))

For a usable panel, align gas-day definitions and local time; save each daily vintage and GIE status flag (estimated, confirmed or no data); keep AGSI stocks (TWh), ALSI tank inventory (10³ m³ LNG), and flow fields (GWh/day), nominations, technical capacity and outages in separate columns. Convert LNG volume to energy only with a documented factor and assumptions. Calculate net storage injection/withdrawal and capacity utilization only from compatible fields. Compare seasonal percentiles, rolling flow changes, weather/demand, and TTF prompt/calendar spreads. Never treat LNG send-out as cargo arrivals, nominations as realized flows, or one corridor's throughput as total European net supply; keep EU, UK and Ukraine perimeters explicit.

### European power, U.S. LNG cargoes, and hourly grid data

For European power, the [ENTSO-E Transparency Platform](https://transparency.entsoe.eu/) provides operator-submitted load, generation by fuel, cross-border physical flow and transfer capacity, outages, and market data. API use requires registration and a token; the [official API instructions](https://www.entsoe.eu/data/transparency-platform/) note that access requests can take several working days. Platform and API delivery changed during 2025–26, so version the extraction and retain data versions/submission timestamps. Build a daily balance from load, wind/solar, thermal generation, available imports and outages, and compare gas-fired generation with GIE/ENTSOG gas supply. Submissions can be corrected; physical flows are not commercial schedules, and a national price spike may reflect local congestion rather than continent-wide scarcity.

For US LNG exports, DOE's monthly [Natural Gas Imports and Exports archive](https://www.energy.gov/hgeo/articles/natural-gas-imports-and-exports-monthly-2026) includes the FE-746R cargo workbook. Fields include vessel, terminal, departure date, destination, volume, export-point price, supplier and purchaser; use DOE's [filing instructions](https://www.energy.gov/hgeo/guidelines-filing-monthly-reports) for definitions. The workbook is typically lagged by one to two months and subsequent reports can revise earlier months. Cross-check DOE cargo volumes with [EIA monthly LNG export volumes by point of exit/destination](https://www.eia.gov/dnav/ng/NG_MOVE_POE2_DCU_NUS-Z00_M.htm), arrivals with AIS, and terminal send-out with AGSI/ALSI. EIA does not separately publish gas consumed in LNG liquefaction, so do not treat inferred feedgas as an official EIA series. Cargo-reported prices are transaction context, not a daily LNG benchmark, and this dataset covers US exports rather than the global fleet.

For US hourly power context, EIA-930 publishes Lower 48 balancing-authority demand, forecast demand, generation by source and interchange, often about an hour after the operating hour; prior-day totals generally appear by late morning ET. The [EIA-930 guide](https://www.eia.gov/electricity/930-content/about.html) describes as-is revisions, time zones, and daylight-saving 23/25-hour days; hourly price data are not included. Pair it with an ISO feed such as [ERCOT real-time/day-ahead prices](https://www.ercot.com/mktinfo/prices) to distinguish system balance from local scarcity and congestion. EIA is Lower 48 system context; ERCOT prices are ERCOT-specific, not a US-wide price signal.

JODI monthly country oil/gas submissions should carry the latest biannual [participation assessment](https://www.jodidata.org/oil/support/user-guide/assessments.aspx), the observation-level confidence flag, and the [downloaded series](https://www.jodidata.org/oil/database/data-downloads.aspx). JODI reports submissions without filling all missing observations; definitions and timeliness differ by country. A missing value is not zero, and cross-country balances should be confidence-weighted and reconciled against national data where possible.

### Oil balance: keep survey estimates, monthly benchmarks, and forecasts distinct

The EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report (WPSR) is a high-frequency sample survey covering around 90% of the latest monthly volume for each supply source, not a census. It can be a fast signal, but its weekly product-supplied measure can swing with sampling error and cargo/customs timing. EIA recommends using a four-week average to assess consumption trends; the monthly [Petroleum Supply Monthly](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/supply/monthly/) covers the broader primary supply universe and is released with a longer lag, while the annual Petroleum Supply Annual is the more fully reviewed historical benchmark. When PSM data arrive, preserve both the original weekly observation and the later monthly estimate instead of silently replacing the old vintage. [EIA comparison of WPSR, PSM and PSA](https://www.eia.gov/tools/faqs/faq.php?id=1398&t=6)

Treat the WPSR crude-oil “adjustment” line as a balancing residual created by independently estimated supply and disposition components, not as a directly observed production source or hidden demand. Check stock changes, imports/exports, refinery inputs, production estimates, petroleum transfers, crude quality/location and seasonal timing together; do not turn one crude-stock draw into a global shortage claim. EIA reviews weekly domestic crude production estimates alongside monthly STEO publication and can rebenchmark them against PSM and other information. Weekly data collection and release formats continued to evolve in 2026, so preserve the source file, release timestamp, and method/format vintage. [EIA crude-oil adjustment explanation](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=21472), [EIA production rebenchmarking notice](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/supply/weekly/), [WPSR format notice](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/supply/weekly/wpsr_notice_06102026.php)

Use monthly [EIA STEO](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/report/) forecasts for the US and global energy balance as model outputs with a documented forecast-completion date, not as observed supply/demand. For global estimates, compare public EIA STEO tables with the IEA [Oil Market Report](https://www.iea.org/data-and-statistics/data-product/oil-market-report-omr) and OPEC's monthly [MOMR archive](https://www.opec.org/monthly-oil-market-report.html), but preserve each agency's forecast vintage, geography, product scope, measurement method and survey/non-survey inputs; do not average different balances into a synthetic consensus without harmonizing definitions. OPEC's report page also provides a monthly Excel appendix with selected world demand/supply balance tables and revisions, OECD oil stocks and oil on water, non-OPEC supply, OPEC NGL and rig counts; for example, the [October 2026 appendix](https://www.opec.org/assets/assetdb/momr-appendix-october-2026.xlsx). Within OPEC production reporting, keep the direct-communication and secondary-source tables separate; the latter is an aggregate of approved secondary sources and can differ from member submissions. [OPEC's production-monitoring methodology](https://www.opec.org/pr-detail/28-05-dec-2024.html). IEA detailed historical tables and forecasts are subscription products; headline public summaries do not reproduce the full data set.

**Calendar example frozen 8 November 2026:** the latest WPSR had been released 4 November; the report for the week ending 6 November was scheduled for Thursday 12 November, with first tables at noon ET and remaining tables at 2 p.m. because of the Veterans Day federal closure. EIA's October STEO was scheduled to refresh 10 November. OPEC's October MOMR was the latest available, with its November report scheduled for 11 November; the IEA November OMR launch was scheduled for 13 November. This clusters several different US/global supply-demand vintages in four days; an analyst should log each release separately and avoid describing the still-unreleased week-ending balance as observed. ### U.S. grain supply chain: export logistics, sales and basis

USDA AMS's weekly [Grain Transportation Report (GTR)](https://www.ams.usda.gov/services/transportation-analysis/gtr) and [downloadable datasets](https://www.ams.usda.gov/services/transportation-analysis/gtr-datasets) link selected freight cost indicators and price spreads across truck, rail, barge and ocean routes to grain movement and export logistics. As checked 8 November 2026, the latest issue was 5 November. Tables include secondary railcar bids/offers, Illinois barge rates and movements at selected locks/dams, export inspections by port region in thousand metric tonnes, and vessel counts loaded or expected to load in the next 10 days for Gulf/PNW and other selected regions. These are route- and port-specific measures, not a comprehensive freight or global-shipping tape.

Pair the GTR with USDA FAS [weekly Export Sales data](https://fas.usda.gov/data) and the [ESRQS public user manual](https://apps.fas.usda.gov/esrqs/assets/ESRQS_UserManual_PublicUser.pdf), which describes viewing/export fields and distinctions among weekly exports, net sales and outstanding sales, plus [FGIS grain inspections](https://esmis.nal.usda.gov/publication/grains-inspected-export-weekly) and WASDE balances. Keep weekly exports/accumulated exports separate from net sales and outstanding (unshipped) sales; export sales are commitments subject to cancellation or revision. Inspection tonnage means grain inspected, weighed or prepared for export; it does not prove a cargo sailed. A vessel expected to load is not a loaded vessel. Align time bases before comparing cumulative totals: GTR Table 18's inspection YTD columns are calendar-year figures, while FAS export-sales and FGIS cumulative totals use commodity-specific marketing years. Preserve the report week, commodity/type, crop or marketing year, destination, port region, unit and release vintage. Compare like weeks and balances; do not infer a global shortage from freight prices, inspection volumes or export commitments alone.

For the local cash-price link, USDA AMS [MyMarketNews state grain reports](https://www.ams.usda.gov/market-news/state-grain-reports) include [Iowa Daily Cash Grain Bids](https://mymarketnews.ams.usda.gov/viewReport/2850) and [Louisiana/Texas Gulf Export Bids](https://mymarketnews.ams.usda.gov/viewReport/3147). Depending on the report, observations include elevator, processor or export-elevator bids, cash-price ranges, futures-linked basis, grade, delivery window and freight terms. These are voluntary, reporter-collected local quotes, not a national index or verified executions; some reports state basis against the current-day or prior-day futures close. Match location, grade, delivery month, futures contract, freight term and timing before comparing. Reports may be corrected; preserve the raw report, ID/date, retrieval time, status and correction details. The [MyMarketNews API](https://mymarketnews.ams.usda.gov/mymarketnews-api/reports) supports report/date and correction retrieval but requires a personal key; public reports remain viewable without API access.

### Global port and chokepoint signals: IMF PortWatch

The IMF [PortWatch platform](https://portwatch.imf.org/) provides daily AIS-derived port-call counts, estimated incoming/outgoing shipment volumes and vessel/transit indicators for maritime chokepoints; its [Access Data page](https://portwatch.imf.org/pages/access-data) exposes the underlying datasets. This supplies a global maritime layer beyond the U.S.-focused [NOAA MarineCadastre AIS traffic proxy](https://marinecadastre.gov/ais/). Treat port calls and tonnage as vessel-based estimates, not customs transactions or commodity-identified cargo. Coverage is seaborne and can be affected by AIS reception gaps, port-boundary assignment, transshipment and the difference between vessel arrival and customs-clearance dates. Daily indicators can also be revised when AIS inputs, methods or port/vessel coverage change, so archive vintages. The platform's separate monthly country trade-value nowcast is experimental, not an official IMF estimate; it is released about seven working days after month-end and revised weekly. Validate against customs and commodity-specific sources. See the IMF [global methodology paper](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2025/05/16/nowcasting-global-trade-from-space-566957), [country-level methodology paper](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2026/05/19/nowcasting-country-level-trade-estimates-using-imf-portwatch-576176), and current [nowcast limitations and update notes](https://data-download.imf.org/ClimateData/portwatch-monitor.html).

### Nonfuel mineral supply, use and stocks: USGS

The USGS [National Minerals Information Center key publications](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/national-minerals-information-center/key-publications) and commodity pages, such as [copper statistics](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/national-minerals-information-center/copper-statistics-and-information), publish Mineral Industry Surveys with U.S. production, trade, use, recycling and selected stock measures at commodity-specific monthly, quarterly, semiannual or annual frequencies. The surveys cover the U.S. nonfuel mining and mineral-processing industry. Copper reports include selected mine/smelter/refinery, consumption, import/export, scrap, price and domestic stock detail, with downloadable PDFs and spreadsheets. As checked 8 November 2026, the latest copper MIS covered July–September 2025 and was published in May 2026; do not infer freshness from a report's nominal frequency. The annual [2026 Mineral Commodity Summaries](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/mcs2026) covers more than 90 nonfuel mineral commodities, with a related [CSV data release](https://www.usgs.gov/data/mineral-commodity-summaries-2026-data-release) for U.S. salient statistics and world production; the release is marked CC0. USGS canvasses are voluntary and company data are aggregated to protect proprietary information; table-level estimates, extrapolations and revisions are identified in report footnotes. Apparent consumption is a balance-sheet estimate, not observed end-use demand, and U.S. exchange/warehouse stocks are not global visible inventory. Keep each report's reference period, publication vintage, units, definitions and footnotes; see the official [data-collection limits](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/national-minerals-information-center/minerals-data-collection).

### Nuclear fuel-cycle facility and capacity map: IAEA NFCFDB

The IAEA's [Nuclear Fuel Cycle Facilities Database (NFCFDB)](https://infcis.iaea.org/NFCFDB/NFCFDB) provides a searchable, facility-level map across the civil chain from uranium mines/mills through conversion, enrichment and fuel fabrication to spent-fuel storage/reprocessing. The open [facility search](https://infcis.iaea.org/NFCFDB/facilities) supports country, facility-type, status and scale filters; public records can show design capacity, process, feed/product material, operator, facility status, dates, remarks and source references. The IAEA says information is continuously updated and expanded, but there is no single common vintage: record/source dates vary, the database relies largely on voluntary member-state submissions and public sources, and the site warns that the list may omit facilities. Use it to map possible bottlenecks and ownership, not to estimate current production, usable spare capacity or tradable supply. Filter planned, commissioning, operating, standby, shutdown and decommissioned sites separately; keep units stage-specific (for example, tU/year versus tSWU/year), archive the source date on each record, and verify material capacities with operators/regulators before aggregating. The need for this control is visible within the database: the U.S. Metropolis conversion-facility record lists a design-capacity field of 7,000 tU/year but remarks cite 17,600 tU/year, while the Rokkasho enrichment page combines an operating level with future expansion values. Treat these as unresolved fields, not as facts to sum. Public country reports are linked from the [IAEA reports page](https://infcis.iaea.org/NFCFDB/reports); no public bulk export/API route was documented on the pages checked. ([Metropolis record](https://infcis.iaea.org/NFCFDB/facility/Details/201), [Rokkasho record](https://infcis.iaea.org/NFCFDB/facility/Details/150), [database scope and caveats](https://infcis.iaea.org/NFCFDB/NFCFDB))

### U.S. waterborne commodity flows: USACE WCSC

The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers [Waterborne Commerce Statistics Center (WCSC)](https://www.iwr.usace.army.mil/About/Technical-Centers/WCSC-Waterborne-Commerce-Statistics-Center/WCSC-Waterborne-Commerce/) collects vessel trips and cargo by commodity, origin/destination, port and waterway. The [NDC Ports and Waterways tool](https://ndc.ops.usace.army.mil/wcsc/webpub/) supports port/waterway queries; annual Waterborne Commerce of the United States files provide detailed cargo and trip tables, plus domestic/foreign loaded/empty TEUs by port, waterway and state. As checked 8 November 2026, the page's latest detailed Excel cargo/trip files were for 2023. For a faster pulse, monthly Waterways Commodity Indicators combine current-year submitted vessel reports with Lock Performance Monitoring System data in regression nowcasts for Total, Coal, Farm & Food, Chemical and Petroleum; USACE explicitly says these estimates are superseded by actual WCSC data as processing completes. TOWS navigation points update quarterly, while most other data update annually, so check each product's vintage. These are waterborne-only measures; Farm & Food is not a grain-specific series. Don't treat a model estimate as a final cargo count, or sum tonnage across successive projects/waterway segments because the same cargo can be counted at multiple points ([USACE double-counting note](https://www.iwr.usace.army.mil/Missions/Value-to-the-Nation/Navigation/)). Archive estimate/final status and distinguish monthly indicators from annual reported flows.

### Fundamental analysis questions that require footnotes

1. **Cash generation:** Is operating cash flow supported by collections, or by working-capital timing? Reconcile revenue, receivables, contract assets, payables and inventory.
2. **Debt and liquidity:** Include leases, pension deficits, guarantees, restricted cash, covenants, refinancing dates and variable-rate exposure. Gross cash can be unavailable or trapped by jurisdiction.
3. **Dilution:** Read stock-based compensation, option/RSU awards, convertibles, warrants, buybacks and weighted-average diluted shares. A buyback authorization is not proof of net share reduction.
4. **Segment economics:** Compare volumes, price/mix, margins, intersegment eliminations and capital employed. Consolidated growth can mask a weakening core unit.
5. **Accounting quality:** Review auditor emphasis, critical estimates, restatements, non-GAAP reconciliations, acquisition-related adjustments and tax/working-capital anomalies.
6. **Guidance credibility:** Store guidance as of the issue date and compare subsequent outcomes to the exact range. Do not rewrite the old guide using current facts.

Use data-vendor screeners to discover candidates; use filings to establish what the company actually reported. A screen value is not a source until its definition and underlying filing are recorded.

## 5. Event and expectation data: preserve the information set

The number that matters for an event is usually not “actual” alone. It is actual versus a timestamped expectation, prior value, and revision, followed by the response across assets. For every event keep:

```text
event_id, source, scheduled_time_utc, actual_release_time_utc,
reference_period, first_print, prior_as_known, revision,
consensus_provider, consensus_capture_time, median/range,
contributor_count, units, source_vintage, raw_file_hash,
reaction_windows, overlapping_events
```

Government releases and revisions can usually be recovered from the primary agency; a market-consensus history is often licensed or unavailable publicly. When consensus history cannot be established, say so and do not manufacture a “surprise.” Use ALFRED/source archives for data vintages where available, while recognizing that they do not reconstruct what analysts expected.

## 6. Access and sourcing tiers

Use four explicit access labels in the source registry:

| Tier | Meaning | Examples | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Open primary** | Official source provides public access and documentation. | BLS, BEA, Treasury, CFTC, SEC filings, EIA, BIS public tables. | Capture source page, exact dataset/table, retrieval date, version and permitted automated use. |
| **Open but operationally awkward** | Public, but spread across files, manual pages, PDFs, release calendars or exchange notices. | Exchange delivery files, some country customs tables, auction archives. | Build retrieval notes, parser checks and human fallback; avoid treating a failed scrape as a zero value. |
| **Licensed / vendor** | Access requires subscription, redistribution permission or a paid API. | Historical consensus, intraday consolidated order books, some options surfaces, analyst estimates. | Record contract, display/storage rights, history depth, timestamp and restatement policy. Do not clone a licensed feed into a public artifact. |
| **Not observable from public data** | Market-wide gross positions or counterparty details are not disclosed. | Full OTC dealer books, beneficial owner identity behind aggregated categories, many bilateral hedges. | State “not observed.” Triangulate with public proxies and set a lower confidence ceiling. Never invent a proxy that appears direct. |

## 7. A high-signal weekly review, built to expose disagreement

### Every day

- Store selected gold/FX/index instrument, venue, bid/ask or settlement, timestamp, session and spread. Keep broker CFD quotes separate from exchange futures and benchmarks.
- Record nominal yields, real yields, breakevens, dollar measure, relevant commodity curves and public funding rates on matched timestamps.
- In funding-stress episodes, capture leading money-fund website liquidity/flow disclosures by fund and preserve any as-of timestamps; don't substitute monthly Form N-MFP holdings for daily flow evidence.
- Check event calendar, policy headlines and auction/settlement calendar; capture source-publication times.
- Flag large dislocations and missing/stale inputs; do not fill gaps silently.

### Once each week

- Update CFTC COT/TFF using its position date; compute category gross/net changes, open-interest share and long-history percentile.
- Add final exchange volume/open interest and available options volatility/skew snapshots.
- Review ETF tonnage versus dollar AUM, exchange stocks/notices, local physical premium data and central-bank reporting schedule.
- Inspect U.S. repo rates/volumes and Fed facility usage; classify calendar effects before calling funding stress.
- Refresh SEC Form N-MFP portfolios when published and compare bills, repo, CDs/CP and maturity buckets with the earlier daily-fund disclosures.
- List the two strongest competing narratives for the week. Identify one additional observation that could separate them.

### Monthly / quarterly

- Reconcile EIA weekly energy estimates with monthly tables; summarize inventories by location/form and curve structure.
- Update Treasury auction participation and issuance mix; compare with yields, term premium and market depth if permitted data exist.
- Refresh BIS GLI/LBS, COFER, external-account and reserve data, keeping release lag explicit.
- Review EDGAR filings for fundamental changes, not just earnings headlines; update shares, debt maturities, segment margins and cash-flow bridges.
- Re-score prior hypotheses and forecasts. Preserve misses and revisions in the record.

## 8. Separate confidence in the measurement from support for the claim

Do not force source quality and causal support into one confidence label. A primary release can establish its own published value with high measurement confidence even when there is no independent duplicate; the same release alone may offer weak support for a market-causation claim.

| Dimension | High | Medium | Low |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Measurement confidence** | Primary source of record, matching definition/unit/period, point-in-time availability known, and no material quality issue for the stated fact. Independent reproduction is useful but not required for a directly reported official value. | Source is authentic but delayed, partial, estimated, revised, or dependent on a proxy/extraction whose limitations affect precision. | Source cannot be verified; key definition or vintage is missing; major coverage/quality issue or vendor estimate is not reproducible. |
| **Interpretation support** | Multiple genuinely distinct evidence-generating processes fit a predeclared mechanism; the strongest alternatives have been tested and materially weakened. | Evidence fits the mechanism but is incomplete, partly dependent, or still compatible with credible alternatives. | Narrative rests mainly on correlation, one proxy, vendor inference, or a post-hoc explanation; key links are unobserved. |

State both labels where useful, for example: **measurement high / causal support low**. Do not count republished data, reports sharing an upstream database, or correlated components of the same release as independent evidence. Neither label is the probability that a price forecast is correct; record forecast probability separately and score it after resolution.
